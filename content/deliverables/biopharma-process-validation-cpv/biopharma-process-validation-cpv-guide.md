# Biopharma Process Validation & Continued Process Verification Guide

**Document ID:** LSA-PRO-BIOPHARMA-PV-CPV-GUIDE-001

**Version:** 1.0.0-review

**Review status:** editorial-reviewed
**Risk level:** high

## Purpose

Use this guide to create a reviewable evidence chain from a defined biologics
process and its knowledge base through enabling qualification, PPQ or process
verification, routine monitoring, signal assessment, change and lifecycle
governance.

It is a decision-support structure. It does not provide an approved validation
strategy, execution protocol, CPV statistical plan, acceptance criterion,
batch-count rule, validation conclusion, regulatory position or batch decision.

## The eight evidence objects

1. Product, process and decision scope.
2. Process knowledge and control strategy.
3. Qualification and enabling-system readiness.
4. PPQ or process-verification design.
5. Execution and validation conclusion.
6. Continued process-verification plan and data lineage.
7. Signal, investigation and action.
8. Lifecycle maintenance, change and governance.

## Stage 1 — freeze the decision basis

Record the exact product/modality, drug-substance or drug-product stage,
process/site/scale/equipment/material/method versions, lifecycle stage, markets,
decision, accountable owner and exclusions. A project name or product family is
not a sufficient scope definition.

## Stage 2 — map process knowledge

For every relevant product attribute, material attribute, parameter and
performance indicator, record:

- unit operation and scientific question;
- direct product/process evidence, platform knowledge or hypothesis;
- study/model/material/scale and version;
- evidence strength and contradictory findings;
- linked control and analytical capability;
- applicability, uncertainty and material knowledge gap;
- owner and qualified reviewer.

Do not infer criticality from monitoring frequency, template labels or the fact
that a parameter can be measured.

## Stage 3 — qualify the evidence-generating system

Map facilities, utilities, equipment, automation and data flows, analytical
procedures, materials, personnel, procedures, maintenance and calibration.
Record the approved version, intended use, qualification state, deviation,
temporary control and effect on the validation question.

Equipment qualification does not establish process validation. Method
validation does not establish process control. Procedural readiness does not
authorize execution.

## Stage 4 — design PPQ or process verification

The design should state the question it can answer and why the selected scale,
equipment, material lots, operators, campaign positions, hold times, sampling,
methods, evaluation and number of batches or runs are representative and
sufficient for that exact question.

Predefine:

- process and control-strategy versions;
- prerequisites and release-to-execute decision;
- sampling points, rationale and sample/data genealogy;
- analytical methods and data systems;
- evaluation and statistical approach;
- acceptance criteria and their controlled source;
- handling of deviations, atypical results and missing data;
- approval, conditional progression and residual commitments;
- CPV handoff.

No fixed batch count is supplied by this package.

## Stage 5 — preserve execution and conclusions

Record what actually happened, including process conditions, materials,
equipment, operators, methods, raw-data locations, protocol changes,
deviations, investigations and atypical results. Separate:

1. protocol completion;
2. evidence interpretation;
3. validation conclusion;
4. authorization to proceed;
5. batch disposition;
6. regulatory or filing decision.

Changing an acceptance criterion after seeing results requires a documented,
scientifically justified and authorized path; it cannot be hidden as workbook
maintenance.

## Stage 6 — build the CPV plan

For each signal define:

- signal ID, unit operation and quality/process question;
- source system and data latency;
- data type, unit, transformation and missing-data treatment;
- stratification factors such as site, scale, equipment train, material,
  campaign, method or analyst where relevant;
- baseline population, exclusions and freeze date;
- statistical method/version and assumptions;
- entered statistical thresholds and controlled source;
- specification or other decision limits and controlled source;
- review window, frequency, minimum evidence and escalation rule;
- signal owner, reviewer and plan-change control.

Do not calculate control limits from an unstable or unreviewed baseline merely
because a spreadsheet can do so. Do not mix materially different populations
to make a trend appear stable.

## Stage 7 — assess signals without collapsing decisions

When a signal occurs:

1. preserve the original record and method/version;
2. confirm data completeness, integrity and analytical-system state;
3. identify concurrent material, equipment, maintenance, process, operator,
   sampling, method and system events;
4. assess statistical and specification status separately;
5. formulate competing hypotheses and required evidence;
6. connect to deviation/OOS/OOT, product-impact, CAPA/change and batch-
   disposition procedures as applicable;
7. record the authorized interpretation and follow-up effectiveness evidence.

A control-chart signal is evidence to review, not a self-executing disposition.
A favorable capability index is meaningful only when the process distribution,
measurement system, stability, independence, stratification and limits support
the calculation.

## Stage 8 — maintain the validated state

Periodic product/process review should reconcile CPV, deviations,
investigations, complaints, stability, yields, rejects/rework, maintenance,
calibration, supplier/material changes, method/reference changes, CAPA,
regulatory commitments and management review.

Monitoring may be increased, reduced or redesigned only through a controlled,
versioned rationale. A change assessment should identify effects on process
knowledge, qualification, validation, comparability, regulatory status and the
CPV baseline before implementation.

## Reviewer coverage

- process development and MSAT;
- manufacturing operations;
- process validation and qualification;
- engineering, utilities, automation and data systems;
- analytical development, QC and measurement-system/statistical expertise;
- materials/supplier quality as applicable;
- product-quality sciences;
- Quality Unit and regulatory CMC;
- modality-specific viral safety, microbiology, fill-finish, device or other
  specialist roles where applicable.

## Fictional example boundary

The completed workbook uses a fictional CHO protein process and normalized
unitless data. Its limits, records, equipment, materials, methods and outcomes
are invented solely to demonstrate structure. They are not platform knowledge,
benchmarks, recommendations or evidence for another product.

The example keeps one statistical signal within specification unresolved while
equipment-maintenance and material-lot events are assessed. It reaches only
`Qualified review required`.

## Limitations

This package does not establish process design, criticality, qualification,
PPQ, CPV, process capability, state of control, validation, comparability,
regulatory acceptability, batch disposition or authorization for manufacture.
Use current approved procedures and qualified decision owners for every
regulated conclusion.
