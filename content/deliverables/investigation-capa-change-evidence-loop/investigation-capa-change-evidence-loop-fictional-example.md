# Fictional example — shared data-processing signal

This is a fictional planning example. It is not an investigation conclusion,
root-cause determination, CAPA or change approval, effectiveness acceptance,
regulatory decision, batch disposition or precedent.

## Scenario

A fictional contract laboratory notices that a recently revised calculation
template was used for one API stability result and one drug-product release
result. The records remain available, but the approved-template status and the
effect of the revision have not been established. No result is invalidated and
no product impact is concluded from the signal alone.

| Loop stage | Fictional working record | Decision state |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Signal and lineage | Preserve both result records, template versions, metadata, audit trail and approval history | Evidence required |
| Immediate control | Prevent further use of the revised template pending authorized assessment | Qualified review required |
| Hypotheses | Unapproved revision; approved but incompletely deployed revision; record-selection error | Evidence required |
| Investigation evidence | Compare controlled versions, formulas, source data, review history and affected-use inventory | Evidence required |
| Action pathway | Separate any record correction from a possible system CAPA and controlled template change | Evidence required |
| Effectiveness | Define a later decision about correct-version use and review detectability; task completion alone is insufficient | Evidence required |
| Knowledge transfer | Share only the reviewed template-control lesson; do not generalize product impact or disposition | Qualified review required |

## Accountable review

Laboratory ownership, the quality unit, data-integrity or system ownership and
the relevant product/process reviewers must decide scope, conclusion, actions,
effectiveness and any product or regulatory consequence. The example remains
`Evidence required` until those records exist.
