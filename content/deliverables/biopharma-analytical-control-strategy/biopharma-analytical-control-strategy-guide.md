# Biopharma Analytical Control Strategy Evidence Map

**Version:** 1.0.0-review

**Status:** Editorial-reviewed; not SME-approved

**Document purpose:** Educational decision and evidence-mapping support

## Decision this package supports

For a defined protein biologic, process, presentation, lifecycle stage, and
intended use, which attributes require measurement; what complementary
analytical evidence can detect relevant differences; where should each control
sit; and what remains unresolved before a result, specification, reference
transition, procedure change, or lifecycle decision can be accepted?

## Use when

- designing or challenging a protein-biologics analytical control strategy;
- separating characterization, development, IPC, release, stability,
  comparability, investigation, and lifecycle uses;
- mapping potency, variants, impurities, contaminants, and method blind spots;
- assessing specification, procedure, site, instrument, reagent, reference,
  process, product, or filing changes;
- preparing evidence for qualified product, analytical, quality, statistics,
  validation, and regulatory CMC review.

## Do not use as

- a universal method panel or specification template;
- a product-specific ATP, method, validation, transfer, or reference-standard
  protocol;
- a source of acceptance criteria, reportable ranges, assigned values, system
  suitability, or batch decisions;
- a comparability, analytical equivalence, method validation, transfer,
  reference commutability, or regulatory conclusion;
- an unchanged template for advanced modalities.

## Workflow

1. **Frame the decision.** Freeze product, presentation, process, site,
   lifecycle stage, region, matrix, intended use, and owner.
2. **Map attributes.** Separate desired product, structure and heterogeneity,
   activity, product-related substances and impurities, degradation products,
   process-related impurities, contaminants, quantity, and performance.
3. **State analytical purpose.** Define the measurement decision before
   selecting technology or reusing a platform procedure.
4. **Map complementary methods.** Record each procedure's unique contribution,
   blind spots, matrix, range, performance, validation, and reportability.
5. **Control potency and biological systems.** Link mechanism relevance,
   reference, cells or critical reagents, calibration model, variability,
   system suitability, and result validity.
6. **Justify control placement.** Explain what belongs in characterization,
   process control, IPC, release, stability, monitoring, or another control.
7. **Build the reference hierarchy.** Preserve identity, tier, assigned
   property, traceability, qualification, stability, use, bridge, and owner.
8. **Manage lifecycle.** Track monitoring signals and changes to product,
   process, procedure, site, reference, reagent, instrument, data, or filing.
9. **Preserve contradiction.** Keep product, sample, method, reference, and
   process hypotheses separate until evidence supports a conclusion.
10. **Qualify the decision.** Close evidence and review flags, then transfer
    approved content into the authorized controlled system.

## Required review roles

- product-quality and characterization sciences;
- analytical development and QC;
- bioassay and statistics where biological activity is measured;
- reference-material or critical-reagent ownership;
- process development or MSAT when process evidence supports the control;
- quality, validation, and regulatory CMC.

## Revision watch

The ICH Q6(R1) concept paper initiates revision work but is not represented as
an effective replacement for Q6A or Q6B. The WHO 2026 recommendations replace
Annex 2 of WHO TRS 932 for international biological reference standards.
Regional implementation, current product commitments, approved procedures,
and applicable pharmacopoeial content still require verification.

## Limitation

This package structures evidence. It does not establish product CQAs,
analytical procedures, validation or transfer designs, reportable ranges,
reference suitability or assigned values, specifications, acceptance criteria,
comparability, regulatory acceptability, or batch disposition.
