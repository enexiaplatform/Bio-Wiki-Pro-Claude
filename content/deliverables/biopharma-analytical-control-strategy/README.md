# Biopharma Analytical Control Strategy Evidence Map — Start Here

This package helps a cross-functional biopharma team connect product-quality
attributes, intended analytical decisions, complementary method capability,
potency and impurity evidence, reference materials, control placement,
specification basis, lifecycle changes, and accountable review.

**Package version:** 1.0.0-review

**Review status:** under-review

**Decision owner:** the authorized product-quality, analytical development,
bioassay, reference-standard, QC, quality, statistics, and regulatory CMC
owners — not Atlas

## Package contents

1. `biopharma-analytical-control-strategy-guide.pdf` — decision workflow,
   evidence model, source basis, revision watch, and controlled-use boundaries.
2. `biopharma-analytical-control-strategy-v1.xlsx` — blank formula-driven
   working file with validations, attribute and method portfolios,
   specification and reference registers, lifecycle changes, evidence,
   sources, and review flags.
3. `biopharma-analytical-control-strategy-v1-fictional-example.xlsx` —
   completed fictional CHO monoclonal-antibody example preserving a polishing
   resin change, charge-profile signal, potency trend, reference transition,
   method transfer, competing hypotheses, and unresolved decisions.

## Recommended order

1. Confirm that the modality, product, lifecycle stage, region, and decision
   are within scope.
2. Freeze one analytical, specification, comparability, transfer, or reference
   transition decision and its owner.
3. Map product attributes and evidence for relevance without labeling every
   measurement a CQA.
4. Assign each analytical procedure an intended purpose, lifecycle use,
   unique contribution, limitation, validation state, and reportability gate.
5. Distinguish characterization, process controls, IPC, release, stability,
   comparability, and investigation uses.
6. Record the evidence for specification inclusion, omission, or change.
7. Build the reference-material and critical-reagent hierarchy, including
   qualification, stability, bridging, and replacement triggers.
8. Preserve analytical and product contradictions, lifecycle changes,
   commitments, actions, and qualified-review ownership.
9. Resolve every `Incomplete`, `Evidence required`, or `Review required` flag.
10. Transfer only qualified, approved content into the site's controlled
    specification, method, validation, quality, or regulatory system.

## Important boundary

The package supplies no universal analytical panel and no product-specific specification.
It supplies no CQA designation, analytical procedure,
parameter, ATP performance criterion, system-suitability criterion, reportable
range, validation protocol, reference value, acceptance criterion, sampling
plan, equivalence conclusion, transfer acceptance, filing category, or batch
decision. Q6(R1) is revision activity, not an effective revised guideline.

Every product-specific claim requires controlled provenance and qualified
review.
