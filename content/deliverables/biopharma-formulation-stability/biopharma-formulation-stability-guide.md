# Biopharma Formulation & Stability Evidence Map

**Version:** 1.0.0-review

**Status:** editorial-reviewed; not SME-approved

**Document purpose:** educational decision and evidence-mapping support

## Decision this package supports

For a defined protein biologic, formulation, process, presentation, and intended
use, which degradation and handling risks are material, what evidence detects and
controls them, and what remains unresolved before a qualified formulation,
fill-finish, container, shipping, storage, or shelf-life decision?

## Use when

- building or challenging a biologics formulation and stability strategy;
- assessing a formulation, material, supplier, filter, hold, fill-finish,
  container, shipping, storage, method, site, scale, or process change;
- connecting product degradation, analytical capability, manufacturing exposure,
  the complete presentation, and stability studies;
- preparing evidence for qualified product, quality, validation, and regulatory review.

## Do not use as

- a platform formulation, process recipe, study schedule, or shelf-life calculator;
- an aseptic-process, filter, container-integrity, shipping, or validation protocol;
- a comparability conclusion, regulatory filing, batch record, or disposition system;
- a substitute for product-specific formulation, analytical, stability,
  fill-finish, quality, validation, container, or regulatory review;
- an unchanged template for advanced modalities.

## Workflow

1. **Frame the decision.** Freeze the product, concentration context,
   formulation, process, presentation, route, markets, lifecycle stage, and owner.
2. **Map degradation.** Record product-specific physical, chemical, functional,
   particle, interaction, integrity, and performance questions with evidence strength.
3. **Map formulation hypotheses.** Link each component and property to its
   intended function, material evidence, compatibility, limitations, and triggers.
4. **Map process and fill-finish exposure.** Trace compounding, filtration,
   holds, transfer, filling, closing, handling, packaging, shipping, and use.
5. **Assess the complete presentation.** Separate component conformance from
   system protection, compatibility, safety, performance, and integrity evidence.
6. **Demonstrate analytical coverage.** Explain what each stability-indicating
   method can and cannot detect and distinguish from measurement variability.
7. **Build the study matrix.** Link representative material, condition and
   schedule references, tests, inventory, protocol, deviations, and conclusion owner.
8. **Preserve change and contradiction.** Keep observations, competing
   mechanisms, commitments, actions, and accountable decisions visible.

## Required review roles

- formulation development and product-quality sciences;
- fill-finish, MSAT, process engineering, and validation as applicable;
- analytical development, bioassay, and stability sciences;
- container-closure or device engineering when the presentation is affected;
- quality and regulatory CMC.

## Revision watch

The consolidated ICH Q1 guideline was a Step 2 draft at the time this package was
prepared. It is monitored as revision activity and is not represented here as the
current effective replacement for Q1A-F or Q5C.

## Limitation

This package structures evidence. It does not establish a formulation,
degradation mechanism, process, presentation, container suitability or integrity,
sterility assurance, stability protocol, shipping qualification, storage
condition, shelf life, validation status, comparability, regulatory acceptability,
or batch disposition.
