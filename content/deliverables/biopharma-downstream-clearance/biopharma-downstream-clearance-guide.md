# Biopharma Downstream Purification & Clearance Evidence Map

**Version:** 1.0.0-review

**Status:** Editorial reviewed

**Document purpose:** Educational decision and evidence-mapping support

## Decision this package supports

Which downstream unit operations contribute to product-quality control,
impurity clearance, viral safety, and recovery for a defined product and
process, and what evidence supports or limits each contribution?

## Use when

- building or challenging a downstream purification control strategy;
- assessing a resin, membrane, supplier, reuse, scale, site, equipment, load,
  hold, or process change;
- connecting upstream load, unit-operation performance, product quality, and
  final controls;
- preparing impurity-clearance or viral-safety evidence for qualified review.

## Do not use as

- a universal purification platform or step sequence;
- a source of operating ranges, lifetimes, impurity limits, or clearance factors;
- a viral-clearance protocol, validation report, comparability conclusion,
  regulatory filing, or batch record;
- a substitute for product-specific downstream, analytical, viral-safety,
  quality, validation, or regulatory review;
- an unchanged template for advanced modalities.

## Workflow

1. **Frame the decision.** Record product, modality, lifecycle stage, process
   version, site, scale, materials, and accountable owner.
2. **Define targets.** Separate intended product, product-related variants,
   process-related impurities, contaminants, viral-safety targets, and recovery.
3. **Map the actual train.** Record unit operations, loads, intermediates,
   handoffs, holds, lifecycle state, and relevant measurements.
4. **Write claims separately.** Do not combine impurity, product-variant,
   viral, and recovery claims into one clearance statement.
5. **Map evidence.** Preserve mechanism, study/model scope, analytical limits,
   contradictory findings, and controlled references.
6. **Challenge viral claims.** Record the scale-down model, virus and method
   rationale, step mechanism, robustness basis, limitations, and qualified review.
7. **Check lifecycle boundaries.** Include resin or membrane reuse, load,
   cleaning, storage, scale, equipment, process, method, and change triggers.
8. **Assign actions and review.** Resolve all formula-driven attention flags
   before transferring content into a controlled system.

## Claim-family definitions

| Claim family | Required separation |
| --- | --- |
| Process-related impurity | Target, source, load, contributing steps, method, control, and lifecycle boundary |
| Product-related variant | Variant or attribute, product relevance, formation or separation mechanism, recovery trade-off, and evidence |
| Viral inactivation/removal | Virus rationale, step mechanism, scale-down model, study conditions, robustness, limitations, and controlled conclusion |
| Recovery/process performance | Product recovery and consistent operation without treating either as proof of quality or viral safety |

## Required review roles

- downstream process development or MSAT;
- analytical development and product-quality sciences;
- viral-safety or virology reviewer for viral claims;
- quality, validation, or change-control owner;
- regulatory CMC when a filing or registered control may be affected.

## Limitation

This package structures evidence. It does not establish a purification train,
product-specific clearance, viral safety, causality, robustness, operating
range, resin or membrane lifetime, validation status, comparability, regulatory
acceptability, or batch disposition.
