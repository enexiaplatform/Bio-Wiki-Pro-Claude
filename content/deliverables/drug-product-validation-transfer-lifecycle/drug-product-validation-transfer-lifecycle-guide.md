# Transfer-to-lifecycle decision guide

## Decision question

How does a drug-product control strategy remain traceable through validation,
receiving-site transfer and lifecycle change?

## Bounded workflow

1. Freeze transfer scope, product/presentation, process version, receiving unit,
   market and decision owner.
2. Map knowledge transfer, equipment/material differences, analytical readiness,
   training, validation and change-control evidence.
3. Preserve open contradictions, deviations, commitments and effectiveness
   signals; do not collapse them into transfer success.
4. Record the authorized reviewers and the evidence required for staged decisions.
5. Carry the evidence map into continued lifecycle review after implementation.

## Discovery questions

- Which validation and transfer evidence demonstrates control at the receiving site?
- What site, equipment, material or method difference could invalidate a simple transfer assumption?
- Which change-control or continued-verification signal should trigger renewed review?

## Controlled source map

| Source | Decision use | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| WHO-TRS-1044-ANNEX4 | Structure technology-transfer expectations | Confirm current version and applicability |
| ICH-Q9-R1 | Prioritize transfer and change risks | Risk ranking cannot replace evidence |
| ICH-Q10 | Govern knowledge, change and continued improvement | Does not authorize implementation |
| FDA-PROCESS-VALIDATION-2011 | Frame lifecycle validation evidence | Does not prescribe a universal validation design |

No universal protocol, batch count, acceptance criterion, validation conclusion
or regulatory filing determination is supplied.

## Next handoff

Carry unresolved differences, validation evidence, commitments and effectiveness
signals into continued lifecycle review. Qualified and authorized transfer, operations,
engineering, analytical, validation, quality and regulatory CMC roles own the
product-specific decision.
