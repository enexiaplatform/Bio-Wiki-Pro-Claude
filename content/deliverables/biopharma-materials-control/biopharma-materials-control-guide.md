# Biopharma Materials & Single-Use Control Evidence Map

**Version:** 1.0.0-review

**Status:** Editorial-reviewed; not SME-approved

**Document purpose:** Educational decision and evidence-mapping support

## Decision this package supports

For a defined protein biologic, process step, lifecycle stage, material or
component version, supplier/site/sub-tier/sterilization configuration and
intended use, what source, functional, process/product, contamination,
single-use, qualification, comparability and change evidence must be resolved
before authorized owners can qualify, use or accept the change?

## Use when

- qualifying or changing raw/ancillary materials, media/feed components,
  process aids, excipients, resins, membranes, filters or consumables;
- assessing a supplier, manufacturing/test site, sub-tier input, material
  process, formulation, packaging, transport, storage or sterilization change;
- assessing single-use components and configured assemblies;
- reconciling incoming controls and supplier data with functional,
  process-performance and product-quality evidence;
- preparing a bounded decision for qualified cross-functional review.

## Do not use as

- supplier approval, material release, change approval, regulatory decision or
  batch disposition;
- a source of specifications, tests, sampling/testing frequency, acceptance
  limits, qualification-lot counts, E&L conditions or toxicology thresholds;
- proof that an unchanged material name, grade, part number, drawing, CoA or
  release specification means unchanged function or product impact;
- proof that a sterilization certificate establishes integrity, compatibility,
  sterility assurance or process suitability;
- an unchanged template for modalities outside its protein-biologics scope.

## Workflow

1. **Freeze material and use.** Record exact identity, version, supplier,
   manufacturer, site, sub-tier, sterilization, process step, contact,
   lifecycle stage, markets and change reference.
2. **Map contact and function.** State direct/indirect contact, exposure,
   material function, plausible failure modes and affected process/product
   decisions.
3. **Trace the supply chain.** Map source, processing, testing, packaging,
   transport, storage, sterilization and change notification.
4. **Define functional attributes.** Separate supplier controls, incoming
   tests, process controls, product evidence and unresolved observability.
5. **Assess the configured SUS.** Reconcile component/assembly design,
   connections, integrity, sterilization, handling, compatibility and E&L
   evidence with the intended use.
6. **Translate the change.** State exactly what changed and map each plausible
   impact to qualification, process/product, contamination, comparability,
   validation, stability and regulatory evidence.
7. **Preserve distinct gates.** Keep supplier approval, material
   qualification, incoming release, process use, change implementation,
   comparability and continuity decisions separate.
8. **Close with accountable review.** Reconcile evidence, contradictions,
   deviations, conditions, actions, residual risks and lifecycle triggers.

## Required review roles

- material/change owner and quality unit;
- raw-material or material science and supplier quality;
- supply chain/procurement with change-notification ownership;
- upstream/downstream/formulation/MSAT and manufacturing, as applicable;
- process engineering and single-use-system expertise;
- analytical development, statistics and QC, as applicable;
- microbiology, contamination control, viral safety or biosafety, as applicable;
- extractables/leachables and toxicology expertise, as applicable;
- qualification/validation and regulatory CMC.

## Applicability and limitations

This package structures material evidence and decision ownership. It does not
establish material suitability, supplier approval, incoming control adequacy,
test reduction, contamination or viral safety, single-use system suitability,
extractables/leachables safety, process validation, product comparability,
regulatory acceptability, change implementation, supply continuity, batch
disposition or product release.

The applicable product, process, material, market, filing, site procedure and
current source edition must be confirmed. The fictional example is not a
benchmark or recommended study design.
