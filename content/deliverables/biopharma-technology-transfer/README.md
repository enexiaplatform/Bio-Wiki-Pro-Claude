# Biopharma Technology Transfer Evidence Map — Start Here

This package helps a cross-functional biologics team turn a bounded transfer
into a traceable evidence and acceptance system across knowledge, materials,
facilities, equipment, process, analytical methods, comparability, validation,
commitments, governance, and routine readiness.

**Package version:** 1.0.0-review

**Review status:** editorial-reviewed

**Decision owner:** the authorized transfer steering team and quality unit,
supported by product/process development, MSAT, manufacturing, engineering,
automation/data, analytical development, QC, validation, supply, and regulatory
CMC owners — not Atlas

## Package contents

1. `biopharma-technology-transfer-guide.pdf` — decision workflow, evidence
   model, sources, reviewer roles, and controlled-use boundaries.
2. `biopharma-technology-transfer-v1.xlsx` — blank formula-driven working file
   with transfer charter, knowledge, capability, process/material, analytical,
   validation/commitment, evidence, action, source, and review controls.
3. `biopharma-technology-transfer-v1-fictional-example.xlsx` — completed
   fictional CHO monoclonal-antibody drug-substance transfer example preserving
   equipment, automation, material, method, reference, comparability,
   validation, and acceptance questions without resolving them.

## Recommended order

1. Freeze one product/process/method/site version and an explicit in-scope and
   out-of-scope transfer charter.
2. Name the sending unit, receiving unit, decision owner, acceptance authority,
   lifecycle stage, markets, and change-control reference.
3. Map every required knowledge object to receiving-unit use, version,
   limitation, training or demonstration, evidence, and owner.
4. Compare facilities, equipment, utilities, automation, and data systems by
   function and product/process impact rather than labels or model numbers.
5. Connect materials and each unit operation to the product-process control
   strategy, including differences, hypotheses, controls, and open evidence.
6. Run analytical transfer as a distinct evidence system covering procedure,
   matrix, samples, standards, reagents, instrument/software, reportability,
   validation, transfer or bridging, and routine QC readiness.
7. Keep comparability, qualification, process validation, stability,
   regulatory implementation, execution readiness, and routine readiness as
   distinct gates.
8. Reconcile deviations, acceptance criteria, commitments, actions, reviewer
   roles, and final project acceptance without silently treating conditions as
   closure.
9. Resolve every `Incomplete`, `Evidence required`, or `Review required` flag.
10. Transfer only qualified, approved content into the site's controlled
    transfer, validation, quality, manufacturing, QC, or regulatory system.

## Important boundary

The package supplies no product-specific process, parameter, range, material,
equipment-equivalence rule, method, transfer approach, validation design,
batch count, acceptance criterion, comparability conclusion, filing category,
execution authorization, routine-readiness decision, site approval, or batch
disposition.

Protocol completion is not successful transfer. Successful engineering or
qualification activity is not regulatory approval or routine readiness.
Method transfer, process transfer, comparability, validation, and filing are
connected but separate decisions.

Every product-specific claim requires controlled provenance and qualified
review.
