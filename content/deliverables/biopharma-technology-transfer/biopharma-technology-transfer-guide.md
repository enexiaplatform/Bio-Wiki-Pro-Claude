# Biopharma Technology Transfer Evidence Map

**Version:** 1.0.0-review

**Status:** Under review

**Document purpose:** Educational decision and evidence-mapping support

## Decision this package supports

For a defined product, process and analytical version, sending unit, receiving
unit, lifecycle stage, market commitment, and transfer scope, what knowledge,
capability, bridging, validation, comparability, governance, and acceptance
evidence must be closed before authorized owners can accept the transfer?

## Use when

- planning or reviewing a development-to-manufacturing or site-to-site
  biologics process transfer;
- transferring a bounded drug-substance, drug-product, fill-finish, packaging,
  testing, storage, or supporting-system scope;
- comparing facility, equipment, utility, automation, material, supply,
  process, method, reference, reagent, data, and people capabilities;
- reconciling transfer execution with comparability, qualification, process
  validation, stability, regulatory commitments, and routine readiness;
- preparing evidence for qualified cross-functional and quality-unit review.

## Do not use as

- a product-specific transfer protocol, validation plan, comparability plan,
  quality agreement, filing strategy, or regulatory assessment;
- a source of process parameters, ranges, methods, batch counts, sampling,
  validation designs, acceptance criteria, or filing categories;
- execution authorization, site approval, transfer acceptance, regulatory
  approval, routine-readiness approval, or batch disposition;
- evidence that equipment identity establishes equivalence or that equipment
  differences require literal duplication;
- an unchanged template for modalities outside the source and reviewer scope.

## Workflow

1. **Freeze the charter.** Record product, presentation, process/method
   versions, sending and receiving units, lifecycle stage, markets, scope,
   exclusions, owner, authority, and change reference.
2. **Map decision knowledge.** Link each controlled knowledge object to its
   receiving-unit use, source, version, limitation, demonstration, evidence,
   and owner.
3. **Assess functional fit.** Compare facilities, equipment, utilities,
   automation, data, maintenance, cleaning, sterilization, containment, and
   interfaces by function and product/process impact.
4. **Preserve the product-process-material chain.** Connect inputs, unit
   operations, parameters, performance, IPCs, outputs, quality attributes,
   deviations, and controls without inventing ranges or criticality.
5. **Transfer analytical capability.** Freeze procedure purpose, matrix,
   sample, reference, reagent, instrument, software, calculation,
   reportability, validation, training, transfer or bridging, and routine use.
6. **Map change impact.** Link every difference to product/process risk,
   representative evidence, comparability, stability, and escalation.
7. **Reconcile validation.** State the distinct question, prerequisite,
   protocol/rationale, representative scope, deviation, result, reviewer, and
   residual commitment for each qualification or validation activity.
8. **Use staged gates.** Separate knowledge, facility/equipment, material,
   analytical, execution, validation/comparability, regulatory, routine, and
   project-acceptance decisions.
9. **Preserve conditions.** Keep open evidence, deviations, residual risks,
   commitments, owners, due dates, and reviewer disagreements explicit.
10. **Qualify acceptance.** Transfer approved decisions into authorized
    systems only after the appropriate owner accepts the exact scope.

## Required review roles

- transfer lead and quality unit;
- product/process development and product-quality sciences;
- upstream and downstream MSAT/manufacturing sciences, as applicable;
- manufacturing operations, engineering, utilities, automation/data, and
  maintenance;
- materials management, supply chain and supplier quality;
- analytical development, bioassay/statistics, reference-material ownership,
  QC and data governance;
- qualification and validation;
- regulatory CMC and market-commitment ownership;
- environmental, health, safety, biosafety or containment expertise where
  applicable.

## Limitation

This package structures transfer evidence and decision ownership. It does not
establish facility or equipment suitability, process robustness, material
equivalence, analytical equivalence, product comparability, validation,
stability, regulatory acceptability, execution readiness, routine readiness,
site approval, or batch disposition.
