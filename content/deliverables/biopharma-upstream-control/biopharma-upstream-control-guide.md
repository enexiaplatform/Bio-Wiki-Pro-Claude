# Biopharma Upstream Control Evidence Map

**Version:** 1.0.0-review

**Status:** Editorial-reviewed; not SME-approved
**Document purpose:** Educational decision and evidence-mapping support

## Decision this package supports

Which upstream material attributes, process parameters, and performance
indicators require control or monitoring for a defined product and process, and
what evidence links them to product-quality attributes?

## Use when

- building or challenging an upstream control strategy;
- preparing a scale, site, equipment, material, or process change;
- identifying process-characterization evidence gaps;
- connecting upstream evidence to comparability or continued verification.

## Do not use as

- a universal CQA, CPP, or material-attribute list;
- a source of platform operating ranges or acceptance criteria;
- a PPQ protocol, batch record, validation report, or regulatory filing;
- a substitute for product-specific process development, MSAT, quality, or
  regulatory review;
- an unchanged template for advanced modalities.

## Workflow

1. **Frame the decision.** Record product, modality, lifecycle stage, process
   version, scale, site, and accountable owner.
2. **Map product attributes.** Separate supported criticality, hypothesis,
   not-material-within-scope, and unresolved status.
3. **Map the process.** Describe each relevant stage, input, parameter,
   performance indicator, output, and handoff.
4. **Write relationship statements.** Name the scope, proposed mechanism,
   affected attribute, evidence, uncertainty, and direction.
5. **Grade evidence.** Keep hypotheses, observed associations, designed-study
   support, multi-source support, and contradictory evidence distinct.
6. **Place controls.** Record material, procedural, parameter, in-process,
   downstream, analytical, or lifecycle control and why it is sufficient.
7. **Record provenance.** Reference the controlled source of every numeric range
   or criterion; leave it evidence-required when provenance is absent.
8. **Assign review and triggers.** Identify accountable roles, open actions,
   contradictory evidence, and lifecycle re-evaluation triggers.

## Evidence-strength definitions

| State | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Hypothesis | Plausible or platform-derived; product evidence is insufficient |
| Observed association | Product data show a relationship, but causal interpretation is limited |
| Designed-study support | A planned study challenged the relationship within a defined scope |
| Multi-source support | Designed studies, manufacturing experience, and orthogonal evidence converge |
| Contradictory or unresolved | Evidence conflicts or the relevant scope was not adequately studied |

## Required review roles

- upstream process development or MSAT;
- analytical development or product-quality sciences;
- raw-material or cell-line subject matter owner where applicable;
- quality/change-control owner;
- regulatory CMC owner when the decision affects a filing or registered control.

## Limitation

This package structures evidence. It does not establish product-specific
criticality, causality, control ranges, validation status, comparability,
regulatory acceptability, or batch disposition.
