# Cleanroom classification & limits under EU GMP Annex 1 (2022) & ISO 14644

Cleanroom classification is far more than a room rating. Under **EU GMP Annex 1 (revised 2022)** and **ISO 14644-1:2015**, cleanroom classification is the empirical foundation of a facility's **Contamination Control Strategy (CCS)** for sterile drug product manufacturing.

Regulators require a clear distinction between **Classification** (initial and periodic qualification proving cleanroom design capability) and **Environmental Monitoring** (routine daily operational surveillance).

Here is an authoritative guide on Annex 1 cleanroom grades, exact airborne non-viable particle limits, microbiological viable limits, air velocity specifications, and ISO sampling location formulas.

---

## 1. Grade A to D Classification & Limits (EU GMP Annex 1 2022)

Annex 1 establishes four cleanroom grades based on operational risk to open sterile product, components, and primary containers.

```text
Grade A: Critical Aseptic Core (Unidirectional airflow 0.36-0.54 m/s)
  ▲
  │ Surrounded by
  │
Grade B: Aseptic Preparation & Background Cleanroom (Open Aseptic Processing)
  ▲
  │ Served by
  │
Grade C: Preparation of Solutions to be Sterilized / Component Assembly
  ▲
  │ Served by
  │
Grade D: Lower Risk Background Support (Washing, Component Staging)
```

## 2. Maximum Permitted Airborne Particle Limits (Annex 1 Table 1)

Classification requires measuring non-viable airborne particles at two particle sizes: ≥ 0.5 µm and ≥ 5.0 µm, in both **At-Rest** and **In-Operation** states.

| Grade | At Rest: ≥ 0.5 µm/m³ | At Rest: ≥ 5.0 µm/m³ | In Operation: ≥ 0.5 µm/m³ | In Operation: ≥ 5.0 µm/m³ | ISO Equivalent (At Rest / In Op) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Grade A** | 3,520 | 29 | 3,520 | 29 | ISO 5 / ISO 5 |
| **Grade B** | 3,520 | 29 | 352,000 | 2,900 | ISO 5 / ISO 7 |
| **Grade C** | 352,000 | 2,900 | 3,520,000 | 29,000 | ISO 7 / ISO 8 |
| **Grade D** | 3,520,000 | 29,000 | Not Defined | Not Defined | ISO 8 / Unclassified |

### Key Technical Rule for ≥ 5.0 µm Particles in Grade A:
The limit for ≥ 5.0 µm particles in Grade A is **29 particles/m³** during classification. In the 2022 revision, a single count of a ≥ 5.0 µm particle is no longer an automatic failure, but consecutive counts require immediate engineering investigation because macro-particles (≥ 5.0 µm) are primary vectors for microbial spores.

## 3. Microbiological Viable Contamination Limits (Annex 1 Table 2)

Microbiological limits apply during dynamic operational state and media fill qualification.

| Grade | Air Sample (CFU/m³) | Settle Plates (Ø 90 mm, CFU/4 hr) | Contact Plates (Ø 55 mm, CFU/plate) | Glove Print (5 Fingers, CFU/glove) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Grade A** | &lt; 1 | &lt; 1 | &lt; 1 | &lt; 1 |
| **Grade B** | 10 | 5 | 5 | 5 |
| **Grade C** | 50 | 50 | 25 | N/A |
| **Grade D** | 100 | 100 | 50 | N/A |

*Acceptance Rule:* In Grade A, any growth (≥ 1 CFU) anywhere on settle plates, contact plates, or operator gloves triggers an immediate formal **Action Level Excursion Investigation** and species-level identification.

## 4. Minimum Sampling Locations (ISO 14644-1:2015 Formula)

During cleanroom qualification, the minimum number of airborne particle sampling locations (`N_L`) is calculated directly from the floor area of the cleanroom suite:

```text
N_L = ceil(sqrt(A))
```

Where:
- `A` = Surface area of the cleanroom in square metres (m²).
- `ceil()` = Ceiling function (round up to next whole integer).

#### Example Calculation:
For a Grade B filling suite measuring 12 m × 8 m = 96 m²:

```text
N_L = ceil(sqrt(96)) = ceil(9.79) = 10 sampling locations
```

*Sample Volume Rule:* At each location, a minimum sample volume of **1,000 litres (1.0 m³)** must be sampled for Grade A areas to reliably quantify the ≥ 5.0 µm particle limit (29/m³).

## 5. Airflow Velocity & Recovery Time Parameters

### Airflow Velocity (Grade A Unidirectional Airflow)
- Grade A zones must maintain continuous unidirectional laminar airflow at a homogenous velocity of **0.36 to 0.54 m/s (70–100 ft/min)** at the working position.
- Smoke pattern studies (airflow visualization) must demonstrate laminar flow without turbulence or eddying over open containers.

### Cleanroom Recovery Time (Clean-Up Period)
- Following a temporary disruption or operator intervention, cleanrooms must demonstrate a **Clean-up / Recovery Time of &lt; 15-20 minutes** to return from an operational particle state to the baseline at-rest particle specification.

## 6. Cleanroom Monitoring & Quality Lab Capacity Integration

Managing cleanroom classification and routine EM for a sterile filling site creates significant laboratory plate-reading and particle counter calibration demands:
- 10 Grade A/B rooms sampled daily generates over **150 contact/settle plates per shift**.
- Incubation involves 20–25 °C (SDA/TSA for fungi, 3–5 days) followed by 30–35 °C (TSA for bacteria, 2–3 days).
