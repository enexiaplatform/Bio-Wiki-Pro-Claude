# Cleaning validation: the paradigm shift from 10 ppm & 1/1,000th dose to HBEL / PDE

Cleaning validation in shared pharmaceutical manufacturing facilities has undergone a profound regulatory transformation over the past decade.

Historically, cleaning acceptance criteria relied on arbitrary rule-of-thumb heuristics: **10 ppm in the next product**, **$1/1,000\text{th}$ of the minimum therapeutic dose**, or **visually clean**.

While simple to compute, these legacy rules failed to reflect toxicological risk. A $10 \text{ ppm}$ limit could be dangerously lenient for a highly potent cytotoxic API or potent hormone, while being excessively punitive for a non-toxic vitamin or amino acid.

Today, global regulators (**EMA/CHMP/CVMP/SWP/169430/2012**, **EU GMP Annex 15**, **FDA**, and **PIC/S PI 006**) mandate that cleaning carryover limits must be anchored to **Health-Based Exposure Limits (HBEL)**, expressed as a **Permitted Daily Exposure (PDE)** or **Acceptable Daily Exposure (ADE)**.

---

## 1. Why Legacy Criteria Failed Regulatory Scrutiny

| Legacy Criterion | Formula / Rule | Toxicological Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **10 ppm Criterion** | $\le 10 \text{ mg}$ previous API per $\text{kg}$ ($10 \text{ ppm}$) of next batch | Completely ignores compound toxicity. Fails to protect patients from highly potent low-dose APIs (PDE &lt; 10 µg/day). |
| **1/1,000th Dose** | $\text{MACO} = \frac{\text{Min Therapeutic Dose}_A \times \text{Batch Size}_B}{1000 \times \text{Max Daily Dose}_B}$ | Relies on therapeutic efficacy rather than toxicological safety. Fails for early-stage APIs without established clinical dose data, and ignores non-dose toxicities (genotoxicity, carcinogenicity). |
| **Visual Clean Only** | No visible residue under $500 \text{ lux}$ light | Subjective. Many potent compounds are active at sub-visible residue levels (&lt; 1-4 µg/cm²). |

## 2. Mathematical Derivation of Permitted Daily Exposure (PDE)

The **PDE** represents a substance-specific dose that is unlikely to cause adverse effects if an individual is exposed to this dose or lower every day for a lifetime.

### The Pharmacological PDE Formula

$$\text{PDE (mg/day)} = \frac{\text{NOAEL (mg/kg/day)} \times \text{Human Body Weight (50 kg)}}{F_1 \times F_2 \times F_3 \times F_4 \times F_5}$$

Where:
- $\text{NOAEL}$ = No Observed Adverse Effect Level ($\text{mg/kg/day}$) obtained from animal or human studies. (If unavailable, $\text{LOAEL}$ — Lowest Observed Adverse Effect Level — is used with $F_5$).
- $\text{Human Body Weight}$ = Standardized regulatory default of **$50 \text{ kg}$** for adult patients.
- $F_1\text{--}F_5$ = Five safety adjustment factors:

### Toxicological Adjustment Factors ($F_1$ to $F_5$)

| Adjustment Factor | Scope & Scientific Rationale | Value Range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **$F_1$ (Species Extrapolation)** | Extrapolates from animal species to humans | $F_1 = 2$ (monkeys), $F_1 = 5$ (dogs), $F_1 = 12$ (mice), $F_1 = 5$ (rats), $F_1 = 10$ (other) |
| **$F_2$ (Inter-Individual Variation)** | Accounts for variability between human individuals | Standard default $F_2 = 10$ |
| **$F_3$ (Study Duration)** | Accounts for short-term toxicity studies vs. lifetime exposure | $F_3 = 1$ (lifetime study), $F_3 = 2$ (6-month rodent), $F_3 = 5$ (3-month rodent), $F_3 = 10$ (short-term) |
| **$F_4$ (Severe Toxicity)** | Applied for severe toxicity (e.g., non-genotoxic carcinogenicity, teratogenicity, neurotoxicity) | $F_4 = 1\text{--}10$ based on severity |
| **$F_5$ (LOAEL to NOAEL)** | Applied when only a LOAEL is available instead of a NOAEL | $F_5 = 1$ (if NOAEL exists), $F_5 = 2\text{--}10$ (if relying on LOAEL) |

## 3. Comparative Worked Example: 10 ppm vs. 1/1,000th Dose vs. PDE MACO

### Case Scenario:
- **Previous Product A:** Potent Immunosuppressant. $\text{NOAEL} = 0.5 \text{ mg/kg/day}$. $F_1=5, F_2=10, F_3=5, F_4=10, F_5=1$. Min dose $= 1.0 \text{ mg/day}$.
- **Next Product B:** Painkiller tablet. Batch size $\text{MBS}_B = 100 \text{ kg} = 100,000,000 \text{ mg}$. Max daily dose $\text{MDD}_B = 2,000 \text{ mg/day}$. Shared surface area $A_{\text{total}} = 300,000 \text{ cm}^2$.

### Step 1: Calculate PDE for Product A
$$\text{PDE}_A = \frac{0.5 \text{ mg/kg/day} \times 50 \text{ kg}}{5 \times 10 \times 5 \times 10 \times 1} = \frac{25 \text{ mg/day}}{2,500} = 0.010 \text{ mg/day} = 10 \:\mu\text{g/day}$$

### Step 2: Compare MACO across the 3 Criteria

1. **HBEL / PDE-Based MACO:**
   $$\text{MACO}_{\text{PDE}} = \frac{0.010 \text{ mg/day} \times 100,000,000 \text{ mg}}{2,000 \text{ mg/day}} = 500 \text{ mg}$$

2. **1/1,000th Therapeutic Dose MACO:**
   $$\text{MACO}_{\text{Dose}} = \frac{1.0 \text{ mg/day} \times 100,000,000 \text{ mg}}{1,000 \times 2,000 \text{ mg/day}} = 50 \text{ mg}$$

3. **10 ppm General Limit MACO:**
   $$\text{MACO}_{10\text{ppm}} = 10 \text{ mg/kg} \times 100 \text{ kg} = 1,000 \text{ mg}$$

*Regulatory Insight:* The $10 \text{ ppm}$ limit ($1,000 \text{ mg}$) is **twice as high** as the toxicologically safe PDE limit ($500 \text{ mg}$), creating patient risk. Conversely, for non-toxic compounds, $10 \text{ ppm}$ causes artificial re-cleaning failures. The PDE-derived limit ($500 \text{ mg}$) is the only scientifically defensible criterion for QA release.

## 4. Total Organic Carbon (TOC) Limits for Cleaning Agents

Cleaning validation must validate the removal of **cleaning agents (detergents)** as well as APIs. Because commercial detergents are proprietary mixtures of surfactants, builders, and chelators, **Total Organic Carbon (TOC)** is widely used for non-specific residue testing.

$$\text{TOC Limit (ppm)} = \frac{\text{PDE}_{\text{detergent}} (\text{mg/day}) \times \text{Carbon Ratio} \times \text{MBS}_B (\text{mg})}{\text{MDD}_B (\text{mg/day}) \times V_{\text{rinse}} (\text{L})}$$

Where:
- $\text{Carbon Ratio}$ = Percentage of carbon in the detergent formulation (obtained from detergent manufacturer CoA).
- $V_{\text{rinse}}$ = Volume of final rinse water ($\text{L}$).

## 5. Summary: Navigating an Audit on HBEL & MACO

When an inspector audits your cleaning validation program, be prepared to present:
1. **Signed Toxicologist PDE Monographs:** Qualified toxicologist signature and documented $F_1\text{--}F_5$ safety factor justifications for every API.
2. **Living Product Matrix:** Change-control SOP requiring automated recalculation of MACO limits whenever a new product is introduced or an API PDE is updated.
3. **Analytical Recovery Data:** Coupon recovery factors ($R \ge 70\%$) for every product-contact surface material (316L SS, PTFE, EPDM, Glass).
