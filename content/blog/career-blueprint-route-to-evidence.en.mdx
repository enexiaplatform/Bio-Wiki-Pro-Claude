# Career Blueprint: turn a route hypothesis into evidence

A career route is not a personality label. It is a decision under uncertainty:

> Given the work you have done, the evidence others can verify, your constraints
> and the roles you are considering, which route is credible to test next?

That framing works for a QC microbiologist considering QA, an analytical scientist
moving toward method lifecycle work, a manufacturing-quality professional moving
into MSAT, or a Biopharma specialist deciding between technical depth and broader
quality ownership.

## Start with the route, not the title

Decompose a target role into requirements that can be observed. Separate recurring
requirements from employer-specific preferences and record the source and date of
each requirement. A useful matrix asks:

- What decision does this capability support?
- What evidence would a manager, reviewer or hiring team recognize?
- Is the evidence a self-rating, an observed contribution or reviewer-confirmed
  ownership?
- What constraint, assumption or missing signal could change the route?

The goal is not to make a perfect labour-market prediction. It is to make the next
evidence request explicit and testable.

## Keep three evidence states separate

### Self-rating

You believe you can perform the work. This is useful for choosing a practice
experiment, but it is not independent proof.

### Observed evidence

You have completed or contributed to a bounded activity and can describe the
context, action, outcome and limitation. Confidential records must be sanitized.

### Reviewer-confirmed evidence

A qualified manager, technical reviewer, mentor or other accountable person has
challenged the claim and recorded what was accepted, corrected or left open.

Strong positioning usually comes from the third state. The Blueprint should never
inflate participation into ownership or treat a certificate as evidence of a
product-specific result.

## A 13-week evidence sequence

The workspace is designed around four phases:

1. **Validate the route.** Decode real requirements, inventory evidence and test
   assumptions with a reviewer.
2. **Build priority proof.** Create one bounded work sample with a clear outcome,
   boundary, reviewer and stop condition.
3. **Prove ownership.** Obtain challenge, revise the claim and make contribution
   and outcome observable without exposing confidential data.
4. **Run the transition.** Translate accepted evidence into positioning, test the
   story in real conversations and choose continue, adjust or pivot.

Every week should produce at least one artifact, feedback checkpoint or decision
signal. "Read more" or "update my CV" is not enough unless it is tied to a
reviewable output.

## Worked fictional profile

Consider a QC analyst with strong execution experience, two investigation
contributions and a self-rated interest in Biopharma process development. The
Blueprint may keep a quality-systems route as the primary option, test a process
control route as an adjacent option and identify three gaps:

- no reviewer-confirmed ownership of a cross-functional investigation;
- no bounded product-process evidence chain; and
- no external conversation testing whether the story is credible.

The route is not rejected because the profile is incomplete. The next two weeks
can create decision signals:

- Week 1: a requirement matrix with evidence state and unknowns for two real role
  families;
- Week 2: a sanitized investigation brief reviewed by a manager or qualified
  mentor, with contribution limits and one corrected claim.

If the reviewer cannot confirm the ownership claim or the work context does not
support the proposed route, the decision should adjust. That is a useful outcome,
not a failure of the Blueprint.

## The route decision gate

At the end of the cycle, record:

- the evidence gained and its classification;
- what a reviewer accepted, corrected or could not verify;
- which assumption changed;
- which route remains credible and which route was ruled out for now;
- the next review date; and
- the next 90-day commitment or fallback experiment.

The result is a Career Operating Blueprint: a personalized decision record and
execution cadence. It is not a hiring guarantee, competence score, compensation
prediction or substitute for a real employer conversation.
