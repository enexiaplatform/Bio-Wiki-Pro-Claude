# HVAC Qualification for Cleanrooms

The HVAC system is the engine of a cleanroom: it supplies filtered air,
maintains dilution and removal of contamination, holds pressure cascades, and
controls temperature and humidity. Qualifying it proves that the room actually
achieves and holds its classification. Two frameworks govern this work: the ISO
14644 series (14644-1 classification, 14644-2 monitoring, 14644-3 test methods)
and, for sterile manufacturing in the EU and PIC/S world, EU GMP Annex 1 (2022
revision), which defines Grades A to D and adds GMP expectations on top of the
particle classes.

## The qualification test menu

ISO 14644-3 lists the test methods used to qualify a cleanroom and its air
system. The qualification program selects the relevant ones and defines
acceptance criteria per room grade.

| Test | What it proves | Typical method basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Installed filter system leakage (HEPA integrity) | Filters and their seals are leak-free | Aerosol challenge (PAO or equivalent) upstream; downstream scan or probe sampling per ISO 14644-3 |
| Airflow velocity and volume | Supply air volume meets design | Anemometer, balometer/hood, or duct traverse |
| Air changes per hour (ACPH) | Sufficient dilution for the class | Calculated from measured supply volume and room volume |
| Room differential pressure | Cascade direction and magnitude hold | Calibrated differential pressure gauges across doorways |
| Airflow visualization | Flow direction protects critical zones | Smoke/fog study, recorded, in Grade A and key transfer points |
| Recovery test | Room returns to class after a particle challenge | 100:1 recovery-time method per ISO 14644-3 |
| Particle count classification | Room meets its ISO class at rest and in operation | ISO 14644-1 sampling plan and limits |
| Temperature and humidity | Environmental conditions stay within defined ranges | Calibrated sensors, mapped across the room |

The qualification sequence matters: balancing and pressure tests come before
filter integrity and particle classification, because airflow and cascade
changes alter everything downstream.

## Particle limits and Annex 1 grades

ISO 14644-1 defines classes by maximum permitted particle concentrations. The
values below are compendial/standard values (particles per cubic meter):

| ISO class | 0.5 micrometers and larger | 5.0 micrometers and larger |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ISO 5 | 3,520 | 29 (per ISO 14644-1:2015 table) |
| ISO 6 | 35,200 | 293 |
| ISO 7 | 352,000 | 2,930 |
| ISO 8 | 3,520,000 | 29,300 |

Annex 1 maps its grades onto these classes and adds at-rest/in-operation states
plus microbiological limits: Grade A corresponds to ISO 5 in both states for
particles, Grade B is ISO 5 at rest / ISO 7 in operation, Grade C is ISO 7 /
ISO 8, and Grade D is ISO 8 at rest (in-operation class is set by the site
based on risk). Microbiological action limits in Annex 1 are "no growth" for
Grade A and tighten progressively from Grade B to D.

## Air changes and airflow velocity

Air changes per hour are the dilution engine of a non-unidirectional cleanroom.
The numbers below are **industry-practice guidance, not compendial limits**:
ISO 14644 does not mandate ACPH values.

| Area | Industry-practice guidance |
| --- | --- |
| ISO 5 unidirectional (Grade A) | Unidirectional airflow, commonly 0.36-0.54 m/s (guidance range from FDA's 2004 aseptic processing guidance) |
| ISO 7 (Grade B background / C) | Commonly 20 or more ACPH as a design starting point |
| ISO 8 (Grade C/D support areas) | Commonly 10-20 ACPH |

The real acceptance criterion is performance: the measured particle counts,
recovery time, and pressure stability must demonstrate the class, whatever the
nominal ACPH. Rooms often need more air changes than the textbook value if
equipment, personnel density, or process emissions raise the particle load.

## Pressure cascades

Adjacent rooms of different grades are held at different pressures so air flows
from cleaner to less-clean areas when doors or transfer hatches open. Annex 1
gives guidance of a minimum of 10 Pascals between adjacent rooms of different
grade (with airlocks and pass-throughs bridging the steps). Two cautions:

- the cascade is verified with doors closed and challenged under worst-case
  operation (door openings, equipment doors, transfer operations);
- pressure alarms and continuous monitoring for critical cascades are expected
  in aseptic facilities, with alert and action limits and defined response
  times.

Cascade reversal, even briefly, is a contamination event for the cleaner side
and should be assessed under the deviation system.

## HEPA integrity testing

Installed-filter leak testing is the single most failure-prone qualification
test. An aerosol (PAO or an approved alternative) is generated upstream and the
downstream face and seal are scanned at close range with an aerosol photometer,
or sampled with a discrete particle counter where justified. The widely used
acceptance criterion is no downstream penetration above 0.01% of the upstream
challenge concentration (industry criterion, per ISO 14644-3 method). Leaks are
most often found at the gel or gasket seal and frame, not through the media
itself. Repair (patching) is possible within limits defined in the site
procedure; oversized or edge repairs usually mean filter replacement and retest.

## Recovery time

The recovery test asks: after a particle challenge (for example from a brief
door opening or a generated aerosol), how fast does the room return to its
classified particle level? ISO 14644-3 describes the 100:1 recovery method,
measuring the time for concentration to fall by a factor of 100. Annex 1
(2022) gives a guidance clean-up time of 15-20 minutes for Grades B and C in
its qualification expectations. Recovery time is one of the best single
indicators of real dilution performance: a room can pass static particle counts
and still recover slowly if airflow patterns are poor.

## Requalification frequency

- Annex 1 (2022): requalification of Grade A and B areas at least every 6
  months; Grade C and D at least every 12 months.
- ISO 14644-2 allows longer intervals (up to 12 months for ISO class 5 and
  cleaner, up to 24 months for ISO class 6 and less clean) where a documented
  monitoring program demonstrates continued compliance. For sterile
  manufacturing under EU/PIC/S GMP, the Annex 1 frequencies take precedence.

Requalification is also triggered by change: filter replacement, ductwork or
balancing changes, room layout or equipment changes, HVAC system maintenance
affecting airflow, and after any shutdown significant enough to disturb the
system's qualified state.

## From qualification to routine control

Qualification output feeds the site's Contamination Control Strategy (CCS) and
the environmental monitoring program: classified states, alert/action limits,
monitoring locations, and recovery expectations all trace back to what the
qualification demonstrated. Pressure differentials and ACPH drift as filters
load, dampers move, and belts age, so the trends from continuous monitoring
between requalifications are part of the qualified state, not an afterthought.

## Common GMP findings

- HEPA integrity test not performed, or performed but with the upstream
  challenge concentration never verified.
- Pressure cascade measured once at qualification and never trended; reversal
  events unnoticed between annual tests.
- Recovery test absent or acceptance criterion undefined.
- ACPH calculated from design drawings instead of measured airflow.
- Smoke studies missing for Grade A or for critical interventions; unrecorded
  studies that cannot be reviewed.
- Filter replacements and damper adjustments made under maintenance orders
  without change control or partial requalification.
- Particle classification performed "at rest" only, with no "in operation"
  data supporting the operating state.
- Temperature/humidity excursions treated as comfort issues rather than
  potential product and contamination-control impacts.

## Documentation checklist

- URS and design documentation for the air-handling system;
- balancing reports (airflow, ACPH, pressures);
- HEPA filter certificates and installed-leak test reports;
- airflow visualization study reports with recordings;
- recovery test data;
- ISO 14644-1 classification reports, at rest and in operation;
- temperature/humidity mapping;
- monitoring-system qualification (sensors, alarms, data recording);
- requalification schedule and change-control triggers.
