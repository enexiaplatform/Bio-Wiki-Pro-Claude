# Cell Bank Establishment & Characterization

Every batch of a biologic traces back to a vial of cells. If that vial is not
controlled, nothing downstream can be. The governing framework is ICH Q5D
(Derivation and Characterisation of Cell Substrates Used for Production of
Biotechnological/Biological Products), with ICH Q5A(R2) covering the viral
safety testing and ICH Q5B covering analysis of the recombinant expression
construct. Together they define the two-tiered banking system and the
characterization panel that makes a cell bank a qualified starting material.

## The two-tiered system: MCB and WCB

| Tier | Role | Access |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Master Cell Bank (MCB) | The definitive, fully characterized source, created from a single clone or homogeneous pool | Rarely accessed; only to manufacture a new WCB |
| Working Cell Bank (WCB) | Expanded from one or a few MCB vials; used to start routine production | Routine production use |
| End-of-production cells (EOPC) / cells at the limit (CAL) | Cells taken at or beyond the maximum production age | Tested once to prove stability across the production window |

The logic: the MCB is finite and precious. Every thaw risks contamination and
genetic drift, so routine work never touches it. When a WCB is exhausted, a new
WCB is prepared from the MCB and characterized (usually an abbreviated panel
focused on identity and purity, plus any tests justified by risk). A new MCB is
a major event with comparability implications, not a routine refresh.

## The characterization test panel

ICH Q5D expects the MCB to be characterized across several dimensions. The
exact panel depends on the cell line (CHO, HEK293, hybridoma, microbial) and
product, but the structure below is standard:

| Category | What it proves | Typical methods |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity | The cells are the claimed species and line | Isoenzyme analysis, STR profiling (human lines), DNA barcoding (COI) for species |
| Microbial purity | Free of bacteria and fungi | Compendial sterility testing (Ph. Eur. 2.6.1 / USP &lt;71&gt;) |
| Mycoplasma | Free of mycoplasma | Compendial culture plus indicator-cell DNA staining, or validated nucleic-acid methods (Ph. Eur. 2.6.7 / USP &lt;63&gt;) |
| Adventitious viruses | Free of contaminating viruses | In vitro virus assays on detector cell lines, in vivo assays where required, transmission electron microscopy |
| Endogenous/retrovirus | Quantify or characterize retroviral particles inherent to the line | Reverse transcriptase assay, infectivity assays, TEM particle counts |
| Genetic stability (with Q5B) | The expression construct is correct and stable | Sequencing of the construct, gene copy number, restriction mapping, transcript analysis |
| Phenotype/productivity | The cells express the intended product consistently | Product expression level and quality attributes at MCB vs EOPC |

Testing of the WCB is usually a reduced panel: identity, sterility, mycoplasma,
and targeted viral tests, since the MCB already carries the full dossier.

## Genetic stability and the limit of in vitro cell age

Cells drift. Gene copies can be lost, sequences can mutate, expression can
fade. ICH Q5D therefore requires that production stay within a validated
window, the **limit of in vitro cell age (LIVCA)**: the maximum number of
population doublings (or passages) from MCB thaw through harvest. Stability is
demonstrated by comparing cells and product at the MCB level against cells at
or beyond the LIVCA (EOPC/CAL testing): construct sequence and copy number,
product quality profile, and freedom from newly detected adventitious agents.

Production beyond the LIVCA is unqualified territory; a campaign that
approaches the limit needs assessment before, not after.

## Storage, inventory, and chain of custody

Characterization is wasted if the bank itself is not controlled:

- storage in the vapor phase of liquid nitrogen (industry standard practice to
  avoid cross-contamination risks associated with liquid-phase immersion);
- split storage of each bank at two geographically separate locations to
  protect against single-site loss;
- controlled inventory: every vial movement logged, with reconciliation;
- qualified freezers with temperature monitoring, alarms, and backup;
- documented bank history: origin, cloning, media and serum exposure (critical
  for TSE/BSE risk assessment), and every manipulation.

## Why regulators care so much

The cell bank is the single most upstream control in biologics. Viral safety
starts here (pillar 1 of the ICH Q5A strategy is source control: a bank known
to be free of detectable adventitious agents). Product consistency starts here
(a stable construct within a defined cell-age window). And comparability after
a cell-line change is one of the hardest exercises in biologics CMC, which is
why the bank dossier is built once and built properly.

## When is a new bank needed?

Three events trigger bank work, and each has a different regulatory weight:

- **Routine WCB replenishment** from the MCB: a defined, repeatable operation
  with the reduced characterization panel; planned years ahead because a
  well-sized WCB should last a long time.
- **New MCB** (new clone, new construct, re-cloning): effectively a new
  starting material; requires the full ICH Q5D panel plus comparability
  assessment of the product it produces.
- **Bank qualification for a new site or new freezer program**: storage and
  transport qualification, with viability confirmation after transfer.

Sites forecast vial consumption decades out; discovering the MCB is nearly
exhausted during a supply crunch is a strategic failure, not an inventory
surprise.

## Common findings and mistakes

- Bank history incomplete: unclear origin, undocumented serum or raw-material
  exposure, missing cloning records.
- WCB prepared from a WCB rather than the MCB, quietly creating a third,
  unqualified tier.
- Mycoplasma or adventitious-agent testing performed with non-compendial
  methods and no validation.
- EOPC/CAL testing missing, so the LIVCA is asserted rather than demonstrated.
- Vial inventory not reconciled; unexplained discrepancies between records and
  physical counts.
- Single-site storage with no contingency for freezer failure.
- Genetic stability assessed only by product titer, not by construct sequence
  and copy number.

## Documentation checklist

- cell-line history and origin documentation;
- MCB and WCB preparation records (media, passage numbers, cryopreservation);
- full MCB characterization reports (identity, purity, viral, genetic);
- WCB reduced-panel reports;
- EOPC/CAL stability data supporting the LIVCA;
- raw-material and serum-exposure risk assessment;
- inventory, storage, and monitoring records for both storage sites;
- change-control linkage for any new bank preparation.
