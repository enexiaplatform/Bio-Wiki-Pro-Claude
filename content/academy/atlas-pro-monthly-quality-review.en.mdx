# Atlas Pro Monthly Quality Review

Atlas Pro is most useful when it changes a recurring professional decision, not
when it becomes another library to browse. The Monthly Quality Review is a small
operating loop for one quality priority:

> frame the question, inspect the evidence, apply one bounded workflow, capture a
> reusable work note, and decide what carries forward.

It supports individual QC, QA, manufacturing-quality, analytical, process and
technical operations professionals. It does not perform project-specific expert
review, replace a qualified role or authorize a batch, method, process, supplier,
product or regulatory position.

## Decision question

Start with one sentence:

> What quality decision or signal needs a better evidence basis this month, and who
> owns the next authorized action?

Good starting questions are narrow enough to finish but consequential enough to
reuse. Examples include:

- Which evidence would distinguish a recurring OOT signal from a change in the
  process or measurement system?
- What is still missing before a method-transfer protocol can be challenged?
- Which supplier-change assumptions affect a biologic raw material or single-use
  system, and what evidence should be requested next?
- Which API process-development claim is observed, inferred, or still a purge
  hypothesis?

Avoid starting with "learn everything about" a topic. The workflow is successful
when it produces a better next decision, not when it produces the longest note.

## The four-part cycle

### 1. Frame

Write the decision, owner, product or process boundary, intended use, current
evidence and do-not-use boundary. Record the version of the procedure, source or
internal record being reviewed. If the question requires an authorized QA,
regulatory, safety or disposition decision, mark that boundary before applying a
Pro workflow.

### 2. Inspect evidence

Use the relevant core lesson to separate facts, interpretations, assumptions and
open evidence. Check source applicability and version. Ask which observation would
disprove the leading explanation. Preserve uncertainty instead of converting a
correlation into a cause or a passing result into a conclusion about the whole
process.

### 3. Apply one workflow

Choose one bounded application: an audit-readiness review, quality-signal
investigation, method-capacity question, Pharma API impurity-control question or
Biopharma control-strategy question. Keep product-specific records sanitized and
do not paste confidential data into a public example. The output should be a short
working note with the question, evidence used, decision options, assumptions,
limitations, reviewer prompt and next action.

### 4. Capture carryover

End with a dated carryover statement:

- what was learned or corrected;
- what remains unverified;
- which asset can be reused;
- who owns the next authorized action;
- when the evidence will be reviewed again; and
- what signal would change the current path.

This is the difference between a lesson completion and a professional workflow.

## Worked scenario: a cross-domain signal

A quality professional supports an API process change and a biologics upstream
trend review. Both teams report "the method is fine" while the observed signals
remain inconsistent. A Monthly Quality Review does not choose a universal method
or assign causality. It asks the professional to:

1. define which decision each signal is meant to support;
2. separate sample, method, process, material and data-lineage observations;
3. identify the evidence that is directly comparable and the evidence that is only
   a platform analogy;
4. select one review question for the responsible technical or quality owner; and
5. retain a carryover note that states what changed and what remains open.

The reusable value is the reasoning trail and the next evidence request. It is not
the creation of a generic conclusion for either product.

## Completion standard

A Monthly Quality Review is complete only when it has:

- one decision question and named boundary;
- source and record versions;
- an evidence note with facts separated from hypotheses;
- one applied workflow or decision aid;
- one reviewer challenge or explicit reason it is pending;
- a reusable sanitized artifact reference; and
- a carryover action, owner, date and change signal.

If any of these are missing, keep the item under review rather than presenting it
as a solved quality conclusion.
