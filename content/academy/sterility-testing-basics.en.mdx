# Sterility Testing - Foundations (USP &lt;71&gt; & Ph. Eur. 2.6.1)

Compendial sterility testing is the ultimate growth-based analytical release gate for sterile pharmaceutical products, parenterals, biologics, and ophthalmic preparations. 

Harmonized across **USP &lt;71&gt;**, **Ph. Eur. 2.6.1**, and **JP 4.06**, the sterility test evaluates whether a representative sample of units from a manufactured batch exhibits microbial growth when exposed to nutrient growth media under strict incubation conditions.

However, a fundamental regulatory truth must be understood by QC analysts and QA managers:

> **The sterility test does not guarantee batch sterility. It only confirms that no recoverable microorganisms grew in the specific units tested under the specific test conditions.**

True **Sterility Assurance Level ($\text{SAL} \le 10^{-6}$)** is created by the entire manufacturing continuum — terminal sterilization cycles, validated aseptic process simulations (media fills), environmental monitoring, barrier technology (Isolators/RABS), and a comprehensive **Contamination Control Strategy (CCS)**.

---

## 1. The Two Compendial Test Methodologies

Compendial harmonized standards define two distinct testing techniques.

| Technique | Scope & Application | Method Principles & Technical Controls |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Membrane Filtration** | **Mandatory** for all filterable liquids, aqueous solutions, IV fluids, soluble powders, and ophthalmics. | Uses $0.45 \:\mu\text{m}$ pore size hydrophobic-edge membrane filter canisters (e.g., Steritest closed systems). The sample is filtered under vacuum or pressure, washed with sterile fluid (Fluid A or D with neutralizers) to strip antimicrobial residues, then filled with FTM and TSB media. |
| **Direct Inoculation** | Reserved **only** for non-filterable products (e.g., ointments, surgical dressings, insoluble suspensions, medical devices). | Small aliquots of sample are inoculated directly into FTM and TSB media tubes. The volume of product must not exceed $10\%$ of the media volume ($1:10 \text{ v/v}$ ratio) to prevent antimicrobial inhibition. |

### Technical Rule:
Membrane filtration is vastly superior to direct inoculation because filtering allows the sample to be thoroughly rinsed with validated rinse fluids (Fluid A, D, or K), removing active preservatives or antibiotics before incubation.

## 2. Compendial Media & Incubation Parameters

The harmonized compendial test requires two complementary growth media to support the recovery of aerobic bacteria, anaerobic bacteria, yeast, and moulds.

```text
                                  ┌── Fluid Thioglycollate Medium (FTM)
                                  │   • Target: Anaerobic & Aerobic Bacteria
                                  │   • Temp: 30°C - 35°C
                                  │   • Key Reagent: Sodium Thioglycollate + L-Cysteine
Sterility Test Sample ────────────┤
                                  │
                                  └── Soybean-Casein Digest Medium (TSB / SCDM)
                                      • Target: Fungi (Yeast/Mould) & Aerobic Bacteria
                                      • Temp: 20°C - 25°C
                                      • Key Reagent: Enzymatic Digest of Casein/Soybean
```

### Media Specifications & Controls

- **Fluid Thioglycollate Medium (FTM):** Contains sodium thioglycollate and L-cysteine as reducing agents to maintain an anaerobic environment at the lower portion of the tube. Resazurin serves as an oxidation-reduction indicator.
  - *Critical Control:* If the upper oxidized (pink) layer exceeds **one-third (30%)** of the medium depth, the tube must be de-aerated by heating or discarded.
- **Soybean-Casein Digest Medium (TSB / SCDM):** Supports fungi and obligate aerobes.
- **Incubation Duration:** Minimum **14 continuous days** for both FTM and TSB.

## 3. Sampling Plans & Minimum Quantities (USP &lt;71&gt; Tables 2 & 3)

The number of containers tested per batch and the volume per container are strictly specified by compendial tables.

| Product Category | Batch Size (Containers) | Minimum Number of Containers to Test | Minimum Quantity per Container |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Parenterals (Small Volume, &lt; 100 mL)** | &lt; 100 containers | $10\%$ or 4 containers (whichever is greater) | $100\%$ of contents (if &lt; 1 mL) or $1\text{ mL}$ |
| **Parenterals (Small Volume, &lt; 100 mL)** | $100\text{--}500$ containers | 10 containers | $2\text{ mL}$ or full content |
| **Parenterals (Small Volume, &lt; 100 mL)** | &gt; 500 containers | $2\%$ or 20 containers (whichever is lower) | Full contents (if $1\text{--}4\text{ mL}$) or $5\text{ mL}$ |
| **Large Volume Parenterals ($\ge 100\text{ mL}$)** | Any batch size | 20 containers | $2\%$ of volume or $50\text{ mL}$ |
| **Ophthalmic & Non-Parenterals** | &gt; 500 containers | 20 containers | Full contents |

## 4. Method Suitability (Bacteriostasis & Fungistasis / B&F Testing)

Before a sterility test method can be used for batch release, the laboratory must perform **Method Suitability Testing** to prove the product matrix does not suppress microbial growth.

### Test Challenge Organisms ($\le 100 \text{ CFU}$ Inoculum)

1. **Fluid Thioglycollate Medium ($30\text{--}35^\circ\text{C}$):**
   - *Clostridium sporogenes* (ATCC 11437) — Obligate Anaerobe.
   - *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* (ATCC 9027) or *Kocuria rhizophila* — Obligate Aerobe.
   - *Staphylococcus aureus* (ATCC 6538) — Gram-positive Aerobe/Facultative Anaerobe.
2. **Soybean-Casein Digest Medium ($20\text{--}25^\circ\text{C}$):**
   - *Bacillus subtilis* (ATCC 6633) — Spore-forming Aerobe.
   - *Candida albicans* (ATCC 10231) — Yeast.
   - *Aspergillus brasiliensis* (ATCC 16404) — Filamentous Mould.

### Acceptance Criteria
Growth in the presence of the product must be **visually comparable** to the positive control (media + inoculum without product) within **5 days** of incubation. If growth is inhibited, the method must be modified by increasing rinse volume, adding neutralizers (lecithin, polysorbate 80, $\beta$-lactamase), or increasing diluent ratio.

## 5. Investigating Positive Sterility Test Results & Strict Invalidation Rules

A positive sterility test (turbidity observed in FTM or TSB) is an immediate critical quality event. Under **FDA 21 CFR 211.192** and **EU GMP Annex 1**, re-testing a positive sterility result without conclusive proof of laboratory error is illegal.

```text
                              Positive Sterility Result (Turbidity Detected)
                                                   │
                                                   ▼
                                 Initiate Immediate QA/QC Investigation
                                                   │
                   ┌───────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────┐
                   ▼                                                               ▼
       No Definitive Lab Error Found                                 Definitive Lab Error Proven
                   │                                                               │
                   ▼                                                               ▼
        BATCH REJECTION MANDATED                                      Test Officially Invalidated
  (Root Cause: Manufacturing/Fill Defect)                        (Repeat Test with Identical Sample Size)
```

### The 4 Compendial Invalidation Criteria (USP &lt;71&gt; / EU Annex 1)

A sterility test result can be declared invalid **only** if one or more of the following 4 conditions are conclusively documented:
1. Microscopic or identity data shows the contamination was caused by a material or equipment failure used during the sterility test procedure.
2. Negative controls show unambiguous microbial growth.
3. Microorganism recovered is identified to species level and indisputably traced to environmental or analyst contamination occurring during the test.
4. Fault in the sterilization process of media/canisters is conclusively established.

## 6. Sterility Testing & Quality Lab Capacity Integration

Performing sterility tests in an **Isolator** vs. a **Class A Laminar Flow Hood inside Grade B cleanroom** significantly alters laboratory capital and operational expenditures:
- **Isolator Technology:** Minimizes false-positive background rates (&lt; 0.01%), eliminates Grade B cleanroom gowning costs, but requires automated VHP (Vaporized Hydrogen Peroxide) decontamination cycles ($2\text{--}4\text{ hours}$ per load).
- **Incubation Capacity:** A site manufacturing 50 batches/month requiring 20 samples $\times$ 2 media containers generates 2,000 canisters occupying incubator shelf space continuously for 14 days.
