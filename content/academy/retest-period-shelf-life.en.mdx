# Retest Period & Shelf-Life Assignment (ICH Q1E)

Stability data exist to answer one commercial and regulatory question: how long
can this material be used? **ICH Q1A(R2)** defines the studies; **ICH
Q1E** explains how to evaluate the data and justify a **retest period** for a
drug substance or a **shelf life** for a drug product. This lesson is the
decision layer on top of the study design covered in
[Stability Studies — ICH Q1A Program Design](/library/stability-studies).

## Retest Period vs Shelf Life

| | Retest period | Shelf life (expiry) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Applies to | Drug substances (and certain intermediates) | Drug products |
| Meaning at the date | Material may still be used **after re-testing** against specification and conforming | Product **must not be used** beyond the date |
| Label statement | "Retest date: ..." | "Expiry / use before: ..." |
| Regulatory basis | ICH Q1A/Q1E | ICH Q1A/Q1E |

The distinction is operationally important. An expired product is gone — full
stop. A substance past its retest date is not automatically waste: it can be
returned to inventory after a documented passing retest, but it must not enter
production before that retest passes.

## How Q1E Evaluation Works

The evaluation asks, per attribute: does it change over time, and if so, when
does the trend cross the specification limit?

1. Confirm all batches are within specification at every long-term time point
   at the recommended storage condition.
2. Identify which attributes change with time (assay, degradants, water
   content, dissolution) and which do not.
3. For attributes that change, fit the trend — regression for quantitative
   attributes — and find where the **95% one-sided confidence limit**
   intersects the acceptance criterion.
4. Test whether batches can be pooled (Q1E provides significance tests for
   common slope/intercept); if one batch degrades faster, the proposed period
   must rest on the worst case, not the pool.
5. Handle qualitative and microbiological attributes by direct evidence, not
   regression.

The proposed period is the conclusion of this analysis — never a target the
data are fitted around.

## The Extrapolation Rules

ICH Q1E's decision tree (its Appendix A) governs how far beyond the long-term
data you may propose. For a room-temperature product, in simplified form:

- **No significant change at accelerated (6 months, 40 deg C / 75% RH)**, and
  long-term data show little or no change with low variability: extrapolation
  up to **twice the long-term coverage, but not more than 12 months beyond**
  the period covered, may be proposed.
- **Significant change at accelerated but no significant change at
  intermediate** (30 deg C / 65% RH): the same logic applies, anchored on the
  intermediate data.
- **Significant change at intermediate**, or data that change appreciably with
  high variability: little or no extrapolation — the proposed period should not
  extend beyond the long-term data, and may need to be shorter.

Two principles sit underneath the arithmetic: extrapolation shrinks as
variability grows, and statistical support (analyzed regression with confidence
limits) is what licenses the more generous extensions.

## Labeling and Storage Statements

The assigned period ties directly to the label. Q1A standardizes storage
statements linked to the supporting conditions — e.g. long-term data at 25 deg
C / 60% RH support "Do not store above 25 deg C" style statements, while
refrigerated products carry their own conditions and excursion allowances. The
storage statement, the retest/expiry period, and the stability data must form a
consistent triangle: a label claiming room-temperature storage on data
generated only at refrigerated conditions is an unsupported claim.

Also remember the lifecycle: the initial assignment is a **proposed** period
supported by primary batches. It is confirmed and potentially extended through
the ongoing stability program as commercial-batch data accumulate — and it can
also be shortened when ongoing data no longer support it.

## Data Pattern to Decision

| Data pattern | Typical Q1E outcome |
| --- | --- |
| Little change long-term, no significant change at accelerated, low variability | Extrapolation up to 2x data coverage, max 12 months beyond |
| Clear trend, low variability, regression fits well | Extrapolation supported by statistical analysis, applied conservatively |
| High variability or poor model fit | Period limited to data coverage, possibly shorter |
| Significant change at accelerated, none at intermediate | Extrapolation anchored on the intermediate data |
| Significant change at intermediate | No extrapolation beyond the long-term data |
| One batch clearly degrades faster | Base the period on that batch, not the pooled estimate |

## Common Mistakes and Inspection Findings

- Calling a drug-product expiry a "retest date" (or vice versa) on labels and
  CoAs — the terms are not interchangeable.
- Extrapolating 24 months from 6 months of data with visible degradant growth.
- Pooling batches without testing poolability, masking one fast-degrading lot.
- Assigning shelf life from accelerated data while long-term data are still
  sparse.
- Using a substance past its retest date in production before the retest
  result exists.
- Never revisiting the assigned period when ongoing stability shows drift.
- Storage statement on the label not matching the conditions actually studied.
