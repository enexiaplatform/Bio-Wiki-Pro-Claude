# Biologics QC - What Makes It Different

Quality control for biologics - monoclonal antibodies, recombinant proteins,
vaccines, cell and gene therapies - is a different discipline from
small-molecule QC. The molecules are large, fragile, and produced by living
cells, so the mental model of "define the molecule, test against a spec"
bends in important ways. The governing framework is the ICH Q5 series
(Q5A viral safety, Q5B expression construct, Q5C stability, Q5D cell
substrates, Q5E comparability) together with ICH Q6B, which defines how
specifications for biotechnological products are set.

## Four structural differences

| Dimension | Small molecule | Biologic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Structure | One defined chemical entity, fully characterizable | Large protein or complex; a population of closely related variants |
| Heterogeneity | Impurities are foreign matter to minimize | Variants (glycoforms, charge variants, oxidized species, aggregates) are inherent; QC controls the distribution |
| Manufacturing | Chemical synthesis, reproducible by recipe | Living cells; sensitive to cell line, media, and process conditions |
| Identity and assay | Identity by one or two chemical tests; assay by chemical measurement (HPLC potency ~ 99-101%) | Identity by orthogonal method panel; potency by bioassay with much wider inherent variability |

## Heterogeneity is normal, not a defect

A monoclonal antibody batch contains millions of slightly different molecules:
deamidated and oxidized species, C-terminal lysine variants, different
glycoforms, low levels of aggregates and fragments. QC does not prove a single
structure; it proves the **distribution of species** stays within ranges shown
to be safe and effective. That is why biologics specifications are built as
profiles (acidic/main/basic species percent, aggregate percent, glycan
distribution) rather than single-number purities.

## The process defines the product

Because cells build and modify the molecule, the manufacturing process shapes
the critical quality attributes. Change the cell line, the feed strategy, the
harvest time, or a chromatography resin, and the glycosylation, charge, or
aggregate profile can shift. Consequences:

- raw-material and media control is QC-relevant, not just procurement;
- process changes require formal **comparability** assessment (ICH Q5E) with
  analytical evidence, sometimes supported by nonclinical data;
- the control strategy spans process parameters and in-process controls, not
  just release testing.

## The four QC assay classes

Biologics QC testing groups into four classes, each with its own method logic:

| Class | Question | Typical methods |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity | Is it the right molecule? | Peptide mapping (LC-MS), icIEF/CEX charge profile, binding assay, intact mass - orthogonal panel per ICH Q6B |
| Potency | Does it do what it should? | Cell-based bioassay (reporter gene, proliferation, ADCC) or justified binding surrogate, reported as relative potency vs a reference standard |
| Purity and impurities | What else is present? | CE-SDS and SEC for size variants; icIEF/CEX for charge; host-cell protein ELISA, residual DNA, residual Protein A for process impurities |
| Safety | Is it safe to administer? | Sterility/bioburden, bacterial endotoxins, mycoplasma, adventitious and endogenous virus testing, visible and subvisible particles |

No single class is optional, and weakness in one cannot be compensated by
strength in another. A potent batch with an unexplained purity shift is not
releasable; a pure batch with drifting potency is not either.

## Why "assay" becomes "bioassay"

A small molecule's potency is its chemical assay: weigh it, inject it, compare
against a reference standard. A biologic can have perfect sequence and mass and
still be inactive if it is misfolded or its binding site is modified. Function
must therefore be measured biologically - typically a cell-based assay -
and reported as relative potency against a reference standard. This one change
drives much of the biologics QC skill set: reference-standard lifecycle
management, parallelism assessment, and tolerance for variability that would
alarm a small-molecule lab.

## QC thinks in trends, not single results

Because the product is a distribution and the methods are noisy, biologics QC
leans harder on trending than small-molecule QC:

- charge-variant and aggregate profiles trended across batches;
- reference-standard behavior in the potency assay trended to separate assay
  drift from product change;
- process and release data reviewed together at annual product review;
- comparability packages reusing the characterization panel after changes;
- specification limits revisited as batch history accumulates, never loosened
  silently to accommodate drift.

## Specifications under ICH Q6B

ICH Q6B structures the biologics specification around the same four classes
and adds two practical rules that shape daily QC work. First, release testing
is only part of the control strategy: some attributes are controlled by
in-process testing or by process consistency rather than by a release limit.
Second, acceptance criteria for a biologic are set from **data** - clinical
batch history, characterization, and process capability - not from
compendial monographs, because most biologics have no monograph. That is why
specification justification is itself a QC document, and why changing a limit
late in development is a regulatory event, not an editorial one.

Stability, container closure, and safety testing complete the picture, and ICH
Q5C sets expectations for stability programs that respect the product's
sensitivity to temperature, agitation, and freeze-thaw.

## Common mistakes when moving from small-molecule QC

- Expecting one identity test to suffice; ICH Q6B expects an orthogonal panel.
- Treating inherent variants (charge species, glycoforms) as impurities to be
  zeroed rather than attributes to be controlled within a range.
- Applying chemical-assay precision expectations (RSD under 2%) to cell-based
  potency, where much wider variability is inherent.
- Releasing on release data alone without reference-standard and profile
  trending.
- Underestimating raw-material risk: media components, serum replacements, and
  single-use materials can all move CQAs.
- Assuming a process tweak is "minor" without a comparability assessment.
