# Biopharma Manufacturing Comparability After Change

Manufacturing changes are normal across a biologic's lifecycle. Scale,
equipment, site, raw materials, cell-culture strategy, purification,
formulation, container, or analytical procedures may change during development
or after approval. The quality question is not whether change occurred. It is
whether the available evidence supports the conclusion that the post-change
product remains comparable for its intended use.

This lesson structures that question. It does not supply a universal study
design, statistical acceptance criterion, regulatory reporting category, or
comparability conclusion.

## Decision question

> What evidence is needed to determine whether a defined manufacturing change
> has an adverse impact on the quality, safety, or efficacy of this product, and
> what differences would require further analytical, nonclinical, clinical, or
> regulatory evaluation?

## Applicability and do-not-use boundary

ICH Q5E addresses specified biotechnological and biological proteins and
polypeptides when one manufacturer can directly compare pre-change and
post-change product after a manufacturing-process change. It can apply during
development and after marketing authorization within its stated scope.

Do not use this workflow as:

- a biosimilarity assessment between products from different manufacturers;
- a substitute for the approved change-control process or regional reporting
  classification;
- proof that passing release specifications alone establishes comparability;
- a universal template for vaccines, cell and gene therapies, viral vectors,
  plasma-derived products, or other modalities outside the cited scope;
- permission to implement a change before required quality and regulatory
  decisions are complete.

## Controlled source map

| Source | Decision use | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ICH Q5E | Core principles for evaluating comparability after a manufacturing-process change | Does not prescribe one analytical, nonclinical, or clinical strategy |
| ICH Q6B | Product characterization and specification principles for applicable biological products | Specification conformance is one part of the control strategy |
| ICH Q5C | Stability considerations for applicable biotechnology and biological products | Real-time and product-specific degradation evidence remain necessary |
| ICH Q5D | Cell-substrate considerations when the change affects cell banking or production substrate | Apply only within its defined cell-substrate scope |
| ICH Q5A(R2) | Viral-safety impact when relevant materials, process steps, or clearance strategy change | Requires an integrated and product-appropriate viral-safety assessment |
| ICH Q9(R1) and Q10 | Risk-based planning, knowledge management, change governance, and lifecycle review | Do not create product-specific criteria or regional filing decisions |

## Comparability is not analytical identity

The pre-change and post-change product do not need to be analytically identical.
The evidence must show that they are highly similar in the context of the
change and that the available knowledge is sufficiently predictive to address
the potential impact of observed differences.

That distinction prevents two common errors:

1. **Too weak:** “All release tests passed, so the products are comparable.”
2. **Too rigid:** “Any analytical difference means the products are not
   comparable.”

Both skip the scientific evaluation of the difference, its relationship to the
process, and its potential relevance to product performance.

## Build the change-to-evidence map

### 1. Define the change precisely

Record what changes, what does not, why the change is proposed, when it occurs,
and which materials, steps, sites, systems, methods, and markets are affected.
“Scale-up” or “new site” is too broad. The assessment needs the specific process
and control differences that could alter product quality.

### 2. Write prospective risk hypotheses

Before reviewing results, state how the change could affect the product. Map
each credible pathway to an observable attribute or process outcome.

| Change dimension | Example question, not a universal risk claim |
| --- | --- |
| Cell substrate or cell age | Could the change alter genetic stability, process performance, heterogeneity, or safety controls? |
| Raw material or media/feed | Could variability affect cell performance, impurities, glycosylation, charge, or activity? |
| Upstream scale or equipment | Could mixing, mass transfer, gradients, shear, time, or control performance alter the product profile? |
| Purification step or resin | Could clearance, recovery, product variants, hold time, leachables, or viral-safety assumptions change? |
| Formulation or fill-finish | Could degradation, aggregation, particles, potency, container interaction, or stability change? |
| Analytical method | Could a method change create bias, change sensitivity, or interrupt historical comparability? |

The product-specific assessment decides which pathways are credible and
material. The table only helps prevent omissions.

### 3. Freeze representative materials and provenance

Comparability depends on knowing what is being compared. The plan should define:

- representative pre-change and post-change material;
- batch, stage, scale, site, process version, and storage history;
- retain availability and sample suitability;
- reference standards, critical reagents, and method versions;
- deviations or atypical events that affect interpretation.

A comparison becomes weak when “before” and “after” materials differ in several
uncontrolled ways or when provenance cannot be reconstructed.

### 4. Select evidence by risk and sensitivity

Use methods capable of observing the attributes plausibly affected by the
change. The package may include routine controls, extended characterization,
biological activity, process-performance and clearance evidence, forced or
accelerated studies, and real-time stability commitments.

The number of methods is not the quality measure. The key questions are:

- Does each method address a change hypothesis?
- Is it capable of detecting a meaningful difference?
- Are sample handling, reference, system suitability, variability, and data
  analysis controlled?
- Are orthogonal methods needed to reduce a material blind spot?

### 5. Define how differences will be evaluated

The protocol should state the comparison logic prospectively. Depending on the
attribute and development knowledge, the approach may use justified ranges,
descriptive comparison, statistical models, equivalence concepts, trend and
profile evaluation, or a structured scientific assessment.

Do not invent a universal number of batches, confidence interval, fold-change,
or similarity threshold. Those choices depend on the attribute, method,
variability, product knowledge, stage, risk, and regulatory context.

### 6. Investigate differences, including unexpected ones

For every confirmed difference, ask:

1. Could it arise from analytical method or sample handling?
2. Is it consistent with the proposed change mechanism?
3. Is it within prior product and process knowledge?
4. Does it change stability or degradation behavior?
5. Could it affect biological activity, safety, immunogenicity, or clinical
   performance?
6. What additional evidence or authority is needed to resolve the question?

The absence of an obvious clinical consequence is not proof of no impact.
Equally, detection of a difference does not automatically prove harm.

### 7. Integrate the conclusion and residual uncertainty

The conclusion should state:

- which change hypotheses were tested;
- which evidence supports comparability;
- which differences were observed and how they were evaluated;
- what uncertainty remains;
- whether additional analytical, nonclinical, clinical, stability, process, or
  regulatory work is required;
- who is accountable for implementation and lifecycle follow-up.

## Decision table

| Evidence state | Defensible next action | What not to claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No material differences detected with a sensitive, justified package | Complete qualified integrated review and required change/regulatory actions | “The products are identical” |
| Confirmed difference with strong product knowledge showing no adverse impact | Document the evidence chain, uncertainty, and accountable comparability conclusion | “Any in-range difference is irrelevant” |
| Difference detected but relevance is uncertain | Add targeted analytical, stability, process, nonclinical, clinical, or authority interaction as justified | “Release pass proves comparability” |
| Method or sample comparability is unresolved | Resolve measurement and material provenance before interpreting product difference | “The observed shift is product-related” |
| Material evidence conflicts or a potentially adverse impact remains | Escalate; do not close or implement on an unsupported conclusion | “Risk assessment alone closes the issue” |

## Worked example: fictional purification-resin change

A fictional manufacturer proposes a new resin for one polishing step in a
cell-culture-derived protein process. Routine release results remain within the
current specification. Extended characterization shows a small shift in a
charge-variant profile, and process data show a different impurity-clearance
pattern.

The comparability team records:

| Question | Evidence held | Open evidence | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Is the charge shift real? | Orthogonal profile results from representative material | Method variability, sample handling, and repeat confirmation | Analytical development |
| Is it linked to the resin change? | Step-specific process and fraction data | Mechanistic and multivariate process review | Downstream development/MSAT |
| Does impurity control remain suitable? | Routine HCP and process trend | Assay coverage and step-clearance assessment | Product quality and analytical development |
| Could stability behavior change? | Limited accelerated comparison | Approved real-time and stress-comparison plan | Formulation/stability |
| Is additional evidence or reporting required? | Draft integrated risk assessment | Qualified quality and regional regulatory CMC decision | Change owner and regulatory CMC |

The example intentionally stops before a comparability conclusion. Its purpose
is to show why passing release tests, an analytical shift, and a process shift
must be integrated rather than interpreted separately.

## Working asset

The Atlas Pro Biopharma workflow brief should capture:

- change ID, scope, rationale, stage, sites, and markets;
- pre-change and post-change process versions and material provenance;
- risk hypotheses and affected attributes;
- method, reference, sample, and statistical decision basis;
- observed similarities, differences, contradictions, and limitations;
- additional evidence requests and accountable reviewers;
- quality/regulatory decision and lifecycle commitments.

This is a planning scaffold. A real study requires an approved protocol,
controlled data, product-specific criteria, and authorized review.

## Limitations

- This lesson does not determine whether a change is reportable or which filing
  category applies.
- It does not define the required number of lots, methods, time points, or
  acceptance criteria.
- It does not address biosimilarity between different manufacturers' products.
- It does not replace product-specific nonclinical, clinical, immunogenicity,
  viral-safety, stability, validation, quality, or regulatory assessment.
- Advanced modalities require dedicated sources and reviewer competence.
