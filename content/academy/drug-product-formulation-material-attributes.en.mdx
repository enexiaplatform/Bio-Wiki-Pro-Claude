# Drug product formulation and material attributes

## Decision question

For this dosage form, API, excipient portfolio and intended presentation, which
material attributes and formulation hypotheses require evidence before a
qualified team can choose the next development or lifecycle action?

## Applicability and do-not-use boundary

This lesson gives a cross-dosage-form evidence structure and a synthetic oral
solid dose (OSD) example. It does not provide a formula, excipient level,
design space, bioequivalence conclusion, dissolution criterion, release limit,
stability claim or regulatory precedent. Confirm dosage-form applicability and
product-specific decisions with formulation, biopharmaceutics, analytical,
quality and regulatory CMC reviewers.

## Controlled source map

| Source | Decision use | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ICH Q8(R2) | Connect material attributes, process understanding and product performance | It does not supply a product formula or design space |
| ICH Q9(R1) | Structure risk questions and review priorities | Risk ranking does not replace experiments or approval |
| ICH Q10 | Keep formulation knowledge and changes in the quality lifecycle | It does not authorize a change |

## Separate seven formulation evidence objects

Do not collapse a formulation decision into one risk score or one development
table. Preserve seven linked objects:

1. **Product intent** — dosage form, route, strength/presentation assumptions,
   intended performance, patient-use context and the exact decision owner.
2. **API state** — identity, form, particle and surface observations, water or
   solvent history, stability knowledge, lot/version and analytical limits.
3. **Excipient function** — intended role, grade/version, supplier/process
   history, variability evidence and the mechanism by which it could affect the
   product.
4. **Formulation hypothesis** — a testable product-specific relationship, not a
   copied formula or a declaration of criticality.
5. **Process interaction** — how blending, granulation, drying, milling,
   compression, coating, filling or another dosage-form operation could modify
   the material relationship.
6. **Performance evidence** — capable methods, representative samples,
   dissolution or other dosage-form performance observations, stability and
   uncertainty.
7. **Lifecycle decision** — what the evidence supports now, what remains open,
   who reviews it and which change reopens the question.

This separation matters because the same material label can hide different
physical histories, and the same analytical result can have different meaning
for different dosage forms, processes or presentations.

## Run the formulation evidence workflow

### 1. Freeze the decision baseline

Record the product/dosage-form concept, API and material versions, process
state, market or development stage, question, accountable owner and due date.
If the team is simultaneously changing API state, excipient source and process
equipment, record each change rather than calling the comparison a simple
formulation study.

**Gate:** the baseline is usable only when every sample and observation can be
linked to a defined material and process state.

### 2. Build the material-function-mechanism map

For every material, state its intended function and the plausible mechanism by
which variability could influence manufacturability, dose delivery, stability
or another product attribute. Mark whether the relationship is prior
knowledge, product evidence, a hypothesis or currently unknown. Supplier
documentation is an input; it is not product-specific functional evidence.

**Gate:** no attribute is labelled critical solely because it appears on a
certificate, risk template or another product's development report.

### 3. Challenge measurement and confounding

Check sampling, sample preparation, method purpose, method version, reference
status, reportable result and missing data. Then list changes that moved
together. A dissolution observation coincident with both an API particle shift
and an excipient-source change cannot distinguish those mechanisms without
additional evidence.

**Gate:** an association remains unresolved when measurement capability or a
material/process confounder could reasonably explain it.

### 4. Frame bounded evidence generation

Write the decision question before selecting factors or responses. Define the
material contrasts, process context, representative samples, analytical
responses, data lineage, handling of inconclusive results and accountable
review. Qualified site teams own compositions, levels, feasible ranges,
protocols, safety and acceptance logic.

**Gate:** the study frame may organize evidence but cannot become a formula,
design space or manufacturing instruction.

### 5. Record the conclusion and next-stage handoff

Separate observations, interpretations, proposed actions and formal
authorization. Carry material risks into the relevant unit operation,
analytical/stability program, packaging question, transfer activity or change
control. Include triggers such as supplier/process changes, new API lots,
method changes or contradictory stability evidence.

**Gate:** completion means ready for accountable review, not formulation
selection, bioequivalence, validation or regulatory acceptance.

## Evidence and study architecture

| Study question | Evidence that improves the decision | Evidence that remains insufficient |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Could a material-version difference alter product performance? | Attributable lots, mechanism-based contrasts, representative processing, capable methods and product-relevant responses | Grade name, certificate comparison or one unreplicated endpoint |
| Is an observed response caused by formulation rather than process or measurement? | Deliberate contrasts or reconciled natural experiments with process, sampling and method versions preserved | A correlation in which several changes moved together |
| Can prior knowledge be applied? | Similarity rationale covering material function, product, dosage form, process, response and uncertainty | “Platform” or literature evidence without applicability review |
| Is the evidence ready for lifecycle use? | Versioned conclusion, limitations, reviewers, open actions and change triggers | A development slide without data lineage or authorization boundary |

Do not force a conclusive result. “Evidence required,” “confounded,” “method
capability unresolved” and “dosage-form applicability not established” are
valid outputs.

## Common failure modes

- Treating material grade or supplier specification as proof of equivalent
  product function.
- Assigning criticality before distinguishing hypothesis from demonstrated
  product impact.
- Copying excipient levels, factor ranges or responses from another product.
- Ignoring API state, process history, sampling or analytical capability.
- Using a risk score as a substitute for experimental evidence.
- Reporting a synthetic OSD example as a validated formulation or regulatory
  precedent.

## Evidence chain

1. Freeze dosage form, API form, intended use, presentation and decision owner.
2. Map material identity, grade/version, supplier evidence and relevant
   functional attributes to each formulation hypothesis.
3. Record the mechanism being tested, study or observation, analytical
   capability, uncertainty, limitation and next qualified review.
4. Preserve links to manufacturing, dissolution/performance, stability,
   packaging and change-control evidence rather than treating formulation as a
   standalone recipe.

## Discovery questions

- What product intent, dosage-form assumptions and quality attributes are fixed for this decision?
- Which material-version or formulation hypotheses have a mechanism and an evidence owner?
- Which observations would change the next study, escalation or lifecycle review?
- What remains explicitly out of scope because the evidence cannot support a formula or design-space conclusion?

## Decision table

| Evidence position | Record | Bounded next action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Material attribute is plausible but mechanism is untested | Hypothesis, version, source and uncertainty | Define a qualified study or evidence request |
| Observation conflicts across lots or methods | Contradiction and affected decision | Escalate to formulation, analytical and quality review |
| Evidence supports a planning direction only | Scope and limitation | Carry the question into process, stability or lifecycle review |
