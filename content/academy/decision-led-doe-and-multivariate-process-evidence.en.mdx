# Decision-led DoE and multivariate process evidence

DoE and multivariate analysis create value when they answer a decision that a team
must make. They are not a license to search every variable until a convenient
relationship appears. A defensible analysis starts with the decision, the data-
generating process and the measurement system, then states what the model can and
cannot support.

## Decision question

> What process or quality decision is being tested, which evidence can distinguish
> the competing explanations, and what action would follow if the evidence changes?

## Applicability and do-not-use boundary

Use this lesson for API process development, Biopharma upstream or downstream
understanding, analytical lifecycle, CPV signal review, investigation support and
cross-functional process decisions.

Do not use it as a universal DoE design, model acceptance threshold, CPP list,
process limit, validation conclusion, specification, filing assessment or batch
disposition. A product-specific analysis may require applied statistics, process
science, analytical, data-integrity, manufacturing, quality and regulatory review.

## Controlled source map

| Source | Decision use | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ICH Q8(R2) | Process understanding and development knowledge | It does not select a product-specific design or parameter range |
| ICH Q9(R1) | Risk-based prioritization of questions and evidence | Risk ranking cannot establish causality |
| FDA Process Validation guidance | Lifecycle process evidence and continued verification | It does not prescribe a universal model rule |
| FDA data-integrity guidance | Data lifecycle, attribution and review controls | It does not validate a statistical model |
| ICH Q10 | Knowledge, change and quality-system governance | Governance cannot replace data or measurement evidence |

## Separate six analysis objects

1. **Decision and consequence** - what action depends on the analysis, owner,
   stage, product/process scope and change trigger.
2. **Causal question and response** - response definition, mechanism, expected
   direction, confounders, practical boundary and evidence that would disprove it.
3. **Factors and design** - factor definition, feasible region, randomization or
   blocking, replication, sample size rationale and safety or material limits.
4. **Measurement and data lineage** - method version, sampling, missingness,
   transformations, reference, data integrity, exclusions and timestamp.
5. **Model and uncertainty** - assumptions, interactions, residuals, overfitting,
   extrapolation, sensitivity and applicability boundary.
6. **Action and review** - scenario, evidence strength, reviewer challenge,
   change trigger, next data request and authorized decision.

## Build the decision-led analysis chain

### 1. Start with the decision, not the algorithm

State what will change if the evidence supports or weakens the hypothesis. A model
that cannot change an action is exploratory context, not a controlled decision
basis.

### 2. Protect the measurement system

Confirm method, instrument, sample, reference, analyst, data capture and process
versions before interpreting relationships. A measurement change can look like a
process change.

### 3. Design within the real operating boundary

Record feasible factor ranges and why they are feasible. Do not turn a development
range, one campaign or a software default into a universal process limit.

### 4. Challenge the model

Keep assumptions, residuals, interactions, missingness, sensitivity, extrapolation
and conflicting evidence visible. A high fit statistic does not prove causality,
robustness or product quality.

### 5. Convert evidence into a review action

State the current inference, what remains unknown, which experiment or record would
resolve it, and who can authorize the next step. Keep exploratory and confirmatory
use distinct.

## Decision table

| Evidence condition | Defensible action | Do not claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Model fit is high | Review residuals, data lineage, assumptions, sensitivity and decision relevance | Causality or robustness from fit alone |
| Measurement system changes mid-study | Reconcile versions and assess comparability before pooling | Process effect from an unexamined data shift |
| Factor range is from development | Keep it as scoped knowledge and plan confirmation | Universal operating range or CPP |
| Model extrapolates outside observed data | Flag the boundary and request representative evidence | Prediction is valid in the new region |
| Statistical signal conflicts with product evidence | Preserve both and define a decision-led investigation | Automatically reject or dismiss one signal |
| Reviewer or evidence coverage remains open | Mark `Evidence required` or `Qualified review required` | Validated model or authorized process decision |

## Worked example: fictional upstream and API questions

A fictional Biopharma upstream study links a media attribute and process indicator
to a product-quality response. At the same time, a Pharma API campaign uses a small
DoE to examine work-up factors and an impurity response. Both analyses show an
association, but the sample preparation changed during the studies. The package
separates measurement, process and product evidence, preserves the feasible design
boundary, records model assumptions and assigns a reviewer question. It does not
declare a CPP, causal mechanism, impurity purge or process validation conclusion.

## Working asset

Use a **Decision-led DoE & Multivariate Evidence Brief** to maintain the decision,
causal question, data and measurement lineage, design basis, model assumptions,
uncertainty, reviewer challenge, limitations and next action. It is a statistical
review record, not an automated process optimizer.

## Limitations

This lesson does not establish a DoE, model, causal relationship, CPP, process
range, specification, validation status, filing, batch disposition or regulatory
conclusion. Product-, process-, method-, data- and market-specific analyses
require current controlled data and qualified review.
