# Investigating Microbial Excursions (EM Data)

An environmental-monitoring (EM) excursion is not automatically a disaster -
but how you investigate it tells an auditor whether your contamination
control is real. The goal of every excursion investigation is threefold:
understand the event, assess product impact, and prevent recurrence.

The framework: **EU GMP Annex 1 (2022)** sets the current global benchmark
for sterile manufacturing EM, embedding excursions in the site's
**Contamination Control Strategy (CCS)**. FDA's **Guidance for Industry:
Sterile Drug Products Produced by Aseptic Processing (2004)** defines US
expectations, including recommended limits. **USP &lt;1116&gt;**
(Microbiological Control and Monitoring of Aseptic Processing Environments)
reframes numerical limits as contamination recovery rates - the insight
that EM measures process control, not product sterility. **ISO 14644**
governs the underlying cleanroom classification, and **PDA Technical
Report 13** (Fundamentals of an Environmental Monitoring Program) is the
standard industry reference for program design.

The high-stakes reality: EM data is the only routine microbiological window
into an aseptic process. An excursion shrugged off is a sterility-assurance
debt that surfaces later - in a media fill failure, a sterility test, or a
patient.

## Alert vs action: the response ladder

- **Alert level** - an early-warning signal set below the action level from
  the site's own historical data. Heighten scrutiny; investigate if it
  recurs or trends. Not necessarily a formal investigation on its own -
  but repeated alert excursions at one location absolutely are.
- **Action level** - exceeds the acceptable threshold. Triggers a **formal
  investigation** with root cause, CAPA, and a documented product-impact
  assessment.

Reference limits (verify against current editions and your CCS):

| Grade / class | Active air (cfu/m3) | Settle plate 90mm/4h (cfu) | Contact plate 55mm (cfu) | Glove print (cfu) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Annex 1 (2022) Grade A | No growth | No growth | No growth | No growth |
| Annex 1 (2022) Grade B | 10 | 5 | 5 | 5 |
| Annex 1 (2022) Grade C | 100 | 50 | 25 | - |
| Annex 1 (2022) Grade D | 200 | 100 | 50 | - |
| FDA 2004 ISO 5 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| FDA 2004 ISO 7 | 10 | 5 | 5 | - |
| FDA 2004 ISO 8 | 100 | 50 | 50 | - |

Annex 1 Grade A is uncompromising: any recovered growth in the critical
zone requires investigation. Note also that USP &lt;1116&gt;'s recovery-rate
concept means a single exceeded number matters less than the *rate* at
which a location produces recovery - trend context is part of the
classification, not an afterthought.

## The end-to-end investigation workflow

| Step | Owner | Key actions | Record |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Confirm the result | Micro lab | Check plate integrity, incubation, counting, sampling technique; rule out lab artefact without "testing into compliance" | Data verification note |
| 2. Classify | Micro supervisor + QA | Alert or action; which location, grade, and activity state (at rest / in operation) | Excursion record opened |
| 3. Identify the organism | Micro lab | ID excursion isolates to genus/species (action-level and adverse trends; Annex 1 expects identification of isolates from Grades A and B) | ID report |
| 4. Correlate | Micro + manufacturing | Same location, same organism, same shift/operator, recent events (maintenance, construction, cleaning, interventions) | Correlation analysis |
| 5. Assess product impact | QA + micro + manufacturing | Batches exposed in the window, proximity to critical zone, organism risk, sterility-assurance context | Impact assessment |
| 6. CAPA | QA + owning department | Root cause, corrective and preventive actions, effectiveness check | CAPA record |
| 7. Close and trend | QA | Recovery demonstrated; flora and trend review updated; APR input | Closure + updated trends |

Timing: open promptly - for Grade A/B action excursions, within days, not
at the next monthly review. The batch impact window closes as batches are
dispositioned, so delay converts an investigation into a release crisis.

## Trending EM data between excursions

The investigation quality you can deliver during an excursion is fixed by
the trending you did in peacetime:

- Trend each location's recovery rate, not just exceedances - USP
  &lt;1116&gt;'s recovery-rate framing means a location creeping from 2%
  to 15% recovery deserves attention before any limit is crossed.
- Review flora distribution by location and grade at a defined frequency;
  the flora library is built here.
- Re-derive alert and action levels from accumulated site data at the
  defined interval, and document the derivation.
- Feed the whole picture into the CCS review and the management review -
  EM trends are a sterility-assurance KPI, not a microbiology side
  report.

## Organism-ID-driven root cause

The isolate ID is the single best clue to where the contamination came
from. Annex 1 expects sites to know their flora; the ID turns a number
into a mechanism:

| Organism type | Typical source | Investigation direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gram-positive cocci (*Staphylococcus*, *Micrococcus*) | People - skin, gowning gaps, technique | Gowning qualification, intervention records, glove integrity, behaviour observation |
| Diphtheroids / *Corynebacterium* | Human skin flora | As above; check glove-sleeve interface and gowning coverage |
| Gram-positive rods / spore-formers (*Bacillus*) | Dust, raw materials, cardboard, inadequate sporicidal cleaning | Material transfer and disinfection, sporicidal rotation, HEPA integrity, cleaning frequency |
| Gram-negative rods (*Pseudomonas*, *Ralstonia*, *Burkholderia*) | Water, wet surfaces, spills, drains, sinks | Water points, wet cleaning practices, drain maintenance, standing water |
| Moulds / fungi (*Aspergillus*, *Penicillium*, *Cladosporium*) | Construction, HVAC, humidity, packaging/cardboard, plant material | Building works, air handling, humidity excursions, material airlocks |
| Yeasts | People, damp surfaces, HVAC | Combined personnel and facility review |

An ID that matches the site's flora library at that location suggests a
resident niche; an organism new to the library suggests an introduction
event - new material, construction, or personnel route. A shift in the
*flora* (e.g. new Gram-negatives in a normally Gram-positive room) is
itself a signal even within limits.

## Correlating with the world outside the plate

Microbiology explains what grew; the investigation must explain why *then*:

- **Operational events** - interventions, line stoppages, extended fills,
  glove changes, first/last air interventions near the location.
- **Maintenance and construction** - work orders in or near the suite,
  HEPA replacements, wall penetrations, adjacent-room activity.
- **Cleaning and disinfection** - was the cycle performed as scheduled?
  Disinfectant preparation and expiry records; sporicidal rotation
  current; disinfectant qualification still valid for the recovered
  organism type.
- **HVAC and differential pressure** - pressure cascade logs, airflow
  alarms, recovery-time data for the period.
- **Personnel** - who was in the room, their gowning qualification
  status, health declarations, training currency.
- **Material flows** - what entered the room and through which
  disinfection route.

The correlation record should name what was checked, not just conclude
"no cause found."

## Product-impact assessment: the template

The question is never "was the room dirty" - it is "could the batch's
sterility assurance or quality be compromised?" Work through:

1. **Exposure window** - batches/lots present between the last acceptable
   result at that location and the excursion.
2. **Location criticality** - proximity to open product or the critical
   zone; a Grade A glove excursion over an open fill line is not a Grade
   C floor event.
3. **Organism risk** - pathogenicity, spore-forming, objectionable status
   for the product and route.
4. **Compensating controls in the window** - Grade A protection,
   unidirectional airflow, intervention log, closed-system technology,
   terminal steps downstream (none for aseptic).
5. **Supporting data** - media fill status, sterility test results (weak
   evidence alone - see USP &lt;71&gt; statistical limits), personnel
   monitoring, adjacent-location results.
6. **Conclusion** - impact / no impact, **with the evidence, signed**.
   "No impact" must be argued, not asserted.

For excursions genuinely touching the critical zone, involve the
sterility-assurance owner early; disposition decisions on exposed batches
belong to QA with a documented rationale that a second reviewer could
reproduce.

## What inspectors look for: the failure modes

1. **"Isolated event, no impact" closures** with no ID, no trend review,
   no rationale - read as a system that does not learn.
2. **Organisms never identified** or identified only above action levels -
   Annex 1 expects isolate identification as part of the program,
   particularly Grades A/B.
3. **No site flora library**, so excursion significance cannot be judged.
4. **Alert excursions never reviewed** until they become action
   excursions.
5. **Investigations that stop at the lab door** - no correlation with
   operations, maintenance, or cleaning records.
6. **Product impact asserted from a passing sterility test alone** - a
   statistically weak sterility test cannot clear a contaminated
   environment.
7. **Same location, same organism, recurring excursions** with no CAPA
   escalation - the trend data existed and was ignored.
8. **Alert/action levels never re-derived** from site data; copied from a
   guideline table and frozen.
9. **Investigations opened late**, after batches shipped.
10. **No effectiveness check** on CAPAs - the same excursion returns next
    quarter.

## Worked example: a Grade B finger-dab excursion

*Fictional but realistic.* During aseptic filling of batch INJ-208, an
operator's post-operation glove print (Grade B background area) recovers
8 cfu against a 5 cfu action level. ID: *Staphylococcus epidermidis* -
skin flora.

- **Confirm:** plate and incubation valid; sampling technique observed as
  correct.
- **Correlate:** the operator performed an extended intervention
  (stopper-bowl adjustment) late in the fill; glove-sanitisation
  frequency during the intervention was below SOP expectation; the
  operator's gowning qualification is current but the site flora library
  shows the same organism at this room's glove locations twice in six
  months - a pattern, not an isolate.
- **Product impact:** the excursion is post-operation on gloves in Grade
  B; Grade A settled plates, active air, and in-operation EM around the
  intervention window are all no-growth; the intervention log and airflow
  visualisation study show the critical zone remained protected. QA
  concludes **no impact on INJ-208**, with the evidence on file - but
  flags the recurring glove-flora pattern.
- **CAPA:** reinforce sanitisation discipline during extended
  interventions (observation-based retraining is one element), add a
  mid-intervention glove-sanitisation prompt to the line-clearance aid,
  and review glove-supplier integrity data. Effectiveness check: zero
  glove excursions above alert across the next five fills in that room,
  verified from EM trends before closure.

Note the shape of a good outcome: the batch decision rests on layered
evidence, and the *pattern* - not the single plate - drives the CAPA.
