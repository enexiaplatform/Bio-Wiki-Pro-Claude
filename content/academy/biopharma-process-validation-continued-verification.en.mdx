# Biopharma Process Validation and Continued Process Verification

Process validation is not a ceremony performed after development, and continued
process verification is not a charting exercise performed after release. Together
they form a lifecycle evidence system for asking whether a defined process,
operated by qualified people and systems under a controlled strategy, continues
to produce material of the intended quality.

This workflow supplies no product-specific critical quality attribute, critical
process parameter, material attribute, performance indicator, normal operating
range, proven acceptable range, sampling plan, statistical method, signal rule,
specification, acceptance criterion, validation-batch count, bracketing strategy,
continued-verification frequency, revalidation trigger, filing category, batch
disposition or manufacturing authorization.

## Decision question

> For this biologic, process version, facility, scale, equipment train, material
> and analytical system, what process-knowledge, qualification, process-
> performance, continued-verification, deviation, change and governance evidence
> must be resolved before authorized owners can establish or maintain a validated
> state?

## Applicability and do-not-use boundary

Use this workflow to structure lifecycle evidence for a biotechnology-derived
protein drug-substance or drug-product process, process performance qualification
(PPQ), continued or ongoing process verification, periodic product/process review,
site or scale transfer, equipment or material change, process drift, revalidation
assessment and related lifecycle decisions.

Do not use it as:

- an approved validation master plan, protocol, report, CPV procedure, control
  strategy, statistical-analysis plan or regulatory submission;
- evidence that a fixed number of successful batches establishes a validated
  state for every product or process;
- a substitute for facility, utility, equipment, computerized-system,
  analytical-method, cleaning, sterilization, shipping or other applicable
  qualification and validation;
- authorization to execute PPQ, release validation material, enter routine
  manufacture, change a process, reduce monitoring, revalidate or disposition a
  batch;
- proof that release conformance, a capability index, absence of an alarm or one
  stable trend establishes total process control;
- an unchanged template for vaccines, viral vectors, cell or gene therapies,
  plasma-derived products, continuous manufacturing or other modalities outside
  the exact product, process, source and reviewer scope.

Process design, equipment/facility qualification, PPQ execution, validation
conclusion, release to the next stage, routine batch disposition, CPV signal
assessment, annual or periodic review, change approval and regulatory acceptance
are related but distinct decisions.

A statistical signal is not automatically a batch failure. A
within-specification result is not automatically evidence that the process
remains in a state of control.

## Controlled source map

| Source | Decision use | Important boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FDA Process Validation (2011) | Lifecycle model spanning process design, process qualification and continued process verification | Nonbinding US guidance; it supplies no universal batch count, sampling design or signal rule |
| EMA biologics process-validation guideline (2016) | Process characterization, evaluation and verification for applicable biotechnology-derived active substances | Protein-biologics active-substance scope and EU regulatory context must be confirmed |
| EU GMP Annex 15 | Lifecycle qualification/validation planning, protocols, deviations, release between stages and change control | Site and EU applicability matter; suggested stages do not create a universal execution design |
| ICH Q8(R2) | Product/process understanding and control-strategy concepts | It does not identify the actual CQAs, parameters, ranges or design space for a product |
| ICH Q11 | Development and manufacture of chemical and biotechnology/biological drug substances | Drug-substance scope and regional implementation must be retained |
| ICH Q9(R1) | Risk-based scope, formality, uncertainty and review | A risk score cannot create missing evidence or replace scientific judgment |
| ICH Q10 | Pharmaceutical-quality-system, knowledge, monitoring, CAPA, change and management-review interfaces | Procedural completion does not prove process performance or authorize a batch |
| ICH Q5E | Comparability after an applicable biologics manufacturing change | Validation and comparability answer different questions |
| ICH Q6B | Product characterization and specification context | Specifications are a selected subset and cannot replace process understanding or characterization |

## Separate eight lifecycle evidence objects

1. **Product, process and decision scope.** Product/modality, substance or
   product stage, process version, site, scale, equipment train, markets,
   lifecycle stage, intended decision and authority.
2. **Process knowledge and control strategy.** Product-quality attributes,
   material attributes, parameters, performance indicators, unit-operation
   relationships, evidence strength, uncertainty, controls and residual gaps.
3. **Qualification and enabling-system readiness.** Facility, utility,
   equipment, automation/data, method, material, personnel and procedural state,
   including deviations and limitations.
4. **PPQ or process-verification design.** Representative conditions,
   batches/runs, scale, campaign position, sampling, methods, predefined
   evaluation, statistical rationale, deviation handling and acceptance pathway.
5. **Execution and validation conclusion.** Actual conditions, genealogy,
   deviations, atypical results, protocol departures, data integrity, results,
   residual commitments and authorized conclusion.
6. **Continued process-verification plan.** Signals, data sources, stratification,
   baseline, limits, method/version, frequency, review window, escalation,
   ownership and plan-change control.
7. **Signal, investigation and action.** Statistical signals, specification
   signals, process events, complaints, deviations, competing hypotheses,
   product impact, CAPA/change and batch-disposition interfaces.
8. **Lifecycle maintenance and governance.** Periodic review, knowledge update,
   monitoring adjustment, revalidation assessment, comparability, regulatory
   commitments, management review and effectiveness evidence.

## Build the validation-to-verification chain

### 1. Freeze one decision and exact process version

State whether the decision concerns initial process qualification, entry to
routine manufacture, ongoing state-of-control assessment, a change, a transfer,
monitoring adjustment or revalidation. Freeze the product, process version,
site, scale, equipment train, material/control versions and applicable markets.

### 2. Trace process knowledge instead of copying a parameter list

For each material attribute, parameter and performance indicator, state the
product or process question, unit operation, evidence type, model or study,
applicability, contradictory evidence and remaining uncertainty. Do not promote
a parameter to critical merely because it is monitored or because a template
labels it CPP.

### 3. Establish enabling readiness before relying on execution

Map qualification of the facilities, utilities, equipment, automation/data
flows, analytical procedures, materials, operators and procedures that generate
or support validation evidence. Record approved versions, open deviations,
temporary controls and their potential effect on interpretation.

### 4. Make PPQ design representative and falsifiable

Explain why the proposed conditions challenge routine variability and why the
material, scale, equipment, campaign position, operators, sampling, methods and
evaluation can answer the stated validation question. Predefine how deviations,
missing data, atypical results and protocol changes will be handled. Do not
repair a failed design by silently changing criteria after execution.

### 5. Preserve execution facts and separate decisions

Link every result to the exact batch/run, process version, material, equipment,
method and raw-data source. Keep protocol conformance, deviation investigation,
validation conclusion, batch disposition and authorization to proceed as
separate records with separate owners.

### 6. Design CPV around questions, not available charts

For every signal, define why it matters, where it comes from, data latency,
stratification, baseline population, statistical method and version, entered
threshold source, specification relationship, review frequency, signal owner and
escalation path. Include process events and qualitative evidence where they are
material; absence of a numeric chart is not absence of process knowledge.

### 7. Interpret signals in context

Confirm data and method integrity, then distinguish special-cause signals,
common-cause variation, specification events, analytical shifts, material or
equipment changes, campaign/scale effects and data-collection artifacts. A
signal begins a governed question; it does not supply its own root cause,
product-impact conclusion or batch disposition.

### 8. Maintain the validated state through controlled learning

Use CPV, deviations, complaints, stability, maintenance/calibration, supplier
changes, investigations, CAPA, periodic review and regulatory commitments to
update knowledge. Changes to the monitoring plan, control strategy or validated
scope require versioned rationale and authorized review.

## Decision table

| Evidence condition | Defensible action | Do not claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Development knowledge is incomplete | Identify the affected validation question and obtain evidence or narrow the claim | PPQ can compensate for every knowledge gap |
| Equipment qualification is complete | Reconcile method, material, personnel, procedure, automation and process readiness | The manufacturing process is validated |
| Planned PPQ runs conform | Review representativeness, deviations, total data, uncertainty and residual commitments | A universal batch-count rule has been satisfied |
| Release specifications conform | Review sensitive process, characterization and trend evidence | The process is necessarily in a state of control |
| CPV crosses a statistical threshold but remains within specification | Follow the approved signal-review pathway and assess process/product context | Ignore it or automatically reject the batch |
| Capability metric is favorable | Confirm distribution, stability, independence, measurement system, stratification and threshold basis | Capability alone proves validation |
| A material process change is proposed | Assess qualification, validation, comparability, filing and CPV impact against the exact change | Prior validation automatically covers the change |
| Evidence or reviewer coverage remains open | Mark `Evidence required` or `Qualified review required` | Validated state, routine authorization or regulatory acceptance |

## Worked example: fictional CHO process moving from PPQ into CPV

A fictional CHO monoclonal-antibody drug-substance process has completed a
proposed PPQ campaign and is preparing the transition into routine CPV. A
normalized upstream performance indicator produces one point outside an entered
statistical threshold while remaining inside the fictional registered
specification. A chromatography-skid maintenance event and a raw-material lot
transition occurred in the same review window. The example contains no real
product, process parameter, range, acceptance criterion, batch count, method,
statistical limit, specification or outcome.

| Decision area | Evidence held | Open evidence | Accountable role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Process knowledge | Controlled product-process map and study index | Criticality, model applicability and unresolved interactions require review | Process development/MSAT and product quality |
| Enabling readiness | Qualification and method references indexed | Two minor deviations and automation-version bridge remain open | Validation, engineering/automation and Quality Unit |
| PPQ | Execution package and predefined evaluation indexed | Representativeness, deviations and final conclusion are not approved | Process validation and Quality Unit |
| CPV plan | Signal list, baseline and fictional thresholds recorded | Statistical method/version, stratification and review frequency require approval | Statistics/process data science and MSAT |
| Signal | One fictional statistical signal within specification | Data integrity, material, equipment, process and analytical hypotheses remain open | MSAT, manufacturing, QC and Quality Unit |
| Lifecycle decision | Five actions and staged review route recorded | Routine authorization, validation conclusion and monitoring commitments remain open | Quality Unit and regulatory CMC |

The example remains unresolved. It demonstrates signal separation and decision
lineage; it does not establish a reusable CPV design or validation conclusion.

## Working asset

Use the **Biopharma Process Validation & CPV Evidence Map** to maintain:

- one frozen validation or state-of-control decision brief;
- lifecycle stage, process version and process/control evidence;
- facility, equipment, utility, automation, method, material and personnel
  readiness;
- PPQ design, execution, deviations, results and residual commitments;
- CPV signal definitions, baselines, entered thresholds, stratification and
  review ownership;
- formula-driven separation of statistical and specification signals;
- signal assessment, evidence provenance, actions and qualified review.

The blank workbook fails closed as `Evidence required`. A structurally complete
package can only reach `Qualified review required`; it never becomes a
validation approval, batch disposition or manufacturing authorization.

## Limitations

This lesson and asset structure evidence and decision ownership. They do not
establish process knowledge, criticality, design space, proven acceptable range,
normal operating range, qualification, process characterization, PPQ design or
success, state of control, statistical method, monitoring frequency, capability,
comparability, regulatory acceptability, validated state, batch disposition or
authorization for routine manufacture.

Every product-, process-, site-, scale-, equipment-, material-, method-, data-
system- and market-specific conclusion requires controlled evidence, current
procedures and qualified review.
