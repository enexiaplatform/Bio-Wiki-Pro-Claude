# Cross-product evidence, investigation and change governance

A quality signal creates several decisions, not one. The team must establish
what was observed, whether the signal is reproducible, which explanations are
credible, what product or process scope may be affected, whether immediate
correction is needed, whether systemic action is justified, how effectiveness
will be assessed, whether a controlled change is proposed and what knowledge
must move to the next owner. Combining these objects into one status hides
uncertainty and weakens accountability.

## Decision question

> How should a versioned analytical, process or quality-system signal move
> through evidence review, investigation, CAPA, effectiveness, change control
> and knowledge transfer without becoming an unsupported conclusion?

## Applicability and do-not-use boundary

Use this lesson across Biopharma, Pharma/API, drug product and quality-laboratory
work when a signal must be reconstructed and governed through a lifecycle. It
supports OOS/OOT or deviation context, continued-verification observations,
method-performance signals, recurring quality events, CAPA effectiveness and
change-impact review.

Do not use it to invalidate a result, determine root cause, disposition a batch,
approve CAPA, set alert/action limits, change a method or process, close change
control, authorize a filing or update an executable Atlas rule. Those decisions
require controlled site procedures, complete product/process evidence and
accountable qualified roles.

## Controlled source map

| Source | Decision use | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ICH Q9(R1) | Apply science- and risk-based assessment, control, communication and review | Risk ranking does not prove cause or authorize action |
| ICH Q10 | Connect monitoring, CAPA, change management, management review and knowledge management | A quality-system model does not replace regional requirements or site procedures |
| FDA OOS guidance | Preserve laboratory assessment, full-scale investigation and documented conclusions as controlled steps | Applicability and current procedural requirements must be confirmed by the site |
| FDA data-integrity guidance | Protect attribution, original records, review and the data lifecycle | A data concern does not establish intent or product impact |
| FDA Process Validation guidance | Connect process/product monitoring to lifecycle state of control | A statistical signal is not a validation or disposition conclusion |
| ICH Q14 | Connect analytical-procedure knowledge, performance and lifecycle change | It does not approve a method, validation or transfer decision |

## Separate eleven governance objects

1. **Signal definition** — source, metric/result, timestamp, scope, rule or
   observation that caused review.
2. **Data and transformation lineage** — original records, query, calculation,
   exclusions, versions and the reproducible result.
3. **Measurement context** — sample, method, reference, instrument/software,
   analyst/environment and known capability limitations.
4. **Process and product context** — material, batch, process, equipment, site,
   storage/package and concurrent events.
5. **Statistical interpretation** — data-generating process, comparison basis,
   assumptions, uncertainty and the distinction between signal and criterion.
6. **Investigation hypotheses** — plausible explanations with supporting,
   contradicting and missing evidence.
7. **Affected-scope assessment** — which results, batches, methods, products,
   sites or periods are potentially relevant and why.
8. **Correction and containment** — immediate controlled actions, their owner
   and the fact that they do not establish cause.
9. **CAPA and effectiveness** — cause-linked systemic action, expected outcome,
   measure, observation period, confounders and decision rule.
10. **Change control** — proposed future state, impact, validation/transfer and
    regulatory review, implementation authority and post-change evidence.
11. **Knowledge handoff** — frozen evidence, limitations, unresolved questions,
    owner, receiving use and triggers carried to the next lifecycle stage.

## Run the signal-to-decision workflow

### 1. Reconstruct the signal

Freeze the original data, query or calculation, transformations, exclusions,
versions and timestamps. Reproduce the signal from attributable records. Record
what rule, expectation or comparison made it noteworthy. If it cannot be
reproduced, document that fact and investigate the data path; do not silently
replace the original analysis.

**Gate:** no investigation conclusion proceeds from an untraceable or
irreproducible signal.

### 2. Establish measurement, process and product context

Align samples, methods, references, instruments/software, analysts, materials,
batches, process/equipment versions, sites and relevant storage or package
states. Identify missing or non-comparable records. A measurement-system change
can look like a process shift; a process change can alter sample behavior; a
data transformation can create or hide a trend.

**Gate:** observation, measurement capability and product/process meaning stay
separate until evidence connects them.

### 3. Test competing hypotheses and affected scope

List plausible laboratory, data, material, process, equipment, environment,
human-system and product explanations. For each, record supporting,
contradicting and missing evidence plus a discriminating check. Determine the
potentially affected scope from evidence and mechanism rather than convenience.

**Gate:** “root cause” remains open when credible alternatives or material
evidence gaps remain unresolved.

### 4. Separate correction, CAPA and effectiveness

A correction addresses the observed condition. Containment manages immediate
risk. Corrective or preventive action should address a supported cause or
systemic risk. Define what should change if the action works, how it will be
measured, the relevant observation window, data quality, confounders, owner and
what outcome triggers continuation, escalation or redesign.

**Gate:** retraining, procedure revision or monitoring is not automatically an
adequate CAPA, and action completion is not effectiveness.

### 5. Govern change and knowledge transfer

When a method, process, material, system or control change is proposed, link it
to the frozen investigation evidence and residual uncertainty. Assess affected
products/sites, validation or transfer needs, data and documentation changes,
regulatory commitments, implementation authority and post-change monitoring.
Transfer the evidence package and open boundaries to the receiving role.

**Gate:** the package can support review but cannot approve implementation,
close the investigation/CAPA/change or modify an Atlas rule.

## Evidence and study activities

### Signal reconstruction challenge

Ask another qualified reviewer to reproduce the signal using the declared data,
query, transformations and exclusions. Compare outputs and reconcile every
material difference. The deliverable is a versioned signal package and a list
of data-lineage limitations, not a compliance conclusion.

### Competing-hypothesis evidence matrix

For each hypothesis, record mechanism, expected observation, evidence for,
evidence against, missing evidence, discriminating action and owner. Preserve
“inconclusive” where the available evidence cannot distinguish alternatives.

### CAPA effectiveness study frame

Define the action mechanism, expected outcome, measure, baseline, observation
period, data source, confounders, review owner and response to success, partial
response or failure. The site owns the controlled protocol and criteria.

### Change and knowledge-transfer simulation

Walk the proposed future state with process/method owners, Quality and receiving
users. Confirm that each can locate the evidence basis, explain limitations,
identify required validation/transfer work and name triggers that reopen review.
Simulation does not authorize the change.

## Evidence and study architecture

| Evidence activity | Minimum controlled inputs | Review output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Signal reconstruction | Original records, query/calculation, transformations, exclusions, versions and timestamps | Reproducible signal package or explicit lineage failure |
| Hypothesis discrimination | Mechanisms, expected observations, supporting/contradicting evidence and affected scope | Ranked evidence needs without forced root cause |
| Effectiveness assessment | Action mechanism, baseline, measure, observation period, confounders and decision owner | Effectiveness review recommendation, not automatic closure |
| Change/knowledge handoff | Frozen investigation basis, proposed state, impact, validation/transfer needs and residual uncertainty | Controlled handoff with authorization still open |

The appropriate depth depends on risk, product and lifecycle context. The table
does not prescribe sample size, statistical rule, action limit, observation
period or acceptance criterion.

## Decision table

| Evidence state | Defensible next action | Do not claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Signal is reproducible but cause is open | Preserve scope, test competing hypotheses and apply proportionate controls | Root cause or product impact |
| Signal cannot be reproduced from declared records | Open a data-lineage assessment and preserve the original observation | That the signal never occurred or that misconduct occurred |
| Correction is complete | Verify immediate condition and continue causal/systemic assessment where warranted | CAPA completion or effectiveness |
| CAPA action is implemented | Run the predefined effectiveness review with attributable data | Effectiveness from implementation alone |
| Change is scientifically reasonable | Complete controlled impact, validation/transfer, quality and regulatory review | Implementation approval |
| Evidence conflicts remain material | Keep the decision open and escalate to accountable roles | Forced consensus or silent exclusion |

## Worked example: fictional cross-product evidence loop

A fictional analytical trend appears across several drug-product stability
pulls. The original query excluded one record and a reference lot changed during
the period. A parallel API process signal and a Biopharma method-performance
question make the issue look systemic, but the data-generating processes are
not equivalent. The team reconstructs each signal, separates method, reference,
process and product hypotheses, records affected scope and uses retraining only
as an immediate correction where justified. A proposed method change remains in
change control until impact, validation/transfer and effectiveness evidence are
reviewed. The example reaches no root cause, batch disposition, CAPA approval or
change authorization.

## Completion standard

The package is ready for accountable review only when the signal is
reproducible or its lineage gap is explicit; context and versions are traceable;
competing hypotheses and affected scope are visible; corrections, CAPA and
effectiveness are distinct; proposed changes retain validation/transfer and
authorization gates; and unresolved evidence has owners and due dates.

## Limitations

This lesson is `specialist-review-required`. It does not establish compliance,
data integrity, root cause, product impact, CAPA adequacy/effectiveness, method
fitness, validation state, change approval, batch disposition or regulatory
conclusion. It does not verify any Domain Pack or expand the executable Quality
Lab Compiler.
