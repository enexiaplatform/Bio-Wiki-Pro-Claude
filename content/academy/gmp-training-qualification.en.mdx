# GMP Training & Personnel Qualification

GMP requires that personnel are qualified by **education, training, and
experience** to perform their assigned functions. The framework is FDA
21 CFR 211.25 (Personnel Qualifications) and 211.25(a)'s requirement for
training in current GMP "on a continuing basis", plus EU GMP Part I Chapter 2
(Personnel), which requires training programs for all personnel whose duties
take them into manufacturing or laboratory areas, with training effectiveness
periodically assessed.

The compliance question is never "did people attend training?" It is "can you
prove the people performing this task are competent to perform it?" Those are
different questions, and audit findings live in the gap between them.

## Training vs qualification

The two words are not interchangeable:

| | Training | Qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose | Impart knowledge or skill. | Verify the person can perform a specific task to a defined standard. |
| Evidence | Attendance, read-and-understand sign-off, course completion. | Observed performance, written/practical assessment, demonstrated technique against acceptance criteria. |
| When done | Onboarding, SOP changes, refreshers. | Before the person performs the task unsupervised; re-qualified on a defined schedule or after failure. |
| Examples | GMP induction, data-integrity awareness, SOP update session. | Aseptic gowning qualification, media-fill participation, method authorization for an analyst, forklift licence. |

A training record says the person was *informed*. A qualification record says
the person was *verified*. Tasks where errors carry real risk - aseptic
technique, sterile testing, data entry in critical systems, cleaning of
product-contact parts - need qualification, not just training.

## Designing the training system

1. **Define curricula by role.** For each job function, list required GMP
   topics, SOPs, and qualifications. A QC microbiologist, a warehouse
   operator, and a QA reviewer share core GMP training but diverge sharply
   after that. Seniority is not a curriculum.
2. **Choose the method by risk.** Read-and-understand suits low-risk awareness
   updates. Classroom/e-learning suits concepts. On-the-job training with a
   qualified trainer suits manual skills. Assessment requirements should rise
   with task risk.
3. **Set effectiveness checks.** A knowledge quiz for concepts; observed
   performance or a practical test for skills. The check should match what the
   person will actually do.
4. **Define retraining triggers.** Material SOP revision, a deviation or CAPA
   pointing at performance, a long absence from the task, a failed assessment,
   new equipment, or a transfer into a new role.
5. **Assign ownership.** Supervisors confirm on-the-job competence; QA owns
   the GMP curriculum and the training matrix; the site training system tracks
   completion and flags overdue items.

## Assessing effectiveness

EU GMP Chapter 2 expects training effectiveness to be *periodically assessed*.
Practical layers, from weakest to strongest:

- **Completion**: the training happened (necessary, never sufficient).
- **Learning**: a quiz or assessment at the end of the session.
- **Behavior**: supervisor observation that the task is performed correctly on
  the floor; error and deviation rates for the trained task.
- **Results**: the process outcome improves - fewer deviations, fewer
  documentation errors, better right-first-time in the trained area.

If your only evidence is layer 1, expect questions in an inspection.

## Beyond training: the other personnel requirements

Training is one leg of the personnel framework. The same regulations also
require:

- **Health and hygiene**: personnel with illnesses or open lesions that could
  affect product are excluded from exposure-prone work (21 CFR 211.28; EU GMP
  Chapter 2 covers hygiene programs and health monitoring proportionate to
  risk).
- **Gowning appropriate to the area**: clean clothing and, for sterile areas,
  qualified gowning linked to the environmental monitoring program.
- **Consultant control**: 21 CFR 211.34 requires that consultants advising on
  GMP-regulated work have the education, training, and experience for the
  subject - and that records of their qualifications are maintained.

A training matrix that ignores hygiene, gowning qualification, and consultant
qualification covers only part of the personnel chapter.

## Onboarding sequence that holds up

A defensible path for a new QC analyst or operator:

1. GMP induction and site data-integrity training before entering GMP areas.
2. Role curriculum assigned in the training matrix.
3. Reading and classroom work for the governing SOPs.
4. On-the-job training under a qualified trainer.
5. Qualification assessment against defined criteria - only then does the
   person work unsupervised.
6. Entry into the refresher and re-qualification cycle from day one.

## Records inspectors check

- An up-to-date **training matrix** per person and per role, showing required
  versus completed items.
- Training records with date, content/version reference, trainer, and
  assessment result - not just a signature list.
- **Qualification records** for verified tasks, with acceptance criteria and
  re-qualification dates.
- Evidence that **retraining occurred before** staff performed a task under a
  revised SOP - auditors cross-check training dates against SOP effective
  dates.
- Continuing GMP training: annual or otherwise defined refreshers with
  attendance records.

## Common inspection findings

- Staff performing tasks for which no training record exists.
- "Read and understand" sign-offs dated *after* the SOP effective date, or
  after the person had already performed the task.
- Training matrices that show 100% green for years with no retraining after
  SOP revisions.
- No assessment of any kind; effectiveness never evaluated.
- Deviations attribute root cause to "training gap" but no retraining record
  follows.
- Contractor and temporary staff working without documented training.
- Training records that cannot be linked to the SOP version trained.
