# Biopharma Upstream Process Control

Upstream process control is not a universal list of bioreactor parameters. It
is a product- and process-specific evidence system that explains how the cell
substrate, raw materials, operating conditions, and process performance may
affect the quality of the harvested material and, ultimately, the drug
substance and drug product.

This lesson helps teams build that evidence system without inventing platform
ranges, criticality labels, or causal conclusions.

## Decision question

> Which upstream material attributes, process parameters, and performance
> indicators require control or monitoring for this product and process, what
> evidence links them to product quality, and which relationships remain too
> uncertain for a controlled decision?

## Applicability and do-not-use boundary

The workflow is designed for upstream manufacture of cell-culture-derived,
well-characterized proteins and polypeptides within the relevant scope of the
cited ICH guidance.

Do not apply it unchanged to:

- cell or gene therapy products, viral vectors, mRNA, vaccines, or
  plasma-derived products;
- microbial fermentation products where source, process, and impurity logic
  materially differ;
- a product-specific control strategy, registered process, PPQ protocol,
  batch disposition, or regulatory filing without authorized review;
- defining numeric operating ranges from generic platform experience.

The mapping method can support other modalities, but their sources, product
risks, process measurements, acceptance logic, and reviewer competence must be
established separately.

## Controlled source map

| Source | Decision use | Important boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ICH Q8(R2) | Risk-based links between material attributes, process parameters, and product CQAs | Does not provide product-specific parameters or ranges |
| ICH Q11 | Development and manufacture of drug substances, including biotechnology/biological entities | Starting-point and control-strategy application depends on product scope |
| ICH Q5D | Cell-substrate and cell-banking evidence | Does not replace product- and process-specific upstream development |
| ICH Q6B | Characterization and specification context for applicable biological products | Specifications remain one part of the total control strategy |
| ICH Q9(R1) and Q10 | Risk-based decisions, knowledge management, change control, and lifecycle governance | Risk tools do not manufacture evidence or approve criticality |
| FDA Process Validation, 2011 | Lifecycle process design, qualification, and continued verification | Nonbinding US guidance; regional and product applicability must be confirmed |

## Separate four kinds of information

An upstream map becomes unreliable when unlike concepts are mixed in one
“critical parameter” list.

| Information type | Question | Example categories, not a universal list |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Material attribute | What property enters the process? | Cell-bank state, medium/feed identity, raw-material variability, inoculum condition |
| Process parameter | What condition is set or controlled? | Time, temperature, pH, gas delivery, mixing, feed, pressure, harvest setting |
| Performance indicator | What shows how the culture or operation is behaving? | Growth, viability, metabolism, consumption, productivity, control performance |
| Product-quality observation | What attribute of product or process output may change? | Activity, heterogeneity, aggregation, impurity, charge, glycan, degradation, safety-related attribute |

The same measurement can serve different roles in different processes. Its
label must follow the decision and evidence, not the instrument tag.

## Build the upstream evidence chain

### 1. Freeze product, process, and lifecycle scope

Record the molecule or product identifier, modality, development or commercial
stage, cell substrate, process version, scale, site, equipment train, and the
decision being supported. A relationship established for one scale or process
version should not silently become universal.

### 2. Define candidate product-quality attributes

Start from the product-quality and patient relevance already established or
hypothesized. For each attribute, distinguish:

- **supported criticality** — a reviewed relationship to safety, efficacy, or
  another material product outcome is documented;
- **working hypothesis** — plausible but not adequately confirmed;
- **not material within scope** — evidence supports exclusion for the defined
  decision;
- **unresolved** — evidence or ownership is insufficient.

These are evidence states, not permanent labels.

### 3. Map the actual process

Describe the unit operations and transfers that exist for this process: cell
bank retrieval, inoculum or seed expansion, production culture, additions and
feeds, holds, harvest, and transfer to downstream processing as applicable.

For each step, capture:

- intended purpose;
- incoming material and process state;
- set, controlled, and observed variables;
- sampling and measurement points;
- output and handoff criteria;
- deviations, scale sensitivities, and open assumptions.

### 4. Propose relationships before assigning criticality

A relationship record should contain a complete sentence:

> Within [defined scope], changing [material attribute or parameter] may affect
> [performance indicator or product-quality attribute] through [proposed
> mechanism], supported by [evidence], with [limitations and uncertainty].

This makes it possible to challenge each part. A bare arrow from “pH” to
“glycosylation” hides scope, direction, interaction, evidence, and uncertainty.

### 5. Grade evidence without confusing association and causality

Use an explicit evidence-strength vocabulary:

| Evidence state | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Hypothesis | Mechanistically plausible or platform-derived; product evidence is insufficient |
| Observed association | Product data show a relationship, but confounding or design limits causal interpretation |
| Designed-study support | A planned study challenged the factor or relationship within a defined range and context |
| Multi-source support | Designed studies, manufacturing experience, and orthogonal evidence converge within a defined scope |
| Contradictory or unresolved | Evidence conflicts, measurement is weak, or the relevant condition was not studied |

Do not upgrade evidence strength because several reports repeat the same
unsupported platform assumption.

### 6. Decide the control placement

Not every material relationship becomes a tight process control. Depending on
the evidence and risk, the control strategy may use:

- material specification, qualification, or supplier control;
- procedural control or recipe setting;
- parameter monitoring with an established response;
- in-process measurement or process-performance criterion;
- downstream control or clearance evidence;
- product characterization, release, or stability testing;
- continued verification and trend review;
- an explicit evidence request before a decision.

The map should show why the selected control is sufficient and where another
control compensates for residual uncertainty.

### 7. Preserve ranges and terminology correctly

Record the controlled document or study that establishes any set point, range,
action boundary, or response. Do not convert a development range, equipment
capability, observed manufacturing range, proven acceptable range, or design
space into another category through casual wording.

The workbook therefore stores the **range or criterion reference**, not a
generic numeric recommendation.

### 8. Challenge scale and transfer assumptions

At a new scale, site, vessel, sensor, control system, or material source, the
same nominal setting may not create the same local environment or biological
response. Ask which physical, biological, measurement, and timing relationships
could change and what evidence is needed for comparability, qualification, or
continued verification.

### 9. Connect the map to lifecycle governance

Every material relationship needs a trigger for re-evaluation. Examples include
a cell-bank change, raw-material change, new scale or site, equipment or control
strategy change, trend shift, new degradation or impurity signal, method change,
or evidence that contradicts the existing rationale.

## Decision table

| Evidence condition | Defensible action | Do not claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product-specific designed evidence and manufacturing experience converge | Propose a controlled relationship within the studied scope for qualified review | Universal causality outside that scope |
| Platform knowledge is plausible but product data are missing | Keep as hypothesis; prioritize study, monitoring, or risk control | “Platform proven” for the new molecule |
| Association appears in trend data with confounding | Investigate measurement, materials, interactions, process state, and study design | That the parameter caused the outcome |
| Product attributes remain unchanged while a process signal shifts | Assess measurement, trend, delayed effects, unmeasured attributes, and process robustness | That the signal is irrelevant |
| Evidence conflicts across studies or scales | Preserve the contradiction, assess scope and method differences, and escalate | Average the conclusions into “medium confidence” |
| Range or criterion provenance is missing | Mark evidence-required and block controlled use of the numeric claim | A generic or vendor range as site control |

## Worked example: fictional CHO culture signal

A fictional CHO-derived monoclonal-antibody process shows a recurring shift in
one late-culture performance indicator after a media-component supplier change.
Routine release attributes remain within the current specification.

The team records the following rather than labeling the supplier change causal:

| Question | Evidence held | Open evidence | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Is the input materially different? | Supplier change record and incoming test results | Comparability of relevant material attributes beyond routine certificate data | Raw-material sciences and quality |
| Is the process signal real? | Repeated process trend | Sensor, sampling, calculation, and batch-context review | Upstream process development/MSAT |
| Could product quality be affected? | Current release and selected characterization results | Assessment of unmeasured or delayed attributes and stability relevance | Analytical development and product quality |
| Is the relationship causal? | Temporal association and biological rationale | Product-specific designed study or stronger convergent evidence | Upstream development and statistics |
| What control is justified now? | Existing supplier and process controls | Interim monitoring, evidence request, and change-control decision | Quality/change owner |

The example stops before setting a parameter range or supplier conclusion. Its
value is keeping observation, hypothesis, evidence, control, and decision owner
separate.

## Working asset

Use the **Biopharma Upstream Control Evidence Map** to maintain:

- a decision brief and explicit product/process scope;
- product-quality attributes and current criticality evidence;
- process-stage, material, parameter, performance, and CQA links;
- source and product evidence with strength and limitations;
- formula-driven completeness and review flags;
- actions, lifecycle triggers, reviewer roles, and sign-off state.

The workbook deliberately contains no generic numeric process ranges. A blank
record or missing evidence reference remains visible as `Evidence required`.

## Limitations

- This workflow does not define product-specific CQAs, CPPs, material
  attributes, operating ranges, action limits, specifications, sampling plans,
  or PPQ acceptance criteria.
- It does not establish causality from trend association.
- It does not replace process characterization, validation, comparability,
  approved control strategy, change control, or regulatory review.
- The fictional example is a usability demonstration, not a platform benchmark.
- Atlas must not call this content SME-reviewed until appointment and review
  evidence are recorded.
