# Pharma API Process Development, Impurity Fate and Control Strategy

API process development is not a sequence of reaction arrows, and impurity
control is not a final-product chromatogram. The decision system connects the
intended drug-substance quality to the route, starting materials, reagents,
solvents, catalysts, transformations, work-up, isolation, solid-state handling,
analytical capability, observed fate, control placement and lifecycle change.

This workflow supplies no chemical route, starting material, reagent, solvent,
catalyst, stoichiometry, concentration, temperature, pressure, pH, addition
rate, endpoint, hold time, crystallization condition, drying condition, milling
setting, particle-size target, impurity identity, purge factor, acceptable
intake, reporting threshold, specification, acceptance criterion, sampling plan,
validation design, filing conclusion or batch disposition.

## Decision question

> For this small-molecule drug substance, route and process version, what
> evidence connects each material impurity risk to its origin, pathway,
> stage-specific observation, analytical capability, fate, control location,
> lifecycle sensitivity and accountable decision?

## Applicability and do-not-use boundary

Use this workflow to structure evidence for a chemically synthesized small-
molecule drug substance during route/process development, scale-up, process
characterization, control-strategy development, technology transfer, validation,
supplier or process change and lifecycle review.

Do not use it as:

- a synthetic route, experiment plan, master batch record, manufacturing
  instruction, process-development report, specification or regulatory filing;
- a safety assessment for an impurity or a source of an acceptable intake;
- proof that an impurity is absent because it is not detected;
- permission to multiply or transfer a purge factor across a different route,
  scale, equipment train, solvent system, analytical method or impurity;
- a substitute for current product-, market- and stage-specific requirements,
  toxicology, qualified process chemistry, analytical development, QC, Quality
  Unit or regulatory CMC review;
- an unchanged template for fermentation-derived APIs, peptides,
  oligonucleotides, radiopharmaceuticals, highly potent compounds, continuous
  manufacturing or another modality with different evidence needs.

Process understanding, impurity identification, impurity qualification or safety
assessment, observed reduction, a defensible purge claim, analytical-method
capability, specification acceptance, validation, filing acceptance and batch
disposition are related but distinct decisions.

An observed ratio is not a transferable purge factor. A result below a reporting
boundary is not proof of zero material, complete removal or absence of risk.

## Controlled source map

| Source | Decision use | Important boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ICH Q11 and Q11 Q&As | Drug-substance process development, commercial-process description, control strategy and starting-material justification | They do not select the route, starting material or process conditions for a product |
| ICH Q3A(R2) | Actual and potential organic impurity understanding for applicable new drug substances | Scope, thresholds and qualification pathways must be applied to the exact product and region |
| ICH Q3C(R9) | Residual-solvent classification and control context | Current maintenance status and product/process applicability must be confirmed; this lesson supplies no PDE or limit |
| ICH Q3D(R2) | Elemental-impurity risk and control context | It is not a generic catalyst-purge calculation or product-specific risk assessment |
| ICH M7(R2) | Assessment and control of applicable DNA-reactive mutagenic impurities | Hazard classification, acceptable intake and control require qualified product-specific work |
| ICH Q6A | Specification definition, justification and relationship to process and development knowledge | A specification is one part of the control strategy, not a substitute for process understanding |
| ICH Q7 | API GMP across materials, production, laboratory control, release, storage and distribution | GMP scope and regional implementation must be confirmed; it does not replace development science |
| ICH Q2(R2) and Q14 | Analytical procedure performance and lifecycle development | Detection, quantitation and reportability claims require the exact method, matrix and intended use |
| ICH Q9(R1) and Q10 | Risk, uncertainty, knowledge, change and pharmaceutical-quality-system governance | A risk score or completed workflow cannot manufacture missing evidence |

## Separate eight API evidence objects

1. **Product, route and decision scope.** Drug substance, salt/solvate or form,
   route and process version, lifecycle stage, site/scale, intended decision,
   markets and decision owner.
2. **Starting materials and process inputs.** Starting materials, intermediates,
   reagents, solvents, catalysts, processing aids, water and recovered materials,
   with supplier, grade, specification, use and change history.
3. **Transformations and unit operations.** Reactions, quench, extraction,
   washing, phase separation, concentration, solvent exchange, crystallization,
   filtration, drying, milling, blending, packaging and holds as applicable.
4. **Impurity identity and origin map.** Process-related impurities, degradation
   products, residual solvents, elemental sources and applicable mutagenic risks,
   keeping predicted, observed and confirmed identity separate.
5. **Observed fate and process evidence.** Stage-specific results, sampling,
   material genealogy, basis and units, method/version, reporting boundary,
   variability, mass-balance limitations and exact process applicability.
6. **Analytical capability.** Intended use, specificity/selectivity,
   sensitivity, response or reference basis, sample preparation, interference,
   recovery, validation/qualification state and data integrity.
7. **Control placement and rationale.** Input control, process control,
   in-process test, purge/process understanding, intermediate control, drug-
   substance specification or another justified layer, including residual risk.
8. **Lifecycle and governance.** Validation, supplier/process/site/scale/method
   changes, stability, periodic review, deviations, investigations, filing
   commitments, reviewer roles, open evidence and authorized decision.

## Build the route-to-control evidence chain

### 1. Freeze one route and one decision

Record the exact drug substance and form, route/process version, sites, scales,
equipment basis, development stage, applicable markets and decision. Do not
combine evidence across route versions without an explicit bridge.

### 2. Map inputs to transformations and plausible impurity origins

For each starting material, intermediate, reagent, solvent, catalyst and
recovered material, record where it enters, what quality attributes matter, what
impurities it can introduce or form, and what supplier/process changes would
invalidate the assumption. Distinguish a mechanistic hypothesis from confirmed
identity and measured occurrence.

### 3. Map work-up, isolation and solid-state operations

Record the purpose and product-quality relationship of each operation. Extraction,
washing, phase separation, solvent exchange, crystallization, filtration, drying
and milling can affect different impurities and material attributes; an equipment
name or standard operating step is not evidence of performance.

### 4. Preserve observations at their actual evidence strength

Link each result to batch or experiment, stage, sample location, process version,
method/version, units and basis. Record non-detects with the relevant reporting
boundary and method capability. Keep calculated observed ratios visible, but
label them as observations bounded by the studied material and conditions.

### 5. Challenge analytical capability before claiming fate

Ask whether the procedure can distinguish and recover the impurity in the exact
matrix and concentration range, whether response/reference assumptions are
appropriate, and whether sampling or preparation could bias the comparison. A
process cannot be credited with removal that the measurement system cannot
support.

### 6. Place controls where the evidence supports them

For each material risk, compare the strength and lifecycle resilience of input,
process, intermediate and drug-substance controls. State the rationale, evidence
locators, uncertainty, monitoring or verification need, change triggers and
owner. Final testing is not automatically the best or only control.

### 7. Separate impurity classes and decision authorities

Organic process impurities, degradation products, residual solvents, elemental
impurities and mutagenic impurities can require different scope, evidence and
specialist review. Do not collapse safety assessment, analytical capability,
process control and specification decisions into one status.

### 8. Reopen the map after material change

A new supplier, route step, reagent grade, recovered solvent, equipment train,
scale, hold, isolation, drying/milling operation, analytical procedure, site or
filing commitment can change origin, fate, detection or control. Preserve the
pre-change basis, predicted impact, evidence plan, actual outcome and authorized
implementation decision.

## Decision table

| Evidence condition | Defensible action | Do not claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An impurity is mechanistically plausible but not confirmed | Record the pathway hypothesis, analytical question and evidence needed to test it | The impurity exists or is absent |
| A stage result is below the reporting boundary | Preserve method capability, sample/basis and downstream evidence | Zero concentration or complete purge |
| Input and output results share units but not sampling or matrix basis | Resolve comparability before calculating or interpreting a ratio | A quantitative purge factor |
| One experiment shows an observed reduction | Treat the ratio as bounded evidence and examine variability, mechanism and scale/process applicability | A transferable purge claim |
| A final API test conforms | Reconcile process, impurity, method, deviation and lifecycle evidence | The total control strategy is adequate |
| A proposed control relies on process understanding | Link mechanism, representative data, variability, analytical capability and change sensitivity | Testing can be removed automatically |
| A supplier or isolation change occurs | Reopen affected origin, fate, analytical, control, validation and filing questions | Prior evidence applies unchanged |
| Evidence or reviewer coverage remains open | Mark `Evidence required` or `Qualified review required` | Controlled approval, regulatory acceptance or batch disposition |

## Worked example: fictional late-forming impurity signal

A fictional three-stage small-molecule API route changes the manufacturing site
of one reagent and modifies a solvent-recovery configuration used before the
final isolation. Development observations show a late-forming unknown signal in
one intermediate sample. A later sample reports the signal below an entered
reporting boundary, but the two samples use different matrices and preparation
versions. The example contains no real molecule, chemical structure, material,
route, condition, method, limit or specification.

| Evidence object | Evidence held | Open evidence | Accountable role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Route and inputs | Frozen fictional route version and change references | Reagent-site and recovered-solvent impurity profiles are incomplete | Process chemistry and supplier quality |
| Origin/pathway | Two competing formation/carryover hypotheses | Identity and pathway are not confirmed | Process chemistry and analytical development |
| Observed fate | Stage-linked fictional observations | Matrix/method comparability and reporting boundary prevent a purge conclusion | Analytical development and process chemistry |
| Control placement | Candidate input, process and intermediate controls listed | Capability, variability, lifecycle resilience and safety context remain open | QC, Quality Unit and regulatory CMC |
| Decision | Five actions and reviewers assigned | No impurity, specification, process-change or batch decision is approved | Authorized cross-functional owner |

The case stays unresolved. Its value is the visible separation of observation,
hypothesis, method capability, observed ratio, control proposal and authority.

## Working asset

Use the **Pharma API Process & Impurity Control Evidence Map** to maintain:

- one frozen product/route/change decision brief;
- route inputs, supplier/use basis and unit-operation map;
- actual and potential impurity origin/pathway records;
- stage-specific observed-fate comparisons with visible comparability gates;
- analytical intended use, capability, limitations and evidence;
- input, process, intermediate and final control placement rationale;
- change impact, evidence provenance, actions and qualified review.

The blank workbook fails closed as `Evidence required`. Even a structurally
complete workbook can only reach `Qualified review required`; it never becomes a
route approval, process instruction, impurity safety conclusion, specification,
regulatory acceptance or batch disposition.

## Limitations

This lesson and working asset organize evidence and decision ownership. They do
not establish starting-material acceptability, supplier or input approval,
reaction mechanism, impurity identity, process capability, purge, analytical
procedure suitability, acceptable intake, specification, validation status,
technology-transfer acceptance, filing classification, commercial
authorization or batch disposition.

Every product-, route-, process-, material-, supplier-, site-, scale-,
equipment-, method-, lifecycle- and market-specific conclusion requires current
controlled evidence and qualified review.
