# Biopharma potency, reference systems and orthogonal characterization

Biopharma analytical control is not a list of assays. Potency, charge variants,
size variants, glycosylation, host-cell proteins, residual DNA, aggregates,
identity, purity and stability each answer different questions. The right strategy
connects the attribute and intended decision to sample, reference, method
capability, data lineage, specification or control placement and lifecycle change.

## Decision question

> For this biologic and intended decision, which complementary evidence supports
> potency and product characterization, and what could change the interpretation?

## Applicability and do-not-use boundary

Use this lesson for potency strategy, reference-standard transition, orthogonal
characterization, charge or size profile changes, residual-DNA questions, method
transfer, stability interpretation and comparability planning for protein
biologics.

Do not use it as a product-specific bioassay, reference qualification protocol,
specification, release decision, stability condition, comparability conclusion or
regulatory submission. Cell and gene therapy, vectors, mRNA, vaccines, plasma
products, ADCs and oligonucleotides require modality-specific scope and review.

## Controlled source map

| Source | Decision use | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ICH Q6B | Biotechnological product quality and characterization concepts | It does not prescribe a product-specific attribute panel or specification |
| ICH Q2(R2) and Q14 | Analytical procedure performance and lifecycle development | They do not select a product-specific assay or acceptance criterion |
| ICH Q5C and Q5E | Stability and comparability context | They do not establish shelf life or comparability for a product |
| ICH Q9(R1) and Q10 | Risk and lifecycle governance | Risk assessment cannot replace reference, sample or method evidence |

## Separate seven evidence objects

1. **Product and intended decision** - molecule, presentation, process version,
   stage, market, attribute and accountable owner.
2. **Biological or quality attribute** - potency, identity, purity, variant,
   impurity, stability or process-link question.
3. **Sample and representativeness** - material state, lot, storage, preparation,
   dilution, matrix, sampling and data lineage.
4. **Reference system** - identity, qualification, assigned value, lifecycle,
   bridging, storage, drift and change control.
5. **Analytical capability** - principle, specificity, precision, range,
   robustness, system suitability, reportability and limitations.
6. **Orthogonal and stability evidence** - complementary methods, degradation,
   trends, contradictions and product-process interpretation.
7. **Decision and lifecycle change** - specification or control linkage, transfer,
   reference change, comparability, review roles, action and authorized conclusion.

## Build the attribute-to-decision chain

### 1. Define what the result decides

State whether the result supports characterization, release, stability, process
control, comparability, transfer or investigation. The same attribute may need
different evidence for different uses.

### 2. Challenge reference and sample systems

Record how reference identity, assigned value, storage, drift, sample preparation,
matrix, lot and dilution affect interpretation. A changed reference can create a
measurement shift without a product shift.

### 3. Use orthogonal methods for a reason

Define what each complementary method contributes and what it cannot detect. More
assays do not automatically make a decision stronger if the samples, references,
specificity or intended uses are not aligned.

### 4. Separate signal from attribution

When potency, charge, size, glycan, residual-DNA or aggregate signals move, keep
product, process, sample, method, reference and data hypotheses separate. Record
which observation would discriminate between them.

### 5. Reopen after lifecycle change

Reference, reagent, cell system, method, instrument, software, site, process,
formulation, container or specification changes can affect more than one evidence
object. Preserve the pre-change basis and the actual bridge.

## Decision table

| Evidence condition | Defensible action | Do not claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Potency moves after a reference change | Assess reference, assay, sample and product evidence together | Product potency changed from raw values alone |
| One orthogonal method is unchanged | Reconcile its scope and sensitivity with the intended decision | No product impact overall |
| Charge or size profile changes | Link method, sample, process, product, stability and comparability evidence | A universal product-quality conclusion |
| Residual DNA is below a reporting boundary | Preserve capability, sample basis and risk context | Zero residual DNA or universal safety conclusion |
| Results are within specification | Keep attribute, method and process questions distinct | Comparability or full characterization from conformance alone |
| Evidence or reviewer coverage remains open | Mark `Evidence required` or `Qualified review required` | Potency, specification, filing or batch approval |

## Worked example: fictional reference transition

A fictional monoclonal-antibody site replaces a potency reference and changes one
sample-preparation step during a method transfer. Potency shifts slightly, while
size and charge profiles remain within the existing control basis. The package
records reference qualification and bridge questions, sample and method differences,
orthogonal evidence, stability context, reviewers and change actions. It does not
conclude potency equivalence, specification adequacy or comparability.

## Working asset

Use a **Biopharma Potency & Characterization Decision Brief** to maintain the
attribute-to-decision map, reference lifecycle, sample genealogy, method
capability, orthogonal evidence, contradictions, change impact, reviewers and
limitations. It is a review record, not a bioassay protocol.

## Limitations

This lesson does not establish an assay, reference qualification, acceptance
criterion, specification, residual-DNA limit, potency result, stability conclusion,
comparability, validation, filing or batch decision. Product-, method-, sample-,
reference-, modality- and market-specific conclusions require current controlled
evidence and qualified review.
