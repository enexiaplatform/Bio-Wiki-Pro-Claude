# Batch Record Review & Product Release

Batch record review is the final quality gate before product reaches
patients. Everything the site claims about a batch - that it was made
according to the registered process, tested against the approved
specification, and that every anomaly was understood - is proven, or
disproven, in this record. The reviewer's signature converts a pile of
executed steps into a legal disposition decision.

The governing framework: **21 CFR 211.22** (quality control unit
responsibilities), **211.100(b)** (deviations must be recorded and
justified), **211.188** (batch production and control records),
**211.192** (investigation of discrepancies and failures), and
**211.180(f)** for reconciliation. In the EU, **GMP Chapter 4**
(Documentation) and **Chapter 5** (Production) define record content, and
**Annex 16** defines certification by the **Qualified Person (QP)** - the
person who carries personal legal responsibility for each batch certified
for the EU market. For electronic batch records, **21 CFR Part 11** and
**EU Annex 11** apply.

The high-stakes reality: inspectors rarely reconstruct your manufacturing
day - they reconstruct your *review*. A batch record with unexplained gaps,
a deviation closed without an impact statement, or a reconciliation
"adjusted" to fit is read as a quality unit that signs without looking.

## The review at a glance: what is being confirmed

| Question | Where the evidence lives |
| --- | --- |
| Was the batch made per the approved master record? | Executed BMR/eBR, equipment logs, cleaning status |
| Were the right materials used? | Dispensing records, weigh/verify signatures, material status |
| Did in-process and finished results conform? | IPC records, QC results, CoA traceable to raw data |
| Were all anomalies resolved? | Deviation, OOS/OOT, and change-control records with impact assessments |
| Is everything accounted for? | Yield, component, and label reconciliation |
| Is the environment/utility context acceptable? | EM, water, HVAC data for the manufacturing window |

## Review by section: the checklist that survives audits

Work the record in the order the batch was made. Each section below lists
what to verify and the classic deficiency it catches.

### 1. Header, identity, and record control

- Correct master record version in force at manufacture date (a batch made
  on a superseded version is a deviation, not a paperwork issue).
- Batch/lot number, product, strength, and dates consistent across all
  pages and attachments.
- Record issued under controlled conditions; all pages accounted for,
  including voided ones.

*Classic deficiency:* pages photocopied and re-issued without explanation,
or an executed record that does not match the approved master - a finding
FDA investigators cite as failure to follow written procedures.

### 2. Materials and dispensing

- Every component: correct material, approved status at time of use, lot
  number, quantity within tolerance.
- Weigh operations signed by performer **and** independent verifier where
  the SOP requires it.
- Balance identification and calibration status current on each date of
  use.
- Reconciled quantities of dispensed vs returned material.

*Classic deficiency:* second-person verification added in the same hand,
or a material used while in quarantine status - caught by comparing
dispensing timestamps against the material status history.

### 3. Equipment, rooms, and cleaning

- Equipment IDs match the logbooks; cleaning and use logs cover the
  manufacturing window with no overlaps or gaps.
- Room and line clearance documented before start (previous product
  cleared, labels removed).
- Calibration/PM status of critical instruments valid on the dates used.

*Classic deficiency:* the use log shows the equipment in two places at
once, or cleaning recorded after the next batch started - indicators of
retrospective documentation.

### 4. Process execution and in-process controls

- Every step signed and dated by the performer, with verifier signatures
  where required; no blanks, no skipped steps without explanation.
- Critical parameters (times, temperatures, speeds, pressures) recorded
  against the specified ranges - actual values, not checkmarks where the
  record asks for values.
- IPC results within limits; any out-of-limit IPC tied to a deviation.
- Corrections follow good documentation practice: single line, original
  legible, initialled, dated, reason where required.

*Classic deficiency:* every recorded value identical across batches ("flat"
data), or a correction without a reason on a critical parameter.

### 5. Yields and reconciliation

- Theoretical vs actual yield at defined stages, each within its limit.
- Reconciliation of components, bulk product, printed packaging materials,
  and labels - **21 CFR 211.180(f)** and EU GMP Chapter 5 require
  significant discrepancies to be investigated.
- Label accountability is unforgiving: issued = used + destroyed +
  returned, with counts and destruction records.

*Classic deficiency:* a label reconciliation forced to zero by an
unexplained "adjustment" entry, or yield limits widened in the master
record after batches kept failing them.

### 6. Packaging and labelling

- Correct packaging components and printed matter versions per the bill of
  materials.
- Line clearance before and after; reconciliation of printed materials.
- Serialization/aggregation data where applicable, with exception records
  resolved.

### 7. Deviations, OOS/OOT, and change controls

- **Every** deviation opened during manufacture and testing is listed,
  closed, and carries a batch-specific impact assessment.
- OOS and OOT results resolved per the investigation procedures; an open
  OOS blocks release.
- Change controls affecting the batch assessed for impact on this batch.

This is the section where releases die. A deviation marked "closed" with
no impact statement is a release that will not survive an audit.

### 8. Laboratory results and CoA

- All specification tests performed and reported; results within limits.
- CoA values traceable to raw data (chromatograms, printouts, worksheets) -
  not retyped summaries.
- Retest/resample history visible and justified; no unexplained result
  versions.
- Stability commitments and post-approval conditions for the batch noted.

### 9. Environmental and utility context

- EM results, water quality, and HVAC status for the manufacturing window
  acceptable, or excursions assessed for impact on this batch.
- For sterile products: media fill status, intervention records, and
  personnel qualification current.

## Deviation impact assessment: the framework

For each item affecting the batch, the record must answer four questions:

1. **What** happened - the event, when, who detected it.
2. **What attribute** could it affect - identity, strength, quality,
   purity, or the evidence trail itself.
3. **What data** addresses that concern - additional testing, comparison
   batches, process data, scientific rationale.
4. **Conclusion** - impact or no impact, signed, with the reasoning on
   paper.

Disposition cannot proceed until each deviation has a documented impact
call. "No impact" must be argued, not asserted.

## Review timing vs release

The reviewer's clock is part of the control:

- **21 CFR 211.192** requires unexplained discrepancies and failures to be
  investigated - industry practice completes batch record review within a
  defined window (often 30 days from batch completion - common industry
  practice, confirm against your SOP), and the batch must not ship before
  QA disposition.
- Review should be **timely, not rushed**: a record reviewed 11 months
  later has lost its corrective value; a record "reviewed" in an hour after
  a complex batch was not reviewed at all.
- Quarantine status must be real: physical or system-enforced segregation
  until disposition. A batch that shipped "pending review" is a
  self-inflicted recall scenario.
- Deviations and OOS investigations may extend the window; the record
  should show the batch remained under control (quarantine) throughout.

## eBR specifics and review by exception

Electronic batch records change the reviewer's job from reading every value
to **verifying the exceptions**:

- The eBR system must be validated (Part 11 / Annex 11): access control,
  audit trails, enforced sequencing, and electronic signatures tied to
  individuals.
- "Review by exception" means the system flags out-of-limit entries,
  missed steps, and overridden interlocks, and the reviewer focuses there -
  but this is only defensible when the exception logic itself is validated
  and periodically verified. An exception report nobody configured is a
  false sense of security.
- The reviewer must still sample the audit trail: signature attributions,
  timestamp plausibility, any manual overrides of automated captures.
- Hybrid records (eBR plus paper printouts) are the riskiest: the reviewer
  must confirm the paper matches the electronic record and that the
  "official" record is defined.

for the reviewer's audit-trail routine, and
[Electronic Records - Part 11](/library/electronic-records-part-11) for the

## The disposition decision

Release is a documented decision with three outcomes: **release, reject, or
hold/quarantine** pending further information. In the EU, the QP certifies
each batch per Annex 16 - a personal, legal certification that manufacture
and testing complied with GMP and the marketing authorisation. In the US,
the quality unit approves or rejects under 211.22(a). Either way:

- The decision must be **reproducible by a second reviewer from the record
  alone**. If the reasoning lives in someone's head, the record is
  incomplete.
- Conditional releases ("release pending stability") need a defined basis
  and a tracking mechanism - they are exceptions, not a workflow.

## Parametric release

For terminally sterilized products, **parametric release** lets validated
sterilization process data (e.g. the full F0 record) substitute for the
finished-product sterility test - only with regulatory approval (in the EU,
per Annex 17; FDA accepts it under approved applications), a robust
contamination control strategy, and tight control of the critical
parameters. It raises, not lowers, the bar on data integrity for those
parameters: the F0 record *is* the sterility evidence, so its review must
be correspondingly rigorous.

## What inspectors look for: the deficiency patterns

1. **Missing signatures and unexplained blanks** - steps performed but not
   contemporaneously recorded.
2. **Corrections without reason** on critical entries, or correction fluid /
   overwritten values destroying the original entry.
3. **Deviations closed without impact assessment** - the single most common
   release-blocker finding.
4. **Reconciliation forced to fit** - adjustment entries with no
   investigation; label counts that only balance on paper.
5. **Backdated or batch-copied entries** - identical data across batches,
   timestamps inconsistent with building access or equipment logs.
6. **Review performed after shipment** - disposition date later than the
   first distribution record.
7. **eBR exception reports never reviewed** - the system flagged overrides
   that nobody read.
8. **Master record version drift** - batch executed on a superseded version
   with no deviation.

## Worked example: a yield discrepancy at compression

*Fictional but realistic.* A tablet batch shows compression-stage yield of
97.1% against a limit of 98.0-102.0% - a shortfall of about 14 kg of
tablet core mass. The reviewer does not sign and moves on; the deviation
is opened instead.

The impact assessment reconstructs mass: granulation yield was in range,
blending reconciliation balanced, so the loss occurred between blender
discharge and compressed-tablet weigh-up. Equipment inspection finds a
damaged feed-frame gasket on press 4 leaking granulation into the deduster
waste stream. Waste is recovered and weighed: 13.6 kg, reconciling the
gap within 0.4 kg.

Conclusion: the loss is physically accounted for, located in waste, and the
compressed tablets themselves show in-range weight, hardness, and assay
results - no impact on the batch, with QA sign-off. CAPA: gasket added to
press preventive maintenance and to the set-up checklist; a spare-parts
review across all presses. The batch releases ten days later with a
complete story. Had the mass not reconciled, the question would have been
different - where did product go? - and disposition would have waited on
the answer.
