# Protein Characterization Methods

No single test defines a large protein. ICH Q6B (Specifications: Test
Procedures and Acceptance Criteria for Biotechnological/Biological Products)
therefore expects characterization by a panel of **orthogonal** methods, each
sensitive to a different attribute, so that a change invisible to one method is
caught by another. Characterization feeds three places: the identity section
of the release specification, comparability assessments after process changes
(ICH Q5E), and investigations when a routine result looks wrong.

## The attribute-method map

| Attribute | Question | Core methods |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary structure | Is the sequence right, and where are modifications? | Peptide mapping (LC-MS/MS), intact and reduced mass, N- and C-terminal analysis |
| Higher-order structure | Is it folded correctly? | Circular dichroism (far-/near-UV CD), DSC, FTIR, fluorescence |
| Charge heterogeneity | What is the acidic/main/basic species profile? | icIEF, cation-exchange HPLC (CEX), capillary zone electrophoresis |
| Size heterogeneity | Fragments and aggregates at what levels? | CE-SDS (reduced and non-reduced), SEC with UV or MALS detection |
| Glycosylation | What glycoforms are present, at which sites? | Released-glycan analysis (HILIC-FLD/MS), site occupancy by peptide map, monosaccharide/sialic acid assays |
| Process-related impurities | What did the process leave behind? | Host-cell protein ELISA, residual DNA, residual Protein A |

## Primary structure: sequence and modifications

**Peptide mapping** is the definitive identity method. The protein is digested
with a specific protease (trypsin is the classic choice) and the resulting
peptides separated by LC with UV or, far more powerfully, MS detection. The
map is a fingerprint: every expected peptide at its expected mass confirms the
sequence, and new or shifted peaks localize modifications - oxidation,
deamidation, clipping, glycosylation occupancy. For comparability, the peptide
map is usually the first method asked to show "same molecule."

**Intact mass** by high-resolution MS confirms the overall molecular weight and
reveals the major glycoform pattern of an antibody in one measurement.
**Reduced mass** breaks the disulfide bonds to weigh heavy and light chains
separately, confirming chain identity and chain-specific modifications.
Together they are fast, information-dense release-supporting characterization.

## Higher-order structure: folded or not

Sequence and mass do not prove folding. Higher-order methods are comparative
rather than absolute: the test article's spectrum or thermogram is compared
against the reference.

- **Far-UV circular dichroism** reports secondary structure (alpha-helix,
  beta-sheet content); **near-UV CD** reports tertiary environment of aromatic
  side chains.
- **Differential scanning calorimetry (DSC)** measures thermal unfolding;
  domain-specific melting transitions are a sensitive fingerprint of
  conformational integrity and formulation effects.
- **FTIR and intrinsic fluorescence** provide complementary structure windows
  at low cost.

These methods rarely appear on release specifications, but they are workhorses
in comparability and forced-degradation interpretation.

## Charge variants: icIEF and CEX

Deamidation, sialylation, glycation, and C-terminal lysine all shift net
charge, so the charge profile is one of the most sensitive routinely monitored
CQAs. **Imaged capillary isoelectric focusing (icIEF)** separates species by
isoelectric point; **cation-exchange HPLC (CEX)** separates by surface charge
interaction. Both resolve the profile into acidic, main, and basic species,
reported as percent of total. A growing acidic-species trend is an early
warning light for degradation or process drift.

## Size variants: CE-SDS and SEC

**CE-SDS** is the modern replacement for SDS-PAGE: quantitative, automatable,
and run in two modes. Reduced CE-SDS quantifies heavy chain, light chain, and
fragments; non-reduced CE-SDS quantifies intact monomer versus clips and
incomplete species. **Size-exclusion chromatography (SEC)** quantifies soluble
aggregates under native conditions; coupling SEC to multi-angle light
scattering (MALS) confirms what the peaks actually are rather than trusting
elution time. Aggregates matter beyond purity numbers because of their
immunogenicity concern, which is why aggregate levels are among the most
scrutinized CQAs in a biologics dossier.

## Release spec versus characterization panel

A key Q6B concept: the release specification contains a subset of methods
chosen for routine control (identity by one or two orthogonal tests, purity,
potency, quantity, safety), while the **full characterization panel** is run at
development, at comparability, and periodically. When a process change is
proposed, it is the characterization panel - not the release spec alone -
that answers "is it still the same product?" The release spec may be blind to
the exact attribute the change touches.

## Glycosylation: the CQA with its own language

For most recombinant glycoproteins, glycosylation is both a potency-relevant
and consistency-relevant attribute: specific glycan features (for example
afucosylation or galactosylation on an IgG Fc) directly modulate effector
function. Characterization works at three levels: released-glycan profiling
(enzymatic release, fluorescent labeling, HILIC separation with MS support)
for the total glycan population; glycopeptide analysis by LC-MS for
site-specific occupancy; and intact or subunit mass for a rapid glycoform
fingerprint. Routine control usually picks the level where the critical
feature is visible and the method is robust enough for a QC lab.

## Common findings and mistakes

- Identity claimed from a single method; regulators expect orthogonality.
- Peptide map run without MS, so modifications cannot be localized or
  confirmed.
- SEC aggregate results reported without confirming peak identity (a column
  artifact and an aggregate can co-elute or be misassigned).
- Charge methods swapped (icIEF to CEX or back) without bridging data, then
  trends misread.
- Characterization methods unvalidated or unqualified where they are used to
  support comparability claims.
- Reference material not characterized with the same panel, so "shift vs
  reference" is uninterpretable.
- Forced-degradation samples analyzed with methods that were never shown to
  resolve the degradation products.
