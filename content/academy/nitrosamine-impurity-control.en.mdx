# Nitrosamine Impurity Control

In mid-2018, N-nitrosodimethylamine (NDMA) was found in valsartan API at
levels far above any defensible intake, triggering global recalls that then
spread to other sartans, ranitidine, and metformin. The episode converted
nitrosamines from a theoretical impurity class into one of the most
consequential quality topics in the industry: many nitrosamines are probable
human carcinogens (IARC Group 2A), so their Acceptable Intake limits sit in
the **nanogram-per-day** range — orders of magnitude below ordinary impurity
thresholds in ICH Q3A/Q3B.

The governing framework is the **EMA/CMDh review under Article 5(3) of
Regulation (EC) 726/2004** (EMA Q&A on nitrosamine impurities, continuously
updated since 2020) and the **FDA guidance *Control of Nitrosamine
Impurities in Human Drugs*** (2021, updated 2024). Both share the same
three-step logic: risk assess every product, test where risk exists,
mitigate and maintain. ICH M7(R2) supplies the underlying mutagenic-
impurity control philosophy.

## Why the Limits Are So Low

Standard mutagenic-impurity control uses the Threshold of Toxicological
Concern (TTC) of 1.5 µg/day from ICH M7. Nitrosamines belong to the "cohort
of concern" — structural classes so potent that the TTC does not apply.
Instead, each nitrosamine gets a compound-specific **Acceptable Intake (AI)**,
derived from rodent carcinogenicity data (TD50-based linear extrapolation to
a 1:100,000 excess cancer risk) where such data exist. Published AIs for the
common small nitrosamines are in the tens-to-hundreds of ng/day range
(e.g. NDMA 96 ng/day, NDEA 26.5 ng/day per FDA/EMA published values). A daily
dose of one gram of product at the NDMA AI translates to a specification in
the **tens of ppb** — which is why the analytical bar is so high.

## The Three-Step Regulatory Framework

| Step | What it requires | Output document |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Risk assessment | Evaluate every marketed and developmental product for nitrosamine formation/contamination potential, across API synthesis, formulation, packaging, and storage | Product-specific nitrosamine risk assessment, signed off by QA |
| 2. Confirmatory testing | Where any risk is identified, test with a validated method sensitive well below the AI-derived limit | Validated method, test results vs limit, conclusion |
| 3. Risk mitigation | Where nitrosamines are confirmed, change the process, materials, or controls; vary the marketing authorization; retest | CAPA, process/formulation change, regulatory variation, updated spec |

EMA set deadlines for each step (risk assessments for chemical products were
due in 2021; the exercise then extended into NDSRI evaluation). FDA's
timelines differ but the logic is identical. "Step 1 concluded no risk" with
a thin rationale is itself a finding — the assessment must be
product-specific and documented.

## How the Crisis Unfolded (and Why It Still Shapes Expectations)

| Year | Event | Lasting consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2018 | NDMA found in valsartan API; global sartan recalls | Regulators realize nitrosamines are a class problem, not a single-site failure |
| 2019 | NDMA in ranitidine (including formation on storage); EMA Article 5(3) review launched | Products themselves, not just APIs, come into scope |
| 2020 | NDMA in metformin extended-release products | Excipient and formulation contributions recognized |
| 2020–2021 | EMA/CMDh three-step deadlines; FDA publishes nitrosamine guidance (2021) | Risk assessments become a formal MAH obligation |
| 2021 onward | NDSRIs found across many APIs (rifampicin, rifapentine, others) | CPCA and read-across frameworks developed; expectations keep tightening |

The practical lesson: every framework document in this area is a living
document. An assessment written in 2021 and never updated is already stale.

## Roles: Who Owns What

- **Marketing authorization holder (MAH)** — legally accountable for the
  risk assessment and mitigation across the product lifecycle, including
  products made by contract manufacturers.
- **API and excipient suppliers** — provide process information, nitrite
  data, and recovered-solvent practices; the MAH must actively request and
  verify this, not assume it.
- **QC laboratory** — develops and validates the confirmatory method, runs
  testing, and flags artifact risks during validation.
- **QA** — signs the risk assessment, tracks commitments to authorities,
  and ensures re-assessment on relevant changes.
- **Toxicology and regulatory affairs** — assign AIs, apply CPCA or
  read-across, and manage variations and authority communications.

## Root Causes: Where Nitrosamines Actually Come From

Nitrosamines form when a **secondary or tertiary amine** meets a
**nitrosating agent** (nitrite, or nitrogen oxides) under suitable
conditions. Every root cause is a variant of bringing those two together.

| Source | Mechanism | Control lever |
| --- | --- | --- |
| API synthesis route | Amine intermediate or reagent + nitrite/nitrosating conditions in the same or downstream step | Route redesign; avoid nitrite; scavengers (e.g. ascorbic acid); purge design |
| Recovered solvents/reagents | Amine contamination in recovered solvent streams, or nitrosating species carried between campaigns | Restrict recovery; test recovered streams; dedicated recovery |
| Vendor/raw materials | Nitrite in excipients (common in many), amine impurities in starting materials, contaminated catalysts | Supplier qualification, nitrite limits on excipients, CoA verification |
| Cross-contamination | Shared equipment or shared vendor plants producing amine-containing products | Cleaning validation with nitrosamine awareness; dedicated equipment for high-risk steps |
| Formulation/storage | API bearing a secondary-amine moiety + trace nitrite in excipients slowly forming an NDSRI over shelf life | Excipient nitrite specification, pH control, antioxidants/nitrite scavengers in formulation, packaging selection |
| Degradation | Some APIs degrade to nitrosamine precursors (ranitidine's NDMA on storage is the canonical case) | Reformulation, storage-condition control, or product withdrawal when unfixable |

## NDSRIs: The Hard Case

A **nitrosamine drug-substance-related impurity (NDSRI)** forms by
nitrosation of the API itself (or an API fragment) — so it cannot be
controlled by avoiding a solvent or reagent; the molecule carries its own
amine. NDSRIs drove the rifampicin/rifapentine findings and later waves of
concern across many APIs with secondary-amine functionality.

Because most NDSRIs have no carcinogenicity data, regulators accept a
**Carcinogenic Potency Categorization Approach (CPCA)**: structural features
(number and steric environment of α-hydrogens on both sides of the
N-nitroso group, activating and deactivating substituents) assign the NDSRI
to a potency category with a default AI (commonly 26.5, 100, 400, or
1,500 ng/day under the FDA-recommended framework). Read-across to a
sufficiently similar nitrosamine with data is the other accepted route. An
**enhanced Ames test** (with the nitrosamine-relevant metabolic activation
conditions) that is clearly negative can support reclassification out of the
cohort of concern and toward an ICH Q3A/B-style qualification threshold —
but the test conditions must be the enhanced, sensitive protocol, not a
standard Ames run.

## Computing the Product Limit from the AI

The specification limit follows directly from the AI and the maximum daily
dose (MDD):

```
Limit (ppm) = AI (ng/day) / MDD (mg/day)
```

*Worked example (fictional product, real arithmetic).* AI = 96 ng/day
(NDMA), MDD = 320 mg/day of drug product: limit = 96 / 320 = **0.3 ppm =
300 ppb**. For a high-dose product (MDD 2,000 mg): limit = 96 / 2,000 =
**0.048 ppm = 48 ppb** — your method LOQ must sit comfortably below that,
which dictates LC-MS/MS for most matrices. If the product contains multiple
nitrosamines, regulators apply a **total-nitrosamine rule**: the sum of
each nitrosamine's level as a fraction of its AI must not exceed 1 (or an
equivalent risk-based cap). Document the arithmetic — inspectors check it.

*Multi-nitrosamine extension.* Suppose the same product (MDD 320 mg) can
contain NDMA (AI 96 ng/day) at 0.1 ppm and NDEA (AI 26.5 ng/day) at
0.02 ppm. Daily intakes: NDMA 0.1 µg/g × 0.32 g = 32 ng/day (33% of AI);
NDEA 0.02 µg/g × 0.32 g = 6.4 ng/day (24% of AI). Sum of AI fractions =
0.33 + 0.24 = 0.57 — within the total limit of 1. Both individual limits
pass and the combined risk passes; a product where the fractions summed
above 1 would fail even though each nitrosamine individually met its own
limit.

## From Risk Assessment to Control Strategy

The risk assessment ends in one of three documented outcomes:

1. **No risk identified** — rationale recorded (no amine + nitrosating
   agent convergence anywhere in the supply chain); no testing, but the
   assessment is revisited on change.
2. **Potential risk, testing rules it out** — confirmatory testing on
   representative commercial batches below the limit; the control strategy
   then specifies skip-lot or periodic verification, or a justified move to
   no routine testing.
3. **Risk confirmed** — mitigation (route, supplier, formulation change),
   a specification with the AI-derived limit, routine or skip-lot release
   testing, stability monitoring, and a regulatory variation.

Whatever the outcome, the decision, the data, and the re-assessment
triggers (new supplier, route change, excipient change, new published AI)
belong in a controlled document owned by QA.

## Confirmatory Testing: LC-MS/MS Done Right

The method requirements follow from the limits:

- **Sensitivity** — LOQ at or below ~10–30% of the limit, so ppb-to-sub-ppb
  quantitation in the drug matrix. Triple-quadrupole LC-MS/MS in MRM mode is
  the workhorse; high-resolution MS (Orbitrap-type) for screening and
  unknowns; GC-MS/MS for the volatile small nitrosamines.
- **Artifact control** — nitrosamines can form *in the sample vial* when the
  matrix contains both residual amine and nitrite under acidic conditions,
  creating false positives. Validate with a nitrosation inhibitor
  (e.g. a sulfamic-acid or antioxidant trap) and demonstrate no in-situ
  formation by spiking precursor amine + nitrite into a blank matrix and
  showing no signal growth over the sample-preparation time.
- **Extraction/recovery** — API and excipient matrices suppress ionization;
  demonstrate recovery and matrix effect per ICH Q2(R2) validation, with
  isotopically labelled internal standards (e.g. d6-NDMA) to correct for it.
- **Specificity** — confirm no interference from the API at the analyte MRM
  transitions; for NDSRIs the API and analyte share structural fragments,
  making chromatographic separation from the API peak essential.
- **Lifecycle** — once validated, the method feeds routine or skip-lot
  testing per the control strategy agreed with the regulator.

Validation checklist for a nitrosamine confirmatory method:

- LOQ demonstrated at ≤30% of the limit with acceptable precision/accuracy;
- recovery and matrix effect assessed in the actual drug matrix;
- isotope-dilution internal standards for each target analyte;
- artifact-formation challenge (precursor spike, no signal growth) passed;
- specificity against the API and formulation blanks confirmed;
- stability of analyte in solution and in processed samples established;
- robustness across extraction-time and pH windows verified.

## Common Failure Modes (Inspection Themes)

- Risk assessment that lists the API synthesis but ignores **excipient
  nitrite** — the single most common gap in later NDSRI findings.
- "No risk" conclusions not signed by QA, not product-specific, or copied
  between products with different routes.
- Analytical methods validated down to a limit, but without artifact-control
  experiments — false positives investigated as product failures, or worse,
  real formation masked by quenching.
- Confirmatory testing done on one early batch and never tied to the
  commercial process or supplier changes.
- Recovered-solvent streams accepted on a CoA that never included
  nitrosamine-relevant impurities.
- Mitigation implemented (route change) but the marketing authorization not
  varied — a compliance gap on top of a quality fix.
- Multi-nitrosamine products controlled per-compound with no total-risk
  summation.
- Shelf-life NDSRI growth missed because testing was release-only with no
  stability arm.
