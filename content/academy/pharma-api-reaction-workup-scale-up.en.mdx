# Pharma API reaction, work-up and scale-up control

Small-molecule API development becomes a manufacturing decision when route
knowledge must survive a different vessel, scale, mixing pattern, work-up,
isolation sequence, operator group or site. A reaction result that looks similar
at two scales is not, by itself, evidence that the process, impurity profile or
downstream material attributes will behave the same way.

## Decision question

> For this API process version and scale or site change, what product, process,
> equipment, sampling and analytical evidence is needed before a qualified team can
> decide whether the next development, transfer, validation or manufacturing step
> is justified?

## Applicability and do-not-use boundary

Use this lesson to structure route-boundary, reaction, quench, extraction, phase
split, filtration, solvent exchange, hold, sampling, scale-up and receiving-site
questions for chemically synthesized drug substance.

Do not use it as a reaction recipe, process parameter recommendation, batch record,
process validation conclusion, impurity limit, specification, filing assessment or
manufacturing authorization. A product-specific bridge may require process
chemistry, chemical engineering, solid-state, analytical, manufacturing, quality
and regulatory CMC review.

| ICH Q9(R1) and Q10 | Risk, knowledge and change governance | Risk ranking cannot replace process evidence |
| FDA Process Validation guidance | Lifecycle view of process design, qualification and continued verification | It does not specify a universal validation design |

## Separate six evidence objects

1. **Route and process version** - reaction sequence, input boundary, intended use,
   scale, site, market and decision owner.
2. **Material and equipment basis** - input attributes, vessel geometry, mixing,
   heat transfer, addition, containment, utilities and equipment differences.
3. **Reaction and work-up knowledge** - transformation, endpoint evidence, quench,
   extraction, phase split, wash, solvent exchange and known failure modes.
4. **Sampling and hold basis** - sample location, timing, representativeness,
   hold, transport, test method, data lineage and review.
5. **Scale-up and manufacturing evidence** - model assumptions, observed outcome,
   deviations, variability, recovery, impurity and material-attribute impact.
6. **Transfer and lifecycle decision** - receiving-site readiness, training,
   unresolved gaps, change triggers, actions and authorized conclusion.

## Build the route-to-scale evidence chain

### 1. Freeze the decision baseline

Name the API form, route and process version, scale or site change, intended use,
market, evidence cutoff and accountable owner. Preserve the pre-change basis so a
later revision does not silently rewrite the original comparison.

### 2. Connect the chemistry to equipment and materials

Record which input attributes, addition rates, mixing, heat transfer, phase
behavior, filtration, solvent exchange and hold conditions can change the result.
Do not treat a unit-operation name as a transferable process description.

### 3. Make sampling part of the process model

For every key observation, retain location, timing, material state, method version,
reporting boundary, sample preparation and reviewer. A different sample basis can
create an apparent scale effect or hide a real one.

### 4. Challenge work-up and isolation effects

Track impurity carryover, phase split, wash efficiency, solvent exchange, filter
behavior, hold exposure and intermediate quality. Link each observation to the
analytical capability that can support it; do not call an impurity absent because
the method could not recover or distinguish it.

### 5. Stage receiving-site readiness

Before transfer or routine manufacture, identify equipment and automation
differences, local materials, analyst and operator training, sampling, deviations,
data review, residual actions and the decision that remains with the authorized
site function.

## Decision table

| Evidence condition | Defensible action | Do not claim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conversion is similar at two scales | Compare work-up, impurity, recovery, material and sampling evidence | Equivalent process from conversion alone |
| Mixing or heat-transfer basis differs | Define the affected mechanism and evidence plan | A copied parameter is transferable |
| An intermediate result is below a reporting boundary | Preserve capability and sample basis | Zero impurity or complete purge |
| A hold is longer at the receiving site | Assess material, impurity, stability, equipment and analytical impact | Hold-time acceptance from elapsed time alone |
| A deviation occurs during scale-up | Record impact, hypothesis, evidence and disposition owner | Ignore it because final API testing passes |
| Evidence or reviewer coverage remains open | Keep `Evidence required` or `Qualified review required` | Scale-up approval or manufacturing authorization |

## Worked example: fictional late-phase work-up change

A fictional API route moves a quench and phase-split operation from a development
vessel to a production vessel. Conversion remains similar, but an intermediate
sample shows a changed unknown response after a longer hold. The package records
the vessel and mixing differences, sample locations, method versions, competing
carryover hypotheses, five evidence requests and the accountable reviewers. It
does not conclude equivalence, impurity fate, specification compliance or batch
acceptance.

## Working asset

Use a **Pharma API Reaction, Work-up & Scale-up Decision Brief** to maintain the
frozen baseline, material/equipment basis, unit-operation map, sampling and hold
record, scale-up observations, deviations, open evidence, transfer readiness and
qualified review actions. The brief is a decision record, not a process recipe.

## Review-ready handoff

Before handing the package to manufacturing, validation, Quality or a receiving
site, confirm that route and equipment versions are exact; material and sample
genealogy is attributable; reaction and work-up observations are not pooled
across incompatible contexts; measurement limitations and below-reporting data
remain visible; deviations and interruptions are linked; competing scale and
chemistry explanations are preserved; and each open question has an owner,
reviewer and trigger. The receiving function should be able to distinguish what
was observed, what is inferred, what evidence is still required and which
decision remains outside the brief. A complete handoff therefore supports the
next controlled review without becoming an operating instruction, safety
assessment, process validation conclusion or authorization.

## Limitations

This lesson does not establish chemistry, process parameters, equipment sizing,
scale-up criteria, hold limits, impurity conclusions, validation status,
technology-transfer acceptance, filing classification or batch disposition. Every
route-, site-, scale-, equipment-, material-, method- and market-specific claim
requires current controlled evidence and qualified review.
