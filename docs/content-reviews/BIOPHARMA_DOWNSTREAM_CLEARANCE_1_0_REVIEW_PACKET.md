# Biopharma Downstream Purification & Clearance 1.0 Review Packet

**Document ID:** LSA-PRO-BIO-DOWNSTREAM-REVIEW-001

**Content version:** 1.0.0-review

**Review status:** under-review

**Risk level:** high

## Review scope

This packet covers the downstream lesson, guide, blank workbook, completed
fictional workbook, quiz v2, workflow placement, source metadata, and quality
gate for version 1.0.0-review.

It does not approve a purification process, resin or membrane lifetime,
impurity limit, viral-reduction claim, scale-down model, validation protocol,
comparability conclusion, specification, filing, or batch decision.

## Required reviewer roles

The package cannot become `sme-reviewed` until the applicable scope has
documented review from:

- downstream process development or MSAT;
- analytical development or product-quality sciences;
- viral safety or virology;
- quality, validation, or change control;
- regulatory CMC when the decision affects a filing or registered control.

## Critical review checklist

- [ ] The package does not present a universal purification platform or unit-operation sequence.
- [ ] Intended product, product-related variants, process-related impurities, contaminants, viral targets, and recovery remain distinct.
- [ ] Final specification conformance is not presented as proof of unchanged process robustness.
- [ ] Impurity progression preserves incoming load, step contribution, downstream burden, method capability, and lifecycle boundary.
- [ ] Viral inactivation/removal claims remain separate from routine impurity-clearance claims.
- [ ] Viral claims require a representative scale-down model, defined step, mechanism, study scope, method, robustness basis, limitations, and qualified review.
- [ ] The package does not calculate, recommend, or reproduce a viral-reduction factor.
- [ ] Contributions from multiple steps are not automatically combined without a controlled rationale for independence and interpretation.
- [ ] Platform, published, and vendor knowledge are not presented as product-specific proof.
- [ ] Resin/membrane reuse, lifetime, cleaning, storage, load, leachables, and change boundaries remain explicit.
- [ ] The fictional example contains no disguised operating range, lifetime, limit, benchmark, or regulatory conclusion.
- [ ] Formula-driven status fails closed when required records, evidence, or review are incomplete.
- [ ] Advanced modalities and materially different processes are outside the current claim.
- [ ] Source versions, locators, and regional implementation remain current at review time.

Any unchecked critical item blocks promotion regardless of numerical score.

## Workbook usability acceptance

- [ ] The blank workbook opens with nine named sheets and no formula error.
- [ ] The blank workbook reports `Evidence required` before required content is entered.
- [ ] Users can define targets before mapping unit operations or claims.
- [ ] Each unit operation records purpose, target links, evidence, control, lifecycle, handoff, owner, and review status.
- [ ] Each clearance claim records category, mechanism, contributing steps, study/model scope, limitations, evidence, conclusion boundary, and reviewer roles.
- [ ] Viral claims without required model and mechanism evidence receive an attention flag.
- [ ] The completed fictional example remains `Qualified review required` while open evidence and actions remain unresolved.
- [ ] Data validation, filters, frozen headers, instructions, and protection are usable in the target desktop spreadsheet application.
- [ ] The guide PDF and both workbooks open and match the deliverable manifest.

## Open evidence and product backlog

1. qualified downstream, analytical, viral-safety, quality, and regulatory review;
2. product-specific impurity, product-variant, recovery, and viral-clearance evidence;
3. a permissioned real case covering a downstream change and resulting decision;
4. method coverage and lifecycle evidence for HCP, residual DNA, residual ligand, leachables, and relevant variants;
5. representative scale-down, resin/membrane lifecycle, connected-process, and robustness evidence;
6. real user evidence that the package changes a decision or creates a reusable controlled record.
