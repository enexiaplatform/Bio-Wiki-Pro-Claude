## Critical review checklist

- [ ] The protein-biologics scope and modality exclusions are correct.
- [ ] Technology transfer is not represented as document delivery, training,
      protocol completion, engineering-run success, or release conformance.
- [ ] Transfer readiness, execution authorization, analytical transfer,
      comparability, validation, filing, routine readiness, and acceptance are
      separate gates.
- [ ] The charter controls product, process, method, site, scale, lifecycle,
      market, scope, exclusions, versions, owners, and authority.
- [ ] Tacit knowledge, development rationale, deviations, trends, failure
      modes, scale-down limitations, analytical blind spots, and change history
      are treated as knowledge objects with limitations.
- [ ] Facility/equipment fit uses functional and product/process impact rather
      than literal sameness or model-number equivalence.
- [ ] Material, supplier, logistics, reference, reagent, and single-use
      differences remain visible and owned.
- [ ] Upstream, downstream, hold, sampling, automation, digital, cleaning,
      sterilization, containment, and maintenance boundaries are coherent.
- [ ] Analytical validation, transfer/bridging, analyst qualification,
      instrument qualification, data flow, and routine QC readiness are not
      conflated.
- [ ] Comparability does not mean analytical identity or release conformance.
- [ ] No universal batch count, process parameter, operating range,
      qualification/validation design, sample plan, or acceptance criterion has
      been supplied.
- [ ] Deviations, conditions, residual risks, commitments, disagreements,
      actions, due dates, and owners cannot disappear behind a completed gate.
- [ ] The fictional CHO transfer remains visibly fictional and unresolved.
- [ ] No execution, release, site, regulatory, or routine-readiness approval is
      implied.
