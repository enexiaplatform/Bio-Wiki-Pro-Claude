## Critical review checklist

- [ ] Protein/polypeptide scope and advanced-modality exclusions are correct.
- [ ] Attribute relevance is separated from automatic CQA designation.
- [ ] Characterization, IPC, release, stability, comparability, investigation,
      and lifecycle uses are not conflated.
- [ ] Orthogonality is treated as complementary evidence rather than instrument
      count.
- [ ] Potency content reflects product mechanism, assay-system, reference,
      critical-reagent, statistical, and reportability evidence without a
      universal assay claim.
- [ ] Desired product, product-related substances, product-related impurities,
      degradation products, process-related impurities, contaminants, and
      analytical artifacts are kept distinct.
- [ ] Reference-material tiers, intended uses, assigned properties,
      qualification, stability, bridging, and replacement controls are
      scientifically coherent.
- [ ] WHO international standards are not represented as automatic
      product-specific reference materials or specifications.
- [ ] Specification conformance is not presented as full characterization or
      proof of unchanged product quality.
- [ ] Specification inclusion, omission, or change remains a product-specific,
      regional, qualified decision.
- [ ] Validation is not presented as a cure for a weak intended purpose or
      method blind spot.
- [ ] Procedure transfer, reference transition, product/process change,
      bridging, revalidation, specification impact, and filing impact remain
      separate evidence questions.
- [ ] Q6(R1) concept-stage revision activity is not treated as effective
      guidance.
- [ ] No product method, parameter, range, system-suitability criterion,
      reference value, specification, acceptance criterion, or regulatory
      outcome has been fabricated.
- [ ] The fictional resin/reference/method-transfer example remains visibly
      fictional and unresolved.

## Workbook usability acceptance
