> **Status:** current Gate 1 commercial-acquisition playbook
>
> **Supersedes:** the legacy Academy/Pro soft-launch campaign
>
> **Canonical basis:** `PRODUCT_SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md`
>
> **Execution boundary:** this document supplies targeting, qualification and copy. It does not authorize external outreach, customer claims, discounts, reviewer claims or publication.

## 1. Outcome

Acquire and deliver three distinct paid, evidence-complete Atlas Quality Lab engagements in the first non-sterile pharmaceutical microbiology wedge.

The purpose of acquisition is not traffic, trial volume or content subscriptions. Each suitable engagement should create:

- a paid and accepted customer decision package;
- controlled corrections and buyer decisions;
- measured delivery time, effort and margin;
- estimate-to-actual observations;
- reusable evidence, rule or benchmark learning where permission and review allow it.

After a request becomes a real engagement, operate it through `QUALITY_LAB_GATE_1_GATE_2_FIELD_RUNBOOK.md`.

## 2. Offer and positioning

### One-sentence offer

Atlas converts a site's product portfolio, applicable quality requirements and testing demand into a vendor-neutral quality-laboratory operating Blueprint with visible assumptions, evidence gaps and review boundaries.

### Founding offer ladder

| Step | Current commercial basis | Buyer outcome | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initial model and public sample | Free | Understand the decision structure and inspect a synthetic concept output | Not a customer result, validated design or approved specification |
| Paid Scope Diagnostic | USD 149 fixed fee | One 60-minute stakeholder workshop plus a written scope and decision memo within two business days after the workshop | Fee is credited to a Blueprint started within 30 days |
| Blueprint Pilot | Starts at USD 990; typical founding scope USD 990–2,500 | One site, first non-sterile microbiology wedge, baseline plus one alternative, controlled XLSX/PDF/register, one workshop and one revision | Exact scope, reviewer, inputs, exclusions, timing and payment are confirmed before kickoff |

Do not lead with Pro, lesson counts, a free trial, toolkit volume or a generic SaaS pitch. Atlas Evidence supports trust; the paid decision package is the commercial center.

## 3. Initial customer profile

Prioritize real projects in this order:

1. ASEAN pharmaceutical manufacturers building, expanding or reassessing a QC laboratory.
2. Manufacturers preparing for PIC/S, EU-GMP or more demanding export-market expectations.
3. Engineering or turnkey firms that need vendor-neutral QC microbiology planning depth.
4. Laboratory equipment distributors that need a defensible application basis before proposing equipment.
5. Contract testing laboratories planning capacity or service expansion.

### Trigger signals

- new site, line, product family or market;
- laboratory expansion, relocation or remediation;
- unresolved staffing, incubator, autoclave, BSC, consumable or turnaround capacity;
- a procurement list exists but its workload and evidence basis are weak;
- engineering, QC, QA and procurement hold inconsistent assumptions;
- an outsource-versus-insource or phased investment decision is open.

### Disqualifiers or explicit exclusions

- the buyer wants CAD, HVAC, construction engineering or a validated design;
- the buyer asks Atlas to select suppliers, certify equivalence, approve a method or make a regulatory decision;
- the request is a generic content subscription, LIMS/ELN/QMS replacement or ordinary distributor recruitment project;
- no accountable sponsor can provide a non-confidential decision basis;
- the scope is outside the current wedge and no qualified reviewer/evidence path exists;
- the prospect requires a fabricated case, testimonial, client logo or outcome claim.

## 4. Privacy-minimal target-account preparation

Build the named account list in the authorized CRM or founder-controlled workspace, not in source control. Start with a bounded planning batch of 12–20 accounts; this is an activity hypothesis, not evidence of market size or conversion.

For each account retain only what is required to decide the next action:

- organization and market;
- account lane: manufacturer, engineering/turnkey, distributor or contract laboratory;
- observed trigger and public source;
- likely decision role;
- warm-introduction path;
- current pipeline status;
- last contact and next action;
- reason for qualification or loss.

Do not copy confidential project facts, personal notes, licensed standards content or full Blueprint inputs into outreach tools or analytics.

## 5. Qualification heuristic

This is a founder operating heuristic, not an Atlas rule or a claim about buyer quality. Award one point for each evidenced condition:

1. A real laboratory build, expansion, change or capacity decision is active or planned.
2. The first non-sterile pharmaceutical microbiology wedge is materially relevant.
3. A decision owner, budget holder or credible internal sponsor is reachable.
4. The team can provide a bounded product/site/workload input basis under agreed handling.
5. The team is willing to purchase the USD 149 Diagnostic if fit is confirmed.
6. Engagement-specific QC/QA/engineering review can be named before Blueprint delivery.

Suggested routing:

- **5–6 points, no disqualifier:** mark `qualified`; invite the Paid Scope Diagnostic.
- **3–4 points:** clarify the missing decision, sponsor, data or reviewer path before making a commercial recommendation.
- **0–2 points or any disqualifier:** decline, refer or record `lost` with a bounded reason.

Do not lower the evidence or reviewer boundary merely to move an opportunity forward.

## 6. Three-round acquisition sequence

### Round 1 — warm design-partner outreach

**Audience:** founder's trusted manufacturer, laboratory, engineering and distributor contacts

**Objective:** identify three to five real project conversations, not collect product feedback

**Action:** send a one-to-one problem-led note linked to the public sample or Quality Lab page

**Decision:** qualify, clarify or decline; do not give a free Blueprint in exchange for vague feedback

### Round 2 — expert and partner referrals

**Audience:** senior microbiology/QC reviewers, engineering firms and equipment partners

**Objective:** secure reviewer pathways and introductions to live projects

**Action:** show the bounded sample and explain how Atlas preserves vendor neutrality, source gaps and accountable review

**Decision:** request an engagement-specific referral or reviewer conversation without implying appointment

### Round 3 — evidence-led public launch

**Audience:** regulated-manufacturing quality leaders and project partners

**Objective:** generate qualified Scope Diagnostic requests

**Action:** publish the decision problem, one synthetic model view and the exact commercial/boundary language

**Decision:** route serious projects to `/quality-lab/review`; route general readers to the public model, sample and Atlas Evidence

Do not revive “first 50 free Pro,” Academy-volume or GMP Kit campaigns as the primary launch motion.

## 8. Intake and discovery questions

Use the existing review intake rather than starting a parallel questionnaire. Clarify only what is required to decide fit:

1. What laboratory decision must be made, by whom and by when?
2. Is this a new build, expansion, transfer, remediation, capacity problem or procurement-basis problem?
3. Which site, product families and markets are in scope?
4. Which microbiology applications are definitely in scope, outsourced or unknown?
5. Which workload, turnaround, equipment, staffing, consumable, space or cost decisions are open?
6. What source, method, specification and operating evidence is available and authorized for review?
7. Which QC, QA, engineering, finance and procurement roles must challenge the output?
8. What cannot be shared, stored or reused?
9. What would a useful Diagnostic memo decide or make explicit?
10. What would make the project a clear no-go?

Do not solve the project during qualification. The paid Diagnostic exists to structure the decision and evidence gaps.

Use the existing Admin commercial pipeline statuses; do not create a separate CRM inside Atlas.

| `diagnostic-paid` | Payment or controlled commercial reference exists |

Status is not evidence by itself. The corresponding commercial or delivery reference must exist in its authorized system of record.

## 10. Metrics and weekly review

Review the smallest metrics that prove movement toward Gate 1:

- qualified Blueprint opportunities;
- paid Diagnostics;
- Diagnostic-to-Blueprint proposals and wins;
- accepted paid engagements;
- time from request to response, workshop, controlled delivery and acceptance;
- project value, direct delivery cost and gross margin;
- corrections, buyer decisions and accepted estimate-to-actual observations;
- referrals that create reviewer coverage or a qualified live project.

Traffic, signups, lesson reads, free trials and content count are supporting signals only.

Weekly operating questions:

1. Which opportunity has the strongest real decision and sponsor?
2. What exact missing evidence prevents the next status transition?
3. Is any message implying review, validation, engineering or customer proof that does not exist?
4. Which outreach lane produced a qualified conversation, not merely engagement?
5. Which lost reason should change targeting, copy or the offer—and which should not change the product boundary?
